# Wife's Career Plan (India)

She is well-positioned: an MBA plus US elementary-school experience maps cleanly onto India's large edtech, school-administration, and education-operations market. As a returning Indian citizen (or OCI holder) she can work freely — no work visa needed. Affordable domestic help makes a working household realistic. **Bottom line: Bengaluru offers far more opportunity for both employment and a baking business; Kolkata is viable but a smaller market with a lower income ceiling.**

## 2. Baking / cooking entrepreneurship

**FSSAI license** (per ClearTax, matching the statutory schedule):

| Category | Annual turnover | Fee/year | For a home baker |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Basic Registration (Form A)** | under INR 12 lakh | **INR 100/year** | ✅ This is the one to start with |
| State License (Form B) | INR 12 lakh – 20 crore | INR 2,000–5,000 | only if she scales up |
| Central License | above INR 20 crore | INR 7,500 | not relevant |

- **Process (FoSCoS portal, foscos.fssai.gov.in):** file Form A online → upload photo ID, premises proof, product list, food-safety declaration, bank details → get a 14-digit registration number + certificate by email; display at premises. Issued in ~7–30 days (ClearTax). Renew 30 days before expiry. A filing service costs ~INR 3,000 if she doesn't DIY.

**Selling on Swiggy / Zomato** (IndiaFilings, ClearTax):
- FSSAI license is **mandatory**; both platforms also commonly ask for a **GST number** (statutory GST thresholds: INR 40 lakh goods / 20 lakh services — verify with a CA, as aggregator supply has special rules). Plus PAN, cancelled cheque, menu, food photos.
- **Commissions:** Swiggy ~15–25%; Zomato base ~7% with low-volume plans. **Effective take rate is widely reported at 18–30%** once delivery, ads, and payment-gateway costs layer in. No fixed onboarding fee.
- Direct channels (Instagram, WhatsApp, word-of-mouth) avoid commissions and suit a home baker better initially.

**Startup cost & income:**
- **Basic home setup: ~INR 25,000–60,000** (OTG oven, hand mixer, ingredients, packaging, tools). More-equipped setup (convection oven, stand mixer, refrigeration, branding): ~INR 80,000–2,50,000+.
- **Beginner income: ~INR 8,000–35,000 gross/month** (Truffle Nation; Cakesify), with home-bakery gross margins of 50–80% (DineOpen).
- **Established baker / small cloud kitchen: ~INR 50,000–2,00,000+ gross/month**; small cloud kitchens typically gross INR 2.5–6 lakh/month at ~10–25% net margin (Restaurant India; Zopping). Top performers (INR 1.5–2.5 lakh/month) are outliers, not the median.
- Market is growing fast: cloud kitchens ~16.7% CAGR to USD 2.84 bn by 2030 (IBEF); online food delivery ~18% CAGR (Bain–Swiggy "How India Eats").

**Bengaluru vs Kolkata for baking:**
- **Bengaluru is the stronger market** — ~2nd-largest city for Zomato orders nationally (~11% of orders; Business Standard), India's #2 cloud-kitchen hub, and very high willingness to pay. Downside: saturated, high customer-acquisition cost.
- **Kolkata** is a less-contested, capital-efficient market with strong heritage bakery culture, but a lower revenue ceiling (WB per-capita income ~INR 1.9 lakh, ~20% below national average). *(Good news for the phased plan: the baking business can launch in Bengaluru and continue in Kolkata, where a differentiated artisanal niche faces less competition.)*

## 4. Practical considerations

- **Work authorization: confirmed — no visa needed.** An Indian citizen works freely. An **OCI cardholder can live and work in India indefinitely with no separate work visa or employment permit**; the MEA OCI FAQ grants OCI holders "parity with NRIs in financial, economic and educational fields" (except agricultural-property purchase). Restrictions (no government service, no agricultural land, no voting) **do not affect private-sector edtech/school/operations/HR/content jobs or a baking business** (MEA OCI FAQ; Bureau of Immigration). Keep her OCI card current and verify the restricted-activities list on the official MEA/BOI pages.
- **Childcare / domestic help is affordable and abundant**, which makes working realistic: full-time cooks/nannies/maids are widely available in both Bengaluru and Kolkata at a small fraction of US costs, and daycare/after-school options are plentiful in Bengaluru. This is a genuine enabler for a working-mother household.

## 5. City recommendation

**For employment: Bengaluru wins clearly** — the largest pool of edtech, education-operations, academic-success, international-school, and content roles, at higher pay than Kolkata. **For the phased move,** structure it as: take a **Bengaluru job/baking launch in Phase 1**, then shift to **remote/freelance + baking in the Kolkata phase**, where costs are lower and the artisanal niche is less contested. Kolkata's thinner job market matters less if her Phase-2 income is remote/portable.

## 6. First-90-day action checklist (prioritized)

1. **Sort work documents:** ensure OCI card (or Indian passport) is current; keep PAN ready (apply if lapsed) — needed for jobs, FSSAI, GST, and banking.
2. **Open/activate an Indian bank account** and get a local mobile number + UPI — prerequisites for everything below.
3. **Rebuild her LinkedIn + Naukri profiles** for the India market: lead with MBA + US-school experience; target keywords like "academic operations," "student success," "instructional design," "admissions." Start applying to Bengaluru edtech firms and international schools.
4. **Decide the primary track** (employment vs. baking vs. both/remote) — many returnees run baking + freelance tutoring in parallel while job-hunting.
5. **If pursuing baking:** file **FSSAI Basic Registration (Form A, INR 100/year)** via FoSCoS; buy the basic home setup (~INR 25,000–60,000); start a branded Instagram/WhatsApp order channel before touching aggregators.
6. **Line up domestic help / childcare** early so her schedule is workable from day one.
7. **Consult a CA** on GST (if selling food and on income) and on Indian tax residency for any remote US income.
8. **Network locally:** join Bengaluru edtech/education and home-baker community groups (LinkedIn, Facebook home-baker groups) — most roles come through referrals.
