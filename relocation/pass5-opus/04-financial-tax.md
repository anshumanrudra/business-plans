# Financial & Tax Plan

> **Disclaimer:** This is general information for planning, not advice. Cross-border US-India tax is complex and penalties are severe. Before acting, confirm everything with a **cross-border CPA (US side)** and a **Chartered Accountant who handles NRI/RNOR returns (India side)**. Figures use 2025-26 rates; INR/USD at ~₹86/$1.

---

## 1. Tax Residency: Where You Owe Tax

**US side (Husband on H-1B):**
- H-1B status by itself does **not** keep you a US tax resident. H-1B is *not* an "exempt individual" — it's pure day-count under the **Substantial Presence Test (SPT)**: 31+ days this year AND 183+ weighted days = (current-year days) + ⅓(prior year) + ⅙(2nd prior year). *(IRS, Substantial Presence Test)*
- Once you move to India and your weighted US days fall below 183, you become a **US nonresident alien** for tax — different from a green-card holder, who is taxed on worldwide income until formally abandoning the card. *(IRS Green Card Test)*
- **Year of departure = dual-status return** (Form 1040 for resident portion + 1040-NR for nonresident portion; see Pub 519). Even after becoming a nonresident, **US-source income still gets a US 1040-NR**, and any year you're still a US tax resident you report worldwide income.
- ⚠️ Important nuance: if you keep enough US ties / US-source W-2 wages, you may *choose* or be required to keep filing as a resident. The bigger lever is the **state** (see §4).

**India side (FY runs April-March):** You become **Resident** if either:
- 182+ days in India in the FY, **or**
- 60+ days this FY **and** 365+ days across the prior 4 FYs. *(ClearTax / Income Tax Act §6)*

**RNOR — your most valuable tax shield.** You are **Resident-but-Not-Ordinarily-Resident** (and pay India tax **only on India-source / India-received income**, NOT on foreign income) if you were:
- Non-resident in **9 of the 10** preceding years, **OR**
- in India ≤ **729 days** over the preceding **7 years**. *(Income Tax Act §6(6))*

Since you've been in the US for years, you'll comfortably qualify as RNOR. **This shields your US salary, 401k, US rental income, and US capital gains from Indian tax for ~2-3 years after you land** — the single biggest savings window in this whole plan.

**Action / Timeline:**
- **Time your move date.** Land **after Sept 30** in any year so you stay under 182 India-days that first FY → likely **Non-Resident** year 1, then **RNOR** for ~2 more years.
- Keep a **day-count log** (boarding passes, passport stamps) for both countries — this is the evidence base for everything below.
- Map out the exact FY your RNOR window ends; plan all big US income events (Roth conversions, gains, 401k moves) **before** you become Ordinarily Resident.

## 2. Avoiding Double Taxation

Because the **DTAA "saving clause"** lets the US tax its own residents/green-card holders regardless of treaty, the practical tools are credits and exclusions, not treaty exemption.

- **Foreign Tax Credit — Form 1116 (use this, not FEIE).** Dollar-for-dollar US credit for India taxes paid. India's top marginal rate (~39-43%) **exceeds** the US top rate (37%), so once you pay India tax, FTC typically **wipes out your US tax and banks excess credits** (carry back 1 / forward 10 years). *(IRS Form 1116)*
- **FEIE — Form 2555** excludes ~**$130,000** of 2025 foreign earned income (~$132,900 for 2026, confirm). It **does** apply to US W-2 wages if the *work is physically performed in India* (employer location is irrelevant). **But two reasons to prefer FTC:** (1) FEIE-excluded income **does not count as compensation for Roth/IRA contributions**, and (2) FTC is usually a bigger offset given high India rates. Note: revoking FEIE locks you out for 5 years. *(IRS Pub 54)*
- **No US-India Totalization Agreement.** ⚠️ This is a real, unavoidable cost. If you stay on US payroll you pay **US FICA 7.65%** (or 15.3% self-employed) **and** potentially **Indian EPF (~12%+12%)** — and **FTC cannot offset US Social Security tax**. Minimizing dual social-security cost is a key reason to get the payroll structure right (§3). *(Greenback)*

**Mandatory disclosure filings (huge penalties — do not skip):**
- **FBAR / FinCEN 114** — if all your foreign accounts combined ever exceed **$10,000** in a year. Non-willful penalty ~$16k *per form*. Due Apr 15, auto-extends to Oct 15.
- **FATCA / Form 8938** — living abroad MFJ thresholds **$400k year-end / $600k anytime**. Penalty $10k+, up to $60k. *(IRS / FinCEN)*

**Action:** Engage a cross-border CPA who files 1116 + FBAR + 8938 as a package. Decide FTC vs FEIE **before** the first India return.

## 3. Remote Work — Husband (US employer) & Wife (India job)

**Husband working remotely from India for a US employer:**
- **Sourcing:** Salary for work *physically performed in India* is **India-source income** and taxable in India once you're India-resident — regardless of where the employer is or what currency pays you. *(Income Tax Act §9(1)(ii))* During your **RNOR/NR window**, foreign-source income is shielded, but income for work done *in India* may still be India-taxable depending on residency status that FY — confirm with your CA.
- **Permanent Establishment (PE) risk for Disney:** A home office used habitually, or an employee concluding contracts, can create a PE; the US-India treaty's **Service PE** triggers at **>90 days** of services in India in a 12-month window. This is **Disney's risk**, and most employers will *not* allow indefinite India remote work on US payroll. *(US-India DTAA Art. 5; Deel)*
- **Confirm with Disney/employer:**
  1. Will they permit remote work from India at all, and for how long?
  2. Will they keep you on **US W-2 payroll**, move you to an **Employer of Record (EOR)** in India, or a Disney India entity?
  3. Who handles India TDS / PF / advance tax?
- **Best structure:** An **EOR or India entity** becomes your legal India employer, runs Indian withholding, and shields Disney from PE. Avoid **dual payroll** (double withholding headaches). If you stay US W-2, you'll likely owe **India advance/self-assessment tax** directly (no Indian TDS), then claim US FTC. *(NB: this also aligns with the immigration recommendation to move to India payroll or take leave — see §1 immigration.)*

**Wife working in India (H-4 EAD becomes irrelevant once in India):**
- Her Indian salary is **India-source and fully taxable in India** from day one of employment, even during the RNOR window (RNOR only shields *foreign* income).
- She files an India return; new regime tax is ~0 up to ₹3-4L, rising to 30% above ₹15L + cess. Her EPF (~12%) is mandatory with an Indian employer.
- ⚠️ If she remains a **US tax resident/green-card holder** (or you file US MFJ), her India salary is **also** reportable to the IRS — offset with FTC. Coordinate filing status (MFJ vs MFS) with your CPA.

## 4. Keeping the US House

- **If you keep it empty:** You still deduct **mortgage interest** (acquisition-debt cap $750k — you're well under at $200k) and property tax if you itemize. No income, no depreciation. Net: you carry ~$3.2k/mo with only the interest portion deductible.
- **If you rent it out (recommended financially):**
  - Report rent on **Schedule E**; deduct mortgage interest, property tax, insurance, repairs, management fees, and **depreciation** (building basis ÷ 27.5 yrs, straight-line). Depreciation often makes rental income near tax-neutral. *(IRS Pub 527)*
  - ⚠️ **Nonresident-alien landlord trap:** If you've become a US *nonresident alien*, default withholding is **30% of GROSS rent**. Avoid this by filing **Form W-8ECI** with your property manager and making the **IRC §871(d) net election** → taxed on *net* income at graduated rates via **Form 1040-NR**. *(IRS Pub 515)* (If you remain a US resident filer, normal Schedule E applies — no 30% issue.)
- **State tax nexus:** ⚠️ State residency follows **domicile**, not federal rules. Keeping the house is a strong domicile tie — aggressive states (CA, NY, etc.) may keep taxing you. If you're in such a state, consider steps to break domicile (change driver's license, voter reg, etc.) before leaving.

**Recommendation:** **Rent it out.** Rent likely covers most/all of the $3.2k mortgage, depreciation shelters the income, and you preserve a US asset and credit. Use a property manager (~8-10% of rent) since you'll be abroad.

## 5. 401k Roth While Abroad

- **Contributions need US earned income that is NOT FEIE-excluded.** If you exclude wages via FEIE you **can't contribute** to Roth/IRA — yet another reason to use **FTC instead of FEIE** if you want to keep contributing. Roth MFJ phase-out is $236k-$246k MAGI (2025). *(IRS Pub 590-A)*
- ⚠️ **India may NOT honor Roth tax-free status.** India has no "Roth" concept; once you're an **Ordinarily Resident**, India could tax Roth growth and/or distributions as ordinary income. The DTAA pension article (private pensions, ~Art. 20) generally assigns taxing rights to the country of residence — which would be India.
- **Strong recommendation: leave the Roth (and 401k) untouched while in India.** Do **not** take distributions once you're Ordinarily Resident. If you *do* want to access or convert, do it **during the RNOR window**, when foreign-source retirement income escapes Indian tax.
- Do **not** roll a 401k into anything India-side or close it. Keep it parked at your US custodian (confirm the custodian allows a foreign mailing address — many restrict it; keep a US address if needed).

## 6. Savings Strategy — The Core Opportunity

**The math that makes this work:** You potentially keep a **US Lead-Engineer salary** (₹86/$ leverage) while spending at **Indian cost of living**, with **RNOR shielding** that US income from India tax for ~2-3 years. This is the wealth-building window.

**Monthly household budget, family of 4 (INR):**

|---|---|---|
| 3BHK rent, good area | ₹60,000-1,50,000 | ₹30,000-70,000 |
| Groceries | ₹25,000-40,000 | ₹20,000-35,000 |
| Cook + maid + driver | ₹40,000-70,000 | ₹30,000-52,000 |
| Transport (car + fuel) | ₹12,000-20,000 | ₹12,000-20,000 |

| **Living subtotal (excl. school)** | **~₹1.4-3.0 L** | **~₹1.0-1.9 L** |
| Intl/premium school, 2 kids (annual) | ₹8-26 L (≈₹0.7-2.2L/mo) | ₹2-12 L (≈₹0.2-1.0L/mo) |

*(Sources: Numbeo Jun 2026; market estimates for help/schools.)*

- **Kolkata is materially cheaper** — rent ~50-60% lower and international schooling far cheaper (e.g. Numbeo intl primary ₹1.67L Kolkata vs ₹4.0L Bengaluru). Bengaluru wins on remote-tech ecosystem, climate, and school *availability* (IB schools).
- **Realistic all-in monthly burn (incl. midrange school):** Bengaluru ~₹2.5-4.0L (~$2,900-4,650); Kolkata ~₹1.5-2.5L (~$1,750-2,900).
- **Savings illustration:** A Lead SWE keeping, say, ~$12-15k/mo net could plausibly **save 50-70%+** of income after a ~$3k US mortgage (ideally offset by rent) and Indian living costs — far more than is achievable on US cost of living. Even part of your wife's India salary becomes pure savings.

**Parents' support (father on dialysis):** Money you send to your parents from your **NRE/NRO** funds is generally not taxable to them as "gift" (gifts to specified relatives are exempt under India law), and dialysis/medical costs in India are a fraction of US costs. Budget a dedicated line (~₹30k-1L/mo depending on care level) — it's affordable within the savings above.

**Currency & remittance:**
- Move US savings to India via **Wise** (mid-market rate + ~0.4-0.6% fee; volume discounts at $25k+/mo) — far cheaper than bank wires (2-4% FX markup + $25-50 fee).
- Park converted funds in **NRE** (tax-free, fully repatriable) — see §7.
- ⚠️ Don't over-convert to INR — keep an emergency USD buffer to avoid rupee-depreciation risk on money you'll need back in the US (mortgage, Tesla loan).

**Tesla loan:** Keep paying the **0% APR $30k** from your US bank via autopay — at 0% there's zero reason to prepay; that cash earns more elsewhere.

## 7. Banking Setup (NRE / NRO / FEMA)

- ⚠️ **FEMA vs Income-Tax residency differ.** Under **FEMA**, leaving for employment/long stay makes you a "**person resident outside India**" (NRI) essentially **immediately** — so you can open NRI accounts right after you move, even before you hit India tax-residency day counts. *(RBI FEMA framework)*
- **NRE account** (rupee, funded from foreign earnings): interest **tax-free in India**, **fully repatriable**. Use for money you bring from the US. Carries INR exchange-rate risk.
- **NRO account** (for India-source income — wife's salary, US-house rent if routed to India, parents' transactions): interest **taxable**, TDS ~31%, repatriation **capped at $1M/FY** (needs Forms 15CA/15CB).
- **FCNR(B)** term deposit: held in **USD/foreign currency**, interest tax-free, no rupee risk, fully repatriable — good for your USD emergency buffer.
- **Convert your existing resident savings/bank accounts to NRO** once you're an NRI (legally required — you can't keep ordinary resident accounts as an NRI).
