## 1. Curriculum Choice for Seamless US Re-Entry

The single biggest lever for avoiding credit loss. Ranked by ease of transferring **back to a US school mid-stream**:

| Curriculum | Age/Grade | US re-entry ease | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **US-accredited / American diploma** (in-India school or accredited online) | All | ★★★★★ Best | Regionally accredited (Cognia/WASC/Middle States) transcript maps directly to Carnegie units + US GPA. No re-evaluation, already on Aug-Jun calendar (US Dept. of Education / USNEI: foreign-credit recognition is decentralized to the receiving school — a US-accredited record sidesteps it entirely). |
| **Cambridge IGCSE** (→ AS/A-Level) | ~14-16, Gr 9-10 | ★★★★ Strong | "Accepted by leading universities and employers worldwide" (Cambridge International). Evaluated course-by-course by US registrars; well understood. IGCSE is course-level, not a diploma — the big US-college payoff attaches to **AS/A-Level (AICE)**, which earns college credit (Cambridge USA recognition). |
| **IB (PYP/MYP/DP)** | PYP 3-12; MYP 11-16; DP 11-12 | ★★★ Strong, needs conversion | DP transfers cleanly (HL scores 5-7 earn US college credit; recorded as honors/AP-weighted). **MYP is a framework, NOT a credit transcript** (IB/ibo.org) — the school issues a 1-7 report; US schools place via course-by-course review + sometimes a placement test, not a credential. |
| **CBSE** (India national board) | All | ★★ Workable, most friction | Rigorous, English-medium, routinely accepted — but **no GPA/credit hours**; needs manual subject-to-credit mapping + marks-to-GPA conversion (CBSE CGPA×9.5 ≈ %). Board exams (AISSE gr10, AISSCE gr12) are terminal Feb-Mar; pre-board years are only school report cards. |

### Recommendations

- **Elder son (13, the critical case):** He will do **grades 8 → 9 → 10 in India, then enter US grades 9-12**. The danger is arriving in US high school (where Carnegie-unit credits start counting toward graduation in grade 9) with a foreign record that must be back-converted.
  - **First choice: Cambridge IGCSE** (Gr 9-10) — the cleanest non-US foreign record for US high school, course-by-course and registrar-familiar. **Strategy: have him sit the IGCSE exams (or CBSE grade-10 AISSE board) before returning** — an externally-validated certificate converts far more cleanly than a half-finished year. Then do US grades 11-12 stateside for a clean, college-ready GPA.
  - **Strongest hedge if budget allows: a US-accredited school in India or an accredited US online high school** so he stays on the US system the entire time (see §5) — eliminates both credit conversion and the calendar mismatch.
  - **Avoid pure CBSE for the elder son** unless cost forces it: most back-conversion friction, weakest mid-grade records.
- **Younger son (7, grade 2):** Curriculum choice is **low-stakes** — primary grades don't carry transferable credit. Any of IB PYP, Cambridge Primary, or even a good CBSE school transfers fine on age/grade norms. Optimize his choice for **fit, location, and cost**, and let the elder son drive the curriculum decision so **both kids attend the same school**.

> **Plan-level note:** Because the family is moving **Bengaluru → Kolkata** mid-stay (see City Decision), prioritize a **board present in BOTH cities** — **Cambridge IGCSE** or the **DPS/CBSE** network both qualify — so the Phase-1→Phase-2 move costs no academic continuity.

## 4. Mid-Year Admission Reality (Apr-Mar vs Aug-Jun)

The ~2-3 month relocation timing is the core gap risk. India ends in **March**; US ends ~June, restarts ~Aug/Sep.

- **Time the move to the US fall (Aug/Sep) for return, and to the Indian April start (or a clean mid-year join) for arrival.** A student finishing an Indian grade in March is naturally placed into the next US grade for the fall — turning the April-August window into a normal summer, not a disruption.
- **Bridging options for the arrival into India:**
  - Apply for a **mid-year/waitlist seat** now (cycle for 2026-27 is mostly closed); top international schools sometimes admit off-cycle.
  - If a seat opens only in April 2026 (next Indian year), bridge the US-summer-to-April gap with **continued US online schooling** (see §5) so there is literally no idle period.
  - For the younger son (grade 2), schools are flexible — mid-year entry is routine.
- **For the elder son**, prioritize entering at a natural grade boundary and, ideally, **complete grade-10 board/IGCSE exams in India before the US return** so he carries an externally-validated record.

## 5. Avoiding an Education Lapse on RETURN to US

A **US-regionally-accredited transcript needs no credential evaluation** — it maps straight to Carnegie units and a US GPA. The accreditors that matter: **Cognia** (ex-AdvancED/SACS), **WASC**, **Middle States (MSA)**, **NEASC**.

**American-curriculum / US-accredited schools in India** (none in Kolkata/Bengaluru-south are American-diploma; nearest options):

|---|---|---|
| American Embassy School | New Delhi | Middle States, CIS, IB |
| American School of Bombay | Mumbai | Middle States, IB |
| American International School Chennai | Chennai | "American" — **verify exact accreditation directly** |
| Canadian International / Stonehill | Bangalore (north) | NEASC, IB, (CIS IGCSE) — IB-led, not American diploma; also far north of your corridor |

**Accredited ONLINE US high school** (lets the elder son keep/earn a US diploma from India — the strongest single hedge against both credit loss and the calendar mismatch):
| School | Accreditation | Approx. annual cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **University of Nebraska High School (UNHS)** | Cognia + Nebraska DOE; NCAA-approved | **~$2,000-3,000/yr** | **Most economical**; full US diploma; welcomes international |
| Laurel Springs | WASC + Cognia | ~$11,700/yr (Academy ~$17,250) | International supported |
| Stanford Online HS | WASC | high-$20Ks-$30K+ | Highly selective, full-time |
| James Madison HS (Ashworth) | Cognia + DEAC | ~$1,000-1,600 total (self-paced) | Confirm international eligibility |
| **Stride/K12** | Cognia | — | **India is on K12's ineligible list — not available** |

**Practical model for the elder son:** Enroll in a strong **IGCSE school in India for the social/in-person experience**, AND/OR keep an **accredited US online enrollment (UNHS is the value pick)** running in parallel or as a fallback so he can graduate with a US high school diploma if return timing slips. The younger son needs none of this.

**Documentation to maintain (critical for course-by-course evaluation on return):**
- Official sealed/translated **transcripts and term report cards** for every year
- **Board certificates** (IGCSE results, CBSE AISSE grade-10, etc.)
- **Course descriptions and syllabi** — the single most useful item for a US registrar matching foreign courses to US credits
- **School accreditation evidence** (WASC/Cognia/MSA/IB letters)
- **Standardized test records:** IB/IGCSE/AP results + PSAT/SAT/ACT, and MAP/NWEA for placement
- For the grade-12 → college step: a **WES (World Education Services) course-by-course evaluation** to convert marks to US GPA
