### H1B Visa Implications of Extended India Stay

**Key Points:**
- H1B status requires active employment with the petitioning employer in the US - working remotely from India for extended periods (1-3 years) is not compliant with H1B regulations
- H1B is tied to a specific Labor Condition Application (LCA) that specifies work location(s) in the US - working outside the US violates LCA terms
- Extended absence from US (more than a few months) while on H1B effectively abandons the status
- H1B visa stamp in passport (valid till Oct 2028) is different from H1B status - status ends when you stop working for sponsor or work from unauthorized location
- Upon returning to US after extended absence, you would need new H1B petition and visa stamping
- Time spent working outside US does NOT count toward H1B six-year maximum or toward maintaining continuous residence for green card
- Employer is required to withdraw H1B petition if employee is not working from approved US location

**Action Items:**
- [ ] Notify Disney Streaming HR/legal team about relocation plans immediately
- [ ] Request employer to place you on unpaid leave of absence (if policy allows) rather than terminate employment
- [ ] Discuss possibility of leave without pay to maintain employment relationship
- [ ] Understand that H1B status will effectively end when you relocate to India
- [ ] Plan for new H1B petition/transfer when ready to return to US (subject to cap if changing employers)
- [ ] Document all employment dates and gaps for future visa applications
- [ ] If employer agrees to remote work arrangement, understand this is technically non-compliant and carries risks

**Timeline:** Immediate - discuss with employer before departure. H1B status ends when you leave US for India relocation or when employer withdraws petition (whichever is sooner).
**Estimated Cost:** $0 for status abandonment. New H1B petition upon return: $460 base fee + $500 anti-fraud fee + $1,500 premium processing (optional) + attorney fees $2,000-$5,000 = approximately $4,460-$7,460 total

**Risks:**
- ⚠️ Working remotely from India on H1B is violation of immigration law - could result in visa revocation and future immigration consequences
- ⚠️ USCIS could issue Notice to Appear (NTA) for status violation if discovered
- ⚠️ Extended absence abandons H1B status - cannot re-enter US on expired status
- ⚠️ Future visa applications could be denied based on previous violations
- ⚠️ Employer could face penalties for LCA violations if employee works from non-approved location
- ⚠️ H1B clock continues to run even while outside US if petition remains active, wasting valuable H1B time

### OCI Card for US-Born Son

**Key Points:**
- OCI (Overseas Citizen of India) card provides lifetime multiple-entry, multi-purpose visa to India for foreign nationals of Indian origin
- 7-year-old US-born son is eligible as he was born to Indian citizen parents
- OCI is NOT dual citizenship - holder remains foreign national with special privileges
- OCI provides visa-free travel and stay in India indefinitely with no registration requirements
- OCI card must be carried with valid US passport for travel
- For minors under 20: OCI card requires renewal only once after age 20 when new passport issued (policy updated June 2021)
- Application filed through VFS Global in USA with jurisdiction based on residence (Connecticut falls under specific consular jurisdiction)
- Online application required first through Government of India OCI Services website, then VFS Global registration
- Processing involves: VFS verification (2-3 days) → Embassy/Consulate review → Ministry of Home Affairs background check
- OCI holders cannot vote, hold constitutional posts, acquire agricultural land in India (can inherit)

**Action Items:**
- [ ] Apply for OCI card immediately - do NOT wait until departure as processing takes 2-4 months
- [ ] Register online at Government of India OCI Services website first
- [ ] Upload digital photo, signature, and required documents online
- [ ] Complete VFS Global registration with Web Reference Number
- [ ] Gather required documents: son's US birth certificate, US passport (current, valid till Jun 2029), parents' Indian passports, parents' marriage certificate, proof of parents' Indian citizenship at time of child's birth
- [ ] Prepare application fee payment ($293.90 total for new OCI from USA)
- [ ] Submit application by mail to VFS Global (walk-in appointments currently unavailable)
- [ ] Track application using VFS reference number online
- [ ] Upon approval, receive OCI card and update it once after age 20 when new passport is issued

**Timeline:** Apply immediately. Standard processing: 2-4 months. Complex cases: 3-6 months. Recommended to apply at least 4 months before travel.
**Estimated Cost:** New OCI Card from USA: Consular fee $275.00 + ICWF fee $3.00 + Service fee $15.90 + Credit card processing 3% (~$9) = Total approximately $303. One-time expense, lifetime validity with one renewal after age 20.

**Risks:**
- ⚠️ Processing delays can extend to 4+ months, especially if additional documents requested or prior MHA approval needed
- ⚠️ Applications with missing/incorrect documents will be returned - causes 21+ day delay
- ⚠️ VFS Global not liable for applications lost in mail transit
- ⚠️ False documents or misrepresentation will result in application denial and potential bar from future applications
- ⚠️ Complex cases requiring prior MHA approval can take 30-120 business days
- ⚠️ Without OCI, child would need regular US citizen visa to India which requires registration for stays exceeding 180 days

### US Tax Implications While Living in India

**Key Points:**
- US citizens (7-year-old son) must file US tax returns and report worldwide income regardless of residence
- H1B holders (husband) must continue filing US taxes on worldwide income while maintaining status
- Foreign Earned Income Exclusion (FEIE) available: $120,000 for 2023, adjusted annually for inflation (likely $126,000-$130,000 for 2024-2025)
- FEIE requires meeting either Bona Fide Residence Test (full tax year residence abroad) or Physical Presence Test (330 days outside US in 12-month period)
- Foreign Tax Credit available for taxes paid to India - can offset US tax liability
- FBAR (Foreign Bank Account Report) required if aggregate foreign accounts exceed $10,000 at any time during year
- FATCA (Foreign Account Tax Compliance Act) reporting required for foreign financial assets above thresholds
- US tax filing deadline extended to June 15 automatically for overseas residents, can extend further to October 15
- Self-employment tax not reduced by FEIE - full 15.3% applies to self-employment income even if excluded from income tax
- State tax obligations continue for Connecticut - may need to establish non-residence
- India tax residency: staying 182+ days in fiscal year (April-March) triggers Indian tax residency

**Action Items:**
- [ ] File Form 2555 (Foreign Earned Income) with Form 1040 annually
- [ ] Establish Bona Fide Residence in India by maintaining home, local connections for full tax year
- [ ] Track days outside US carefully to meet 330-day Physical Presence Test if using that method
- [ ] Open Indian bank accounts and report on FBAR (FinCEN Form 114) if aggregate exceeds $10,000
- [ ] File Form 8938 (FATCA) if foreign assets exceed thresholds ($200,000 single, $400,000 married on last day of year)
- [ ] Consult with US CPA experienced in expat taxation before departure
- [ ] Register with Indian tax authorities and obtain PAN (Permanent Account Number)
- [ ] File Indian tax returns if staying 182+ days and earning Indian income
- [ ] Claim Foreign Tax Credit (Form 1116) for Indian taxes paid to offset US tax
- [ ] Update Connecticut to establish non-residence if planning extended stay (file final resident return)
- [ ] Set up estimated quarterly tax payments for US taxes to avoid penalties
- [ ] Consider Social Security Totalization Agreement between US-India to avoid double social security taxation

**Timeline:** Pre-departure tax planning: 2-3 months before leaving. Annual US filing: by June 15 (automatic extension) or October 15 (with extension request). Indian tax registration: within 1 month of arrival. Indian tax filing: by July 31 following fiscal year end (March 31). FBAR filing: April 15 (automatic extension to October 15).
**Estimated Cost:** US expat tax preparation: $500-$2,000 per year (depends on complexity). Indian tax consultant: ₹10,000-₹25,000 per year ($120-$300). Tax planning consultation pre-departure: $1,000-$3,000 one-time. Estimated annual total: $1,620-$5,300 for both countries' tax compliance.

**Risks:**
- ⚠️ Failure to file US returns while abroad results in penalties - $10,000 per year for willful FBAR violation
- ⚠️ Not claiming FEIE correctly results in double taxation
- ⚠️ Becoming Indian tax resident (182+ days) without proper planning creates dual tax obligations
- ⚠️ Connecticut may claim continued tax residency if home not rented/sold and ties maintained
- ⚠️ US retirement account (401k) distributions while in India may have different tax treatment
- ⚠️ Foreign exchange reporting violations carry severe penalties - up to $100,000 or 50% of account balance
- ⚠️ Indian TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) may be withheld at 30% for non-residents without PAN
- ⚠️ Dual tax residency can occur in year of relocation - may owe taxes to both countries on same income

### India Tax Residency and Tax Implications

**Key Points:**
- India tax residency determined by physical presence: 182+ days in fiscal year (April 1-March 31) establishes residency
- Indian tax residents pay tax on worldwide income in India
- Indian non-residents pay tax only on India-sourced income
- PAN (Permanent Account Number) required for all tax-related activities in India
- TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) withheld at 30% for non-PAN holders vs. applicable slab rates for PAN holders
- India has progressive tax slabs with new and old regimes - new regime has lower rates but fewer deductions
- US-India tax treaty prevents double taxation through Foreign Tax Credit mechanism
- NRI status changes to Resident Indian after 182 days - affects banking, investments, property rights
- As parents are Indian citizens returning, no FRRO registration required
- Income from US sources while Indian tax resident is taxable in India (with credit for US taxes paid)
- Remote work for US employer while in India creates tax nexus in both countries

**Action Items:**
- [ ] Apply for PAN card for all family members immediately upon India arrival
- [ ] Register with local income tax office in India
- [ ] Open NRE (Non-Resident External) or NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary) bank accounts initially, convert to resident accounts after 182 days
- [ ] File Indian tax returns (ITR) by July 31 for previous fiscal year if resident
- [ ] Maintain records of all foreign income and foreign taxes paid for tax credit claims
- [ ] Consult Indian Chartered Accountant (CA) for tax planning and compliance
- [ ] Update status with Indian banks and financial institutions when becoming tax resident
- [ ] Consider timing of arrival to optimize tax residency for first year (arriving after April 1 gives partial year)
- [ ] File Form 67 with Indian return to claim credit for foreign taxes paid
- [ ] Consider new vs old tax regime choice annually

**Timeline:** PAN application: apply within 1 month of arrival, receive in 15-30 days. Bank account opening: immediate upon arrival. First tax filing: July 31, 2027 for FY 2026-27 (if arriving in 2026). Ongoing: annual filing by July 31.
**Estimated Cost:** PAN card application: ₹110 per person x 4 = ₹440 ($5-6). Chartered Accountant fees: ₹10,000-₹25,000 per year ($120-$300). Tax compliance software: ₹2,000-₹5,000 per year ($25-$60). Estimated annual: ₹12,000-₹30,000 ($145-$360) plus one-time PAN fees.

**Risks:**
- ⚠️ 30% TDS without PAN vs 10-20% with PAN - significant difference on interest, dividends
- ⚠️ Penalties for late filing of Indian tax returns: ₹5,000-₹10,000
- ⚠️ Interest charged at 1% per month on unpaid taxes
- ⚠️ Failure to report foreign assets in Indian return can result in penalties
- ⚠️ Working remotely for US company from India may trigger permanent establishment issues for employer
- ⚠️ Dual tax residency in year of transition without proper planning
- ⚠️ Currency exchange rate fluctuations affect tax calculations and credits
- ⚠️ Black Money Act penalties for undisclosed foreign assets are severe
