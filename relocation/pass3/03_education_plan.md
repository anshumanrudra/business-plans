
# EDUCATION PLAN

## CURRICULUM CHOICE RECOMMENDATION: IB (International Baccalaureate) or American Curriculum

### Rationale:
1. **Smooth US Re-entry**: IB and American curricula are globally recognized and align with US Common Core standards
2. **Credit Transfer**: US schools readily accept IB/American curriculum transcripts
3. **College Preparation**: IB Diploma Programme (grades 11-12) is highly valued by US colleges
4. **English Language**: IB/American schools teach in English, maintain language proficiency
5. **Extracurriculars**: IB schools emphasize well-rounded education (sports, arts, community service)

### Why NOT CBSE/ICSE:
- **Curriculum Gap**: CBSE/ICSE curricula differ significantly from US Common Core (especially math sequence)
- **Grade-Level Risk**: Transitioning back may require repeating a grade or summer catch-up courses
- **Language**: More local language exposure (Hindi/Kannada), less English emphasis
- **College Prep**: US SAT/ACT prep not integrated; would need separate tutoring

### Curriculum Choice for US Return

**Key Points:**
- IB (International Baccalaureate) curriculum provides the smoothest transition back to US schools with direct credit recognition and global standardization
- IGCSE/Cambridge curriculum (British system) is widely recognized by US schools and colleges, offers good international mobility

- [ ] Keep detailed academic records and syllabi for all courses taken in India
- [ ] Consider supplementary SAT prep if 13-year-old will be applying to US high schools from India

**Timeline:** Research and finalize curriculum choice: Immediate (Week 1-2). Schools typically admit mid-year (January) or for new academic year (April-June)

### School Admission Process & Mid-Year Considerations

**Key Points:**
- Indian academic year runs April-March, opposite to US August-May calendar
- Main admission intake: January-March for April start
- Mid-year admissions (October-January start): Available at some international schools but limited availability
- Admission typically requires: entrance tests (age-appropriate), previous school records, transfer certificate from US school, immunization records, passport/visa documents
- International schools more flexible with mid-year admissions than CBSE/ICSE schools
- Waiting lists common at top schools - apply to multiple schools simultaneously
- Some schools require parent interviews and student interaction sessions
- Processing time: 2-6 weeks from application to admission decision

**Action Items:**
- [ ] If relocating in 2-3 months (September-October 2026), target January 2027 mid-year admission at international schools
- [ ] Request Transfer Certificate from current Connecticut schools (required for Indian school admission)
- [ ] Get school records apostilled through Connecticut Secretary of State if required
- [ ] Complete immunization updates per Indian school requirements (typically similar to US but may require additional vaccines)
- [ ] Register both children for entrance assessments at multiple schools to maximize acceptance chances
- [ ] Prepare children for potential grade-level assessment tests in Math and English
- [ ] If mid-year admission unavailable, consider online US curriculum school for gap months (October-March) then enroll in April 2027

**Timeline:** Start applications immediately upon city selection. Plan for 3-month lead time minimum. If mid-year unavailable, arrange temporary online schooling for October 2026-March 2027 gap

### Credit Transfer Back to US Schools

**Key Points:**
- IB curriculum credits transfer most smoothly to US schools - recognized by all states
- IGCSE credits generally accepted but may require transcript evaluation
- CBSE/ICSE transcripts require evaluation by credential evaluation service (e.g., WES, ECE) for formal US equivalency
- Grade placement upon return determined by US school district - may differ from Indian grade level
- Advanced coursework in Math/Science in Indian schools often exceeds US grade-level expectations
- US schools may require placement tests rather than automatic grade advancement
- Gaps in US-specific subjects (US History, State History, American Literature) will need remediation

**Action Items:**
- [ ] Before leaving Connecticut, obtain written guidance from school district on re-entry procedures and credit acceptance
- [ ] Keep detailed records: course syllabi, textbooks used, grading scales, teacher credentials, assessment reports
- [ ] Request official transcripts from Indian school every semester in English on school letterhead
- [ ] Upon return, submit transcripts to credential evaluation service 2-3 months before re-enrollment
- [ ] Arrange for US History/Civics tutoring or online courses during India years to prevent gaps
- [ ] Consider having children take Connecticut Mastery Tests or similar assessments remotely to maintain state-aligned benchmarks
- [ ] Maintain Connecticut residency documentation to avoid non-resident enrollment issues
- [ ] Document all extracurricular achievements, awards, competitions for US school applications

**Timeline:** Begin credit transfer documentation from Day 1 in Indian school. Plan 2-3 months for credential evaluation upon US return
**Estimated Cost:** Credential evaluation service: $150-300 per student. Supplementary US curriculum tutoring: $30-60/hour if needed
