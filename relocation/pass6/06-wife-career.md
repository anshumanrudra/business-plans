## 3. Baking / Cooking Entrepreneurship

**FSSAI license:**

| Category | Annual turnover | Fee/year |
|---|---|---|
| **Basic Registration (Form A)** | under ₹12 lakh | **₹100/year** ✅ start here |
| State License (Form B) | ₹12 lakh – 20 crore | ₹2,000–5,000 (only if scaling) |
| Central License | above ₹20 crore | ₹7,500 (n/a) |

- **Process (FoSCoS, foscos.fssai.gov.in):** file Form A online → upload photo ID, premises proof, product list, food-safety declaration, bank details → 14-digit registration number + certificate in **~7–30 days**. Renew 30 days before expiry. Also do the free **Udyam/MSME registration** (loans/govt-scheme credibility).
- **Selling on Swiggy/Zomato:** FSSAI mandatory; both commonly also require a **GST number**. **Commissions: Swiggy ~15–25%, Zomato base ~7%; effective take rate 18–30%** after delivery/ads/gateway. **Start with direct Instagram/WhatsApp channels** (no commission) before touching aggregators.

**Four business models (investment tiers):**
1. **Home baking** — ₹50,000–1,00,000 setup (OTG/convection oven ₹15–25k, stand mixer ₹10–15k, pans/tools ₹5–10k, ingredients ₹10k, packaging ₹5k, marketing ₹5k).
2. **Cloud/commercial kitchen** — ₹2–5L; rent ₹15–30k/mo; trigger at 20+ orders/week.
3. **Catering** — ₹1–2L; corporate/birthday/wedding events; ₹50,000–1,50,000/month by events.
4. **Cooking classes (high-margin, scalable)** — ₹20–50k setup; **American-baking classes** for Indian home bakers, online or in-person; ₹1,500–3,000/person/session; 2–3 classes/month → ₹15,000–90,000/month.

**Product pricing:** cookies ₹200–300/dozen; cakes ₹800–2,000; custom cakes ₹1,500–5,000.

**Income & margins:** home-bakery gross margins **50–80%**; beginner **₹8,000–35,000 gross/month**; established / small cloud kitchen **₹50,000–2,00,000+/month** (₹1.5–2.5L/month are outliers, not median).

**The niche:** **American-style baking (brownies, cookies, cupcakes, artisan breads) is underserved** — target Bengaluru's expat + high-disposable-income tech crowd via a branded Instagram/WhatsApp presence.

**Bengaluru vs Kolkata for baking:** BLR = larger market, high willingness-to-pay, but saturated/high customer-acquisition cost. Kolkata = 30–40% lower operating cost, strong heritage bakery + wedding-catering demand, less competition but lower ceiling. **Good for the phased plan: launch in Bengaluru, continue as a differentiated artisanal niche in Kolkata.**

## 9. Green-Card Path From Her Credentials (researched 2026-07-11)

*You asked for a GC pathway built on the wife's credentials, as a parallel to your EB-1A/EB-1C. Researched against USCIS category pages, the DOS Visa Bulletin (July 2026), 9 FAM, and credential-evaluator (WES) guidance. **Honest bottom line: an independent track for her is a low-value hedge, not a real second engine.***

**The decisive point — she's already covered as your derivative spouse.** If your **EB-1A or EB-1C is approved, she gets her green card automatically** as your spouse: same petition, your **May-2021 priority date**, same Mumbai consular interview, no separate I-140. Since EB-1 India is far more current than EB-2/EB-3 India, **your petition is both faster and easier than anything she could file herself.** So default to derivative-spouse; treat any independent filing as pure insurance.

**What she could realistically qualify for on her own — and why each is worse than riding your petition:**

| Category | Realistic? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| EB-1 (A/B/C) | **No** | Needs national/international acclaim or a multinational manager/exec year abroad — not her profile. |
| EB-2 (advanced degree) | **Unlikely** | Her **distance MBA + 3-yr bachelor almost certainly fails** the "US master's or single foreign equivalent" test (see degree problem below). Also **EB-2 India is "Unavailable"** on the July 2026 bulletin. |
| EB-2 exceptional ability | **Unlikely** | Requires ≥3 of 6 stringent criteria showing expertise "significantly above the ordinary." |
| **EB-2 NIW** | **Stretch — the only *self-petition* option** | Waives job offer + PERM, so she could file from India. But she must first clear the EB-2 bar (which her degree likely doesn't), then pass the *Dhanasar* 3-prong test. An education/edtech "endeavor" framing is directionally right but she's weak on "well-positioned to advance it." **Break-glass only.** |
| EB-3 (skilled/professional) | **Most qualifiable on paper, worst in practice** | Fits her best as "skilled worker," BUT needs a **US employer + PERM labor certification** (impractical from India) AND **EB-3 India final-action date is 01-JAN-2014** — a ~12-year backlog, *worse than your EB-1*. |

**The distance-MBA / 3-year-degree problem (why EB-2-by-degree fails):** USCIS counts *years of study*, not content. A **3-year Indian bachelor's ≠ a US bachelor's** (needs 4 years); a US-master's-equivalent generally needs a single ~6-year degree chain built on a 4-year bachelor's — and you **can't staple a 3-yr bachelor + MBA together** to synthesize one. Sikkim Manipal is UGC-recognized so **WES can evaluate the distance MBA**, but a favorable WES opinion is **not binding** — USCIS decides equivalency at its discretion, and distance degrees draw extra scrutiny. Realistic outcome: evaluates to a **US bachelor's at best → routes to EB-3, not EB-2.**

**Cross-chargeability (verify her birthplace):** If she — or you — was **born outside India** in a country with a current visa bulletin, the family can charge the principal to that birthplace (INA 202(b) / 9 FAM 503.2) and skip the India backlog. Turns on country of **birth, not citizenship.** If she was born in India, this gives **nothing** — but it's worth confirming her exact place of birth, since it would be a large lever if a non-India birthplace exists.

**➡️ Recommendation:**
1. **Default: derivative spouse on your EB-1A/EB-1C** — fastest, cheapest, highest-probability, inherits your 2021 PD. Do nothing else unless a failure scenario hits.
2. **Break-glass hedge (EB-2 NIW, education/edtech framing)** — pursue *only* if **both your EB-1A and EB-1C stall/deny**, or derivative status is ever at risk. First concrete step if triggered: a **detailed WES/AACRAO course-by-course evaluation** of her MBA + bachelor's to see if it reaches a US master's (unlikely) or only a US bachelor's (likely).
3. **Verify her (and your) country of birth** for cross-chargeability — cheap to check, potentially decisive.
4. Her India-years work (edtech ops, a recognized cert like SHRM-CP, any published/leadership work) modestly strengthens a *future* NIW endeavor argument — a reason to build the résumé regardless, but not a reason to file now.
