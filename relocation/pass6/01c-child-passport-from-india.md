# Renewing the 7-yo's US Passport from India

*pass6 addendum, 2026-07-02. Step-by-step path to obtain a new US passport for the younger son (US citizen) from India. Core requirements below were verified on **travel.state.gov** (US Dept of State, Jun 2026); India-post logistics marked **[verify]** should be confirmed on the relevant US Embassy/Consulate page before you act, as appointment systems change.*

> **Timing:** His passport expires **Jun 2029**. Under-16 passports are valid only **5 years and CANNOT be renewed by mail — every one is a fresh in-person DS-11 application.** Two viable windows: **(A) renew NOW in the US before you leave** (strongly preferred — simplest), or **(B) apply from India** if it lapses during the stay. This file covers both, emphasizing (B).

---

## 0. The Core Rule That Shapes Everything

A child under 16 **cannot renew** — you **reapply in person on Form DS-11**, and **both parents/guardians must appear with the child** (or the absent parent consents via a notarized **DS-3053**). This is true whether you do it in the US or at a US post in India. So the whole task is really about **getting both parents + the child + the right documents to a US post**, once.

---

## 1. STRONGLY PREFERRED: Renew in the US Before You Leave (Window A)

Because his passport is valid until Jun 2029 and you're leaving ~late 2026, **you can apply for a fresh 5-year passport now** while all three of you are in the US.

- **Why do it now:** a US-side DS-11 at any passport-acceptance facility (post office/library/clerk) or regional agency is far easier than a consular appointment in India — no overseas notarization, no long consular waits, both parents already present, records on hand.
- **The math:** a passport issued in late 2026 is valid **5 years → ~2031**, comfortably covering your entire 2–3 year stay and the return. **This likely eliminates the need to deal with India passport logistics at all.**
- **Action:** add "renew 7-yo's US passport" to the Phase-0 pre-move tasks; it's already on the tracker. Do it early (routine processing is 4–6 weeks; add mailing time).

**Only fall to Window B (apply from India) if:** you didn't renew before leaving, OR the passport is lost/damaged/expires while abroad.

---

## 2. Where to Apply in India (Window B)

US passport services for citizens are provided at the **US Embassy New Delhi** and the **Consulates in Mumbai, Chennai, Hyderabad, and Kolkata** through **American Citizen Services (ACS)**, **by appointment**. **[verify which post covers your location + that it processes minor DS-11s]**
- From **Bengaluru (Phase 1):** **Chennai** or **Hyderabad** consulate is closest. **[verify current jurisdiction]**
- From **Kolkata (Phase 2):** the **Kolkata** consulate. **[verify it offers full passport/ACS services — some posts have limited ACS]**
- Appointments are booked online through the post's ACS system; **book early — slots can be weeks out.** **[verify booking portal]**

---

## 3. Required Documents (DS-11, child under 16) — verified from travel.state.gov

Bring **originals + one photocopy** of each (8.5×11, single-sided). Do **not** sign the DS-11 until the ACS officer directs you.

**The application:**
- [ ] **Form DS-11** — complete via the online Form Filler, print single-sided, **do not pre-sign**.

**Evidence of the child's US citizenship** (submit ONE, physical not digital — and it's returned to you):
- [ ] His **current/expiring US passport** (a full-validity undamaged US passport is itself citizenship evidence — ideal here), **or**
- [ ] **US birth certificate**, or Consular Report of Birth Abroad, or Certificate of Citizenship.
- *(He was US-born, so the US birth certificate is your backup if the passport is lost.)*

**Evidence of parental relationship:**
- [ ] The child's **birth certificate listing both parents** (or the passport + birth certificate together).

**Both parents' ID:**
- [ ] Both parents bring **physical photo ID** (passport is fine here) **+ a photocopy of the front/back** of each.

**Photo:**
- [ ] **One** US-passport-spec photo (2×2 in, white background) — do not staple; the officer attaches it. *(Get it done at a photo studio that knows US-visa/passport specs — widely available in Indian metros.)*

**Fees (under 16):**
- [ ] **$100 application + $35 execution/facility = $135** for a passport book. (Card is +$15; expedite +$60 where offered.) **[verify payment method at post — often USD or INR-equivalent; card/DD]**

---

## 4. The Both-Parents Rule + DS-3053 (the part that needs planning abroad)

Both parents must appear with the child. If **one parent cannot attend** the India appointment:

- The **absent parent completes Form DS-3053 (Statement of Consent)** + a photocopy of their photo ID.
- ⚠️ **Critical for overseas:** for applications made abroad, the DS-3053 for the absent parent generally **must be notarized at a US Embassy/Consulate** (not a local Indian notary) for certain-country cases — **so both parents may effectively need to visit a US post anyway.** **[verify whether India requires consular notarization of the DS-3053]**
- The DS-3053 must be submitted **within 90 days** of being signed/notarized.
- **Practical implication:** it's usually simplest for **both parents + the child to go to the same ACS appointment together** — trying to avoid one parent's trip via DS-3053 often just creates a second consular visit to notarize the form.

*(Sole-custody / can't-locate-other-parent paths exist — DS-5525 etc. — but don't apply to your intact-family situation.)*

---

## 5. Processing & Logistics from India

- **Processing:** routine **4–6 weeks** at the post + **mailing/return time** — from an overseas post, allow a **generous buffer (plan 8–12 weeks door-to-door)**. **[verify current India post times — can be longer]**
- **He can't travel internationally without it** — so **never let it lapse while you might need to travel** (e.g., a family emergency trip). This is the strongest reason to **renew in the US now (Window A)** so it's valid through the whole stay.
- **OCI coordination:** he holds OCI (a lifelong visa on his US passport). A **new passport number** may require updating/re-linking the OCI ("OCI miscellaneous services" / re-issue) so the OCI stays valid for his stay in India. **[verify current MHA/OCI re-linking rule for a new passport for a minor]** — plan this right after the new passport issues.

---

## 6. Recommended Path (decision)

1. **Renew his US passport NOW, in the US, before you leave** (Window A). A late-2026 issuance is valid ~5 years (~2031) — covers the entire relocation and return, and sidesteps all India consular hassle. **This is the plan.**
2. **Only if it lapses/is lost abroad** → Window B: book an ACS appointment at the nearest US post (Chennai/Hyderabad from Bengaluru; Kolkata from WB), bring **both parents + child + DS-11 + citizenship evidence + birth certificate + photo + $135**, and budget **8–12 weeks**.
3. **After any new passport issues** (in US or India) → **update/re-link his OCI** to the new passport number.
4. Keep a **digital + physical copy** of his birth certificate and current passport with you in India — they're the citizenship evidence you'd need for Window B.
