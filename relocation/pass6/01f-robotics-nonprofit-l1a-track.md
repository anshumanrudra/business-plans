## §1 — Why a nonprofit cannot be the petitioner (and what to do instead)

Nonprofits *can* hold L-1s — religious and educational organizations do it routinely. The problem is narrower and structural.

### The qualifying-relationship problem

L-1 requires a **qualifying relationship** between the US petitioner and the foreign employer — parent, branch, subsidiary, or affiliate (8 CFR 214.2(l)(1)(ii)). Every one of those definitions is written in terms of **ownership and control**. A nonprofit corporation **has no owners**. So a US-nonprofit/India-nonprofit pair must prove the relationship on **control alone** — overlapping boards, reserved appointment powers, bylaws. It is *arguable*, and it is an RFE magnet at exactly the company size where you can least afford one.

A Delaware C-corp holding 100% of an India Pvt Ltd is the textbook case. Same reasoning as `01e §1`.

### ⚠️ The FCRA problem — the decisive one

Under India's **Foreign Contribution (Regulation) Act 2010**, money sent by a foreign source to an Indian nonprofit (Section 8 company, society, or trust) is a **"foreign contribution"** and the recipient needs **FCRA registration or prior permission**.

- **Registration requires ~3 years of existence and ≥₹15 lakh spent on core activities** over the preceding three years **[verify current thresholds]**. A brand-new entity cannot get it.
- **Prior permission** is project-specific, discretionary, slow, and increasingly refused.
- The **2020 amendments** banned **sub-granting** outright and require the designated FCRA account at **SBI New Delhi Main Branch**.

**So the obvious design — US 501(c)(3) funds an Indian Section 8 company — is blocked for at least three years.**

✅ **The lawful channel:** FCRA §2(1)(h) **excludes** payments received "by way of fee, or towards the cost in lieu of goods or services rendered in the ordinary course of business" **[verify wording]**. An **Indian for-profit Pvt Ltd invoicing a US company for services rendered** is not a foreign contribution — it is **export of services**. That is the channel, and it requires the India entity to be a **for-profit**.

### Two more reasons the C-corp does the work

| | Nonprofit-only | C-corp petitioner |
|---|---|---|
| **EB-1C ability to pay** | Must prove it from donations | Proved from contract revenue |
| **Your economics** | Nothing to own; salary only, set by an independent board under §4958 | Equity, options, an asset |

### ❌ And debunk the myth the nonprofit idea usually rests on

**A robotics-education nonprofit is NOT H-1B cap-exempt.** Cap exemption (INA 214(g)(5)) reaches only institutions of higher education, nonprofits **related to or affiliated with** one, nonprofit **research** organizations, and governmental research organizations. Teaching K-12 kids robotics is none of those. ⚠️ If anyone has told you "start a nonprofit and the H-1B cap goes away," that is wrong here — and it does not matter anyway, since the Disney H-1B is revoked on resignation and `00-MASTER-PLAN` already demotes H-1B to a distant fallback.

### So why keep the nonprofit at all?

Because you asked for it, and it earns its place — just not as the visa spine:

- ✅ **Donations, grants, and corporate STEM funding** (Amazon Future Engineer, Micron/Intel/Broadcom foundations, community foundations) that a for-profit cannot touch.
- ✅ **School and district partnerships** open to a 501(c)(3) that are closed to a vendor, plus **FIRST / VEX** ecosystem credibility.
- ✅ **Scholarship and free-cohort programs** — the actual mission.
- ✅ **EB-1A texture** for `01d`: founding and directing an educational nonprofit is real criterion-#8 (leading/critical role) material. ⚠️ Judging FIRST/VEX competitions is *weak* criterion-#4 evidence — USCIS pushes back on judging outside your field of specialization and at pre-collegiate level. Treat as supplementary, not a criterion-satisfier.
- ⚠️ **It also adds a whole compliance stack and a related-party-transaction risk to a plan that does not need either.** Honest test in §10.

## §2 — Corporate architecture

### Entity A — US 501(c)(3) nonprofit corporation ("the Foundation")

**Not an LLC** (§0). Form a **Massachusetts nonprofit corporation** under M.G.L. c.180 — the co-founder is in MA, the programs are online, and forming in MA avoids Delaware-nonstock + MA-foreign-qualification double registration. (Delaware nonstock is the alternative; it buys nothing here.)

- **Purpose clause:** **educational** under §501(c)(3), with the mandatory dissolution/asset-dedication language. Get this right in the articles — retrofitting requires an amendment and delays the determination letter.
- **Board:** ⚠️ **at least 5 directors with an independent majority.** A board of you + your wife + your friend is a related-party-controlled board: survivable for exemption, but it triggers Form 990 Part VI and Schedule L disclosure, invites state-AG attention, and — the part that matters here — **destroys the one piece of L-1A/EB-1C evidence a nonprofit board is genuinely good at producing.** An independent board approving your compensation *is* "general supervision from a board" under INA 101(a)(44)(B).
- **Exemption application: file the full Form 1023, not 1023-EZ.** The EZ ($275, ~2–4 weeks) is available under ~$50k/yr gross receipts and $250k assets, but it **does not ask about foreign activities or related organizations** — and you have both. Full 1023 is **$600 and 3–9 months** (sometimes 12+) **[verify current fee]**, and the determination letter is **retroactive to formation if filed within 27 months**. File it from India; nothing about it needs you in the US.
- **Public charity classification:** plan for **§509(a)(2)** — tuition and program fees are *program service revenue* and count toward that support test, subject to the per-payer cap. Do not assume §509(a)(1).
- ⚠️ **Private inurement / excess benefit (IRC §4958).** You are an insider. If the Foundation ever compensates you, contracts with the C-corp, or licenses anything from it, use the **rebuttable presumption of reasonableness** procedure (Treas. Reg. 53.4958-6 **[verify cite]**): independent body, comparability data, contemporaneous written documentation, recusal. Excise tax on excess benefit lands on *you personally*, not just the org.
- **Registrations:** MA AG **initial charitable registration + annual Form PC**; **Form 990** series annually (990-N/990-EZ/990 by size). ⚠️ **Soliciting donations nationally online triggers charitable-solicitation registration in ~40 states.** Practical containment: restrict *active* solicitation to MA and CT for year one; add states deliberately.
- ✅ **Useful later:** a 501(c)(3) *can* own a **disregarded single-member LLC** — the right wrapper if you ever run in-person camps and want the liability isolated.

### Entity B — Delaware C-corporation ("the Company") — the petitioner

Mirrors `01e §1` exactly, and for the same reasons (no Form 5472/$25k penalty exposure, a real board, a shareholder-officer who can be a W-2 employee, fundability).

- **MA foreign qualification** (co-founder's location), MA annual reports, and a **Written Information Security Program under 201 CMR 17.00** — the same obligations `01e` already carries.
- ⚠️ **Papered by a startup lawyer before any code exists:** founders' agreement, vesting, and **IP assignment from every founder and contractor to the Company.** See §8 for why this one is sharper here than in `01e`.

#### ⚠️ Ownership — and the FEMA decision you have nine days to make

**L-1A and EB-1C impose no ownership requirement on the beneficiary.** You can be the beneficiary of a company you own none of. That opens a genuinely useful fork:

| | **D1 — you own nothing** | **D2 — minority stake, issued pre-departure** *(recommended)* |
|---|---|---|
| Who owns the C-corp | MA co-founder (majority), wife (H-4 EAD, minority) | Co-founder majority; **you a minority, non-controlling stake** |
| FEMA round-tripping | ✅ **Problem disappears** — no Indian resident holds foreign equity | ⚠️ Written FEMA opinion still required, but a **minority non-controlling** stake is a materially easier opinion than `01e`'s majority case |
| Your economics | Compensation only; you rely on the friendship | You own the asset |
| Deadline | none | ⚠️ **shares must be issued while you are still a non-resident of India — i.e. before you leave, ~30 Sept 2026** |

Same logic as `01e §1`: acquiring foreign equity **as a non-resident** is a much better posture than acquiring it as an Indian resident under the **Overseas Investment Rules 2022** (round-tripping, two-layer cap). ⚠️ And the LRS/ODI path for capitalizing a US entity *after* you become resident is slower and capped — **put the money in before you go.**

**Fallback if Disney clearance (§8) does not land in nine days:** form under **D1**, and revisit your equity from India once counsel has the FEMA opinion in writing. Losing the stake is survivable; a written Disney IP claim over the platform is not.

### Entity C — India Private Limited Company ("India Ltd"), wholly-owned subsidiary of Entity B

- **Not an LLP, not a proprietorship.** A proprietorship is not a separate legal person, so **there is no foreign entity to have a qualifying relationship with** — fatal on its own (`01e §1`).
- Parent holds all but one share; a nominee holds one (Pvt Ltd needs two shareholders). Two directors, **≥1 resident in India ≥182 days in the FY — applied proportionately for a newly incorporated company** (Companies Act §149(3)). ⚠️ Confirm with the CA; getting it wrong means adding an outside resident director.
- **Inbound FDI, automatic route** (education services is 100% automatic **[verify current FDI policy]**), **FC-GPR within 30 days** of allotment.
- **Transfer pricing:** India Ltd builds and delivers; the C-corp holds US customer contracts. Intercompany services agreement, **cost-plus** (Safe Harbour margins for ITES), **TP study + Form 3CEB annually**, ~₹1.5–3L/yr. ⚠️ Indian TP audits are aggressive on exactly this captive-service-provider pattern — document from the first invoice.
- **GST, two separate questions:**
  - **Services billed to the US parent = export of services, zero-rated.** File a **LUT** to export without paying IGST; keep **FIRC/BRC** evidence of realization in convertible forex. ✅ A wholly-owned Indian subsidiary supplying its foreign parent *does* qualify as export — an Indian company and a foreign company are distinct persons, not establishments of the same person (CBIC Circular 161/17/2021-GST **[verify]**).
  - ⚠️ **Indian tuition billed directly to Indian parents = 18% GST.** The "educational institution" exemption (Notification 12/2017, entry 66 **[verify]**) covers pre-school to higher secondary **as part of a curriculum leading to a recognized qualification** — robotics enrichment is not that. Price it in from day one; discovering 18% at the first audit is a margin event.

### ⚠️ The firewall between the Foundation and the Company

Both entities share your name, your co-founder, and your brand. The moment money moves between them you are in **private-benefit and excess-benefit territory**, defending a transaction between a charity and a for-profit controlled by the charity's own founder.

✅ **The clean design: no money flows between them, ever.**

| | **Foundation (501(c)(3))** | **Company (C-corp → India Ltd)** |
|---|---|---|
| Who it serves | Free and scholarship cohorts; Title I schools; competition teams | Paying families; school/district contracts |
| Revenue | Donations, grants, corporate STEM funding | Tuition, B2B contracts |
| Brand | Royalty-free trademark licence **approved by the independent board, documented, no consideration either way** | Owns the marks |
| Reporting | 990 Schedules L and R disclose the relationship; do not try to hide it | — |

If you later need the Foundation to use the Company's platform, that is a **below-market or free licence approved under §4958 procedure with comparability data** — never a payment from charity to insider-controlled vendor.

## §3 — Your qualifying year: the timing decision that decides the whole track

### The rule

**8 CFR 214.2(l)(3)(iii):** one **continuous year of full-time employment abroad** with the qualifying organization, **within the three years preceding filing**, in a **managerial or executive** capacity.

Three consequences, in order of importance:

1. ⚠️ **You cannot be full-time at Disney India and full-time at India Ltd at the same time.** There is no reading of "full-time" that survives a 40-hour Disney job. The year has to be a genuine, exclusive, documented year.
2. ✅ **"Within the three years preceding filing" means the year can be banked.** It does not have to be the immediately preceding year. This matters in §7.
3. ⚠️ **"Managerial or executive," not "technical."** See §4.

### The two sequences

| | **S1 — resign at the 12-month mark** *(recommended)* | **S2 — stay at Disney into 2028** |
|---|---|---|
| Disney India | Oct 2026 → **31 Oct 2027** (12 months) | Oct 2026 → ~30 Jun 2028 |
| EB-1A filed | ~Sept 2027, **with Disney employment intact** ✅ | ~Sept 2027 ✅ |
| Your clock | **1 Nov 2027 → 1 Nov 2028** | 1 Jul 2028 → 1 Jul 2029 |
| L-1A filed | **Dec 2028** | Aug 2029 |
| Mumbai interview (3–6 mo queue) | Mar–Aug 2029 | Nov 2029 – Feb 2030 |
| **Entry** | ✅ **mid-to-late 2029 — matches the fall-2029 default return** | ⚠️ **early/mid 2030 — 6–12 months late** |
| Disney India income | 12 months | ~21 months |
| Regular vs new-office petition | Regular (C-corp has ~2 yrs + two 1120s) | Regular |

**S1 is the only sequence where this track is a return vehicle rather than a consolation prize.** It costs roughly 9 months of Disney India salary and requires India Ltd to be able to pay you by Nov 2027 — which is a revenue gate, not a wish (§9 G-R3).

⚠️ **S1's real cost is not the salary. It is §5.**

### Requirements at filing

- **Regular, not new office.** The C-corp operating from Oct 2026 with the co-founder on US payroll means a Dec 2028 filing shows ~26 months of operations, US revenue, MA registration, and **two filed Forms 1120** → **3-year validity, normal scrutiny**, rather than new-office's 1 year and heightened scrutiny.
- **"Doing business"** = the regular, systematic, continuous provision of services — **not mere existence.** Evidence file: bank statements, invoices, signed school and family contracts, co-founder payroll records, MA foreign qualification, coworking lease, Forms 1120.
- **No blanket L** at this size (needs 3+ US branches plus $25M sales or 1,000 US employees). Individual petition only.
- ✅ Because you keep working for India Ltd, the banked year stays fresh — a standing option, not a one-shot.

## §4 — The org chart, built backwards from the petition

### ⚠️ "CTO" is a weak L-1A title

At a small company, USCIS reads *CTO* as the person who builds the thing — which points at **L-1B specialized knowledge** (5-year max, **no EB-1C**), not L-1A. Fix it at the source:

- **Title: Managing Director & CTO.** Petition in **executive capacity** under INA 101(a)(44)(B) — directs the management of the organization, sets policy, receives only general supervision from the board — with **function manager** under (44)(A) as the fallback.
- **Board resolution + appointment letter + executive JD**, dated 1 Nov 2027, with percentage-of-time allocations weighted away from delivery.

### The test, and the two provisions that dominate at this size

**INA 101(a)(44)(A)**: supervise and control the work of other **supervisory, professional, or managerial** employees, or manage an essential **function**, with authority over personnel actions.

- ⚠️ **The express exclusion:** a **first-line supervisor of non-professional employees** does not qualify. **Instructors are the trap in this business.** A robotics instructor teaching an 8-kid cohort is exactly the role an officer will call non-professional. **Instructors must not be your direct reports**, and the ones you hire should hold **B.Tech / B.Ed / M.Ed** with the degree requirement written into the JD.
- ✅ **The shield — INA 101(a)(44)(C):** staffing levels cannot be the **sole** factor, and the **reasonable needs of the organization, including its stage of development**, must be considered. Build the record to invoke this deliberately.

### Target chart at filing (Dec 2028) — two tiers, so you supervise supervisors

| Your direct reports | Comp | Their reports |
|---|---|---|
| **Head of Platform Engineering** (B.Tech) | ₹22–28L | 2 engineers |
| **Head of Curriculum** (M.Ed / B.Tech + pedagogy) | ₹16–20L | 1 curriculum designer |
| **Head of Instruction & Programs** (B.Ed/B.Tech) | ₹14–18L | **4–8 instructors** (⚠️ they report *here*, never to you) |
| **US Commercial Lead** (C-corp, US payroll) | $45–80k | school/district pipeline |

India payroll at filing: roughly **₹52–66L of leads + ₹18–26L of instructors + your ₹15–20L ≈ ₹85–110L (~$100–130k) annualized.**

⚠️ **The revenue that has to exist to fund it:** at ~60% gross margin (Indian instructors serving both markets — §5), that needs roughly **₹1.4–1.8Cr (~$170–215k) annualized**. Sketch: **~700 India students × ₹9,000 + ~250 US students × $400.** That is a real business, not a shell — which is the point, and also the risk.

⚠️ **Contractors do not count.** USCIS discounts them heavily. **Put Indian instructors on payroll with EPF** (~12% + 12%) and treat the cost as evidence spend, not overhead. This is the single most expensive structural choice in the track and it is not optional.

⚠️ **The US side must be managerial too.** The petition has to show the *US* position is managerial or executive. The US Commercial Lead is not decoration, and the business plan must show US hiring in year one after arrival.

### Documentation regime — starts at incorporation, not at filing

Same discipline as `01e §2`, and equally impossible to reconstruct later:

- [ ] Written appointment letter for **every** hire, with time allocations and the **degree requirement stated**
- [ ] Your appointment letter + **C-corp board resolution** naming you MD & CTO with an executive JD
- [ ] **Quarterly C-corp board minutes** recording that you report to the board — an hour a quarter, among the strongest executive-capacity evidence available
- [ ] India payroll trail: salary slips, **EPF challans**, Form 24Q, **Form 16 for every employee**, your ITR
- [ ] **Versioned quarterly org charts** with names, titles, salaries, degrees
- [ ] Your personnel authority in writing: offers you signed, appraisals you wrote, promotions, comp decisions
- [ ] Executive discretion: school contracts, vendor and platform agreements, the bank mandate, spending-approval thresholds
- [ ] ⚠️ **Proof you direct rather than build** — sprint boards assigning work to engineers, **commits by them and progressively not by you**, curriculum authored by the curriculum team
- [ ] Your calendar, weighted to board, strategy, partnerships and hiring

⚠️ **The uncomfortable inversion.** In `01e §8` you are firewalled *out* of the tech. Here you are the founder-technologist — and you still have to stop being the person who builds it, because *executive capacity* requires it. **From 1 Nov 2027 your job is to hire the engineer who replaces you.** A bootstrapped venture wants a founder who does everything; L-1A requires one who does none of it.

## §5 — ⚠️ Where this track collides with `01d` (EB-1A)

**This is the part the ask did not account for, and it is the most expensive risk in the file.**

`01d`'s self-filing case rests on an **8 CFR 204.5(h)(5) statement that you are coming to the US to continue work in your area of extraordinary ability** — documented as **streaming / distributed systems engineering leadership at Disney**. `00-MASTER-PLAN` calls this "your weakest prong."

Now consider the 2029 consular officer's file: EB-1A extraordinary ability in streaming distributed systems, filed Sept 2027 — and the petitioner resigned that field in Oct 2027 to run a children's robotics-education company.

⚠️ **That is a field-change argument against your EB-1A, arriving at the worst possible stage.** Mitigations, all of which need to be decided *before* you resign, not after:

1. **Frame the role as software engineering leadership, not teaching.** You are MD & CTO of a **software platform company** — simulation engine, real-time telemetry, multi-tenant delivery at scale. The customers are children; the work is distributed systems. Job title, JD, board minutes, and your own bio must say this consistently from day one.
2. **Keep the `01d` evidence engine running through the transition** — IEEE/ACM membership, reviewing, articles, talks. Do not let the publication record stop in Oct 2027.
3. ⚠️ **Do not resign while the I-140 is under RFE.** `01e §5` flags the same hazard. Get counsel's read on the actual posture before the G-R4 decision.
4. ⚠️ Confirm with counsel whether resigning the employment that supplies **criterion #8 (leading/critical role)** weakens the filed petition retroactively. Our read: the criterion is judged as of filing, so a Sept 2027 filing captures it — **but the prospective-work prong is judged later, and that is the exposure.**

✅ **The one clean mitigation: file the EB-1A in September 2027, before you resign.** `01d` already requires that for CSPA. This track makes it doubly binding.

## §6 — ⚠️ Read this before you build your own L-1A

**`01e` may already have solved the problem this track is trying to solve.**

If your wife's L-1A is approved, you take **L-2 with work authorization incident to status** — get the I-94 annotated **L-2S**. On L-2 you can be employed in the US by **anyone**, including the robotics C-corp, as its CTO, **with no petition of your own, no qualifying year abroad, and no Disney resignation.**

So the honest cost/benefit of *your* L-1A:

| Reason to build your own L-1A | Weight |
|---|---|
| ✅ **Redundancy** if `01e`'s L-1A is denied, 221(g)'d, or never triggered | **High** — `00-MASTER-PLAN` carries "no safety net" as a live risk row, and two independent L-1A options is genuine diversification |
| ✅ **A second EB-1C queue position**, from your own entity, on your ported May-2021 PD | **Medium** — cheap, and EB-1 India retrogression means queue position is the asset |
| ✅ **You own and run the venture regardless** | **Your call** — this is a life decision, not an immigration one |
| ❌ Getting *you* work-authorized in the US | **Low** — `01e` already does this |

⚠️ **Which means S1 (§3) — resigning Disney India in Oct 2027 — is not obviously correct.** It costs ~9 months of income and creates the §5 exposure, to buy redundancy you may not need. **Decide it at G-R4 (31 Oct 2027), with `01e`'s actual status in hand**, not now.

### ✅ The AOS upside applies here too

If you are in the US on L-1A/L-2 when the EB-1A is approved and your ported **May-2021 PD** is current, file **I-485 adjustment of status** instead of consular processing at Mumbai. L-1 is **dual-intent**. AOS gives the family **EADs and advance parole**, removes the consular interview, and lets you wait in the US (`01e §5`).

### ❌ And what this does not do

- ❌ **No effect on the elder son's CSPA.** That rests entirely on the EB-1A being filed by ~Sept 2027.
- ✅ It does give him **L-2 status to 21 (~2034)**, then F-1 — but `01e` already does that, ~12–18 months sooner.
- ⚠️ **Runway, not a cure.** He still needs a green card before ~2034.

### A cheaper variant worth pricing

**EB-1C with no L-1A at all.** Once the C-corp has been doing business a year and you have completed your managerial year abroad, it can petition you for **EB-1C ($715 + ~$2,805 premium)** and you consular-process the immigrant visa from India — no L-1A, no $15–27k petition, no Mumbai nonimmigrant interview. ⚠️ EB-1 India retrogression makes it slow, so it is a queue play, not a timeline play. **But it is the cheapest thing in this file, and it is worth filing regardless of what you decide about the L-1A.**

## §7 — ⚠️ Two collisions with `01e` you are creating

### 7.1 — Your wife as an operating co-founder breaks her own clock

You have chosen **operating co-founder**. Here is what that runs into, stated plainly.

`00-MASTER-PLAN` records the decision as: *"she founds and runs a company full-time as her single occupation — **required**, because L-1A needs one continuous year of full-time managerial employment abroad and **any outside job breaks it**."* `01e §3` sets her clock at **2 Nov 2026 → 2 Nov 2027**, with readiness on **31 Dec 2027** and filing **Q1 2028**.

An operating role in a second company during that window causes three distinct problems:

1. ⚠️ **Her appointment letter and India payroll say full-time MD of the health Pvt Ltd.** If her actual time is split, the petition asserts something untrue. That is not an RFE — under **INA 212(a)(6)(C)(i)** that is material misrepresentation territory, and `01e §8` already establishes that a misrepresentation here contaminates your EB-1A, which the elder son's status rides on. **Same bar, pointed at her.**
2. ⚠️ **The RFE question has no answer.** *"How is she full-time at Company A while serving as an officer of Company B?"*
3. ⚠️ **It corrodes her executive-capacity record.** `01e §2` calls her calendar "genuinely persuasive." A calendar showing two companies is persuasive of something else.

#### ✅ The resolution: defer, don't drop

| Period | Her role in the robotics venture |
|---|---|
| **Now → 31 Mar 2028** | ⚠️ **Unpaid, non-operating director of the Foundation only.** Board service is a few hours a quarter, is **not employment**, touches no India payroll, and contradicts nothing. She may be a **shareholder** of the C-corp — ownership is not employment. |
| **From 1 Apr 2028** | ✅ **Operating co-founder, unrestricted.** |

Why **1 April 2028** specifically: by then her year is banked (Nov 2027), the readiness gate has passed (31 Dec 2027), and either her I-129 is filed (a trigger fired) or `01e §7` G5 has passed and the family knows where it stands. ### 7.2 — Moving the MA co-founder off `01e` is expensive

You have chosen **same person, robotics only** — which means `01e`'s Delaware corp loses its CTO of record. `01e §2` is explicit about what he was carrying:

> *"The co-founder as CTO of record removes the largest line in the budget (a ₹32–38L Bengaluru CTO) **and** gives the Delaware entity genuine US substance from October 2026 — which is what makes §3's regular-petition argument work."*

So removing him does two kinds of damage:

| What breaks | Consequence |

#### Options, ranked

- ✅ **O1 — a part-time US W-2 employee for `01e`, not a co-founder.** What `01e §3` actually needs is *US substance*: a real US person on payroll, filed 941s, a W-2. - ❌ **O4 — accept the new-office downgrade for `01e`.** Worst outcome. Do not drift into this by default.

## §8 — ⚠️ Disney: outside activity and invention assignment

**In `01e` a clearance refusal was survivable** — you were not involved, so refusal only removed IP protection. **Here it is not.** You are the founder, the technologist, and the future beneficiary.

### Two separate exposures

1. **Outside-activity / conflict-of-interest policy.** Serving as an officer or director of an outside company, and holding a financial interest in one, almost certainly requires written disclosure and approval. ⚠️ Unwritten tolerance is worthless at the moment it matters.
2. ⚠️ **Invention assignment — the sharper one.** Your employment agreement almost certainly assigns inventions conceived during employment, and typically reaches anything **related to the employer's business** or developed with employer resources. **Disney is a children's entertainment and education company.** "Robotics education for kids" is *closer* to Disney's field of business than the health venture in `01e` ever was — which makes Disney's colourable claim on the platform materially stronger. Connecticut's §31-51r narrows such clauses **[verify scope]**, but "related to the employer's business" probably still reaches this, and once you are on Disney India payroll the governing law changes again.

**If Disney holds a claim on the platform, it surfaces in diligence, in an acquisition, or in a dispute — over the company's only real asset.**

### What to do, in order

1. **This week:** written request to Disney Ethics/Legal disclosing the venture and requesting clearance **plus an express carve-out** that the Foundation's and the Company's IP is not assigned. Ask for it in writing; a verbal "that's fine" is not a carve-out.
2. ⚠️ **Until you have it, write no code and author no curriculum.** The MA co-founder builds and owns his contributions, assigned to the C-corp under the founders' IP assignment. Your contribution is capital, board service, and introductions.
3. **Re-confirm after the Disney India transfer** — the US clearance may not travel to the India entity, and the India employment agreement is a fresh document. Get it cleared again in Oct/Nov 2026.
4. **Document a clean break at resignation:** inventory of what existed before you joined India Ltd, dated and signed, so the pre- and post-Disney IP boundary is provable.

⚠️ **If clearance is refused:** the venture still exists (co-founder builds, you hold no operating role and possibly no equity — design **D1**), but *your* L-1A track is deferred until after you leave Disney. It does not die; it just cannot start early.

## §10 — The honest test on the nonprofit

The 501(c)(3) costs real money and real attention and carries **none** of the immigration load. Before committing, answer these in writing:

1. **Will you actually raise grant or donor money in year one?** If the answer is "eventually," the Foundation is a ~$8–20k/yr compliance obligation buying optionality you are not using. **Form it in 2028 instead.**
2. **Do you need 501(c)(3) status to get into schools?** Sometimes decisively yes (Title I partnerships, district procurement, FIRST affiliation) — in which case it pays for itself. Test it: **email five target schools now and ask.**
3. **Can you seat an independent-majority board?** If the board is you, your wife, and your friend, you get the compliance cost, the Schedule L disclosure, and **none** of the governance benefit.
4. ⚠️ **Is either founder going to be tempted to move money between the Foundation and the Company?** If yes, do not build the hybrid. An excess-benefit finding against you personally, while an EB-1A and two L-1As are pending, is a catastrophe with a tax bill attached.

✅ **If 2 is a clear yes → build the hybrid as specified.** ⚠️ **If 1–4 are soft → build the C-corp now, the Foundation in 2028.** Nothing in the L-1A pathway needs the nonprofit to exist.

## §11 — Program design and child-safety compliance (the load-bearing parts)

Not the full business spec — only what the pathway depends on. A complete `06b` can follow.

### ⚠️ The hardware problem decides the business

Teaching robotics online means one of three things, and only one of them scales:

| Model | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Ship kits** | ❌ Cross-border customs, duty, breakage, inventory, working capital, returns. You become a logistics company with a teaching division. |
| **Simulation only** | Zero logistics, weaker value proposition ("that's just coding"). |
| ✅ **Simulation-first + BYO or partner-fulfilled kit** | Browser-based robotics simulator as the core product; optional kit from a **local partner** (Avishkaar or STEMpedia in India; Adafruit/Amazon in the US), or **BYO** micro:bit / Arduino / LEGO SPIKE. **Recommended.** |

This also happens to be what makes the C-corp a **software platform company** — which is exactly the framing §5 needs for your EB-1A.

### The margin that funds the org chart

Indian instructors (₹400–700/hr on payroll) teaching **both** markets, against US pricing of **$30–45/session** and India pricing of **₹600–1,000/session**, in cohorts of 6–8. That is a **55–70% gross margin** and it is the arbitrage that makes India Ltd a genuine operating subsidiary rather than a visa shell. ⚠️ US-timezone evening cohorts taught from Bengaluru means a night shift — plan the shift differential and the attrition.

⚠️ **The cautionary tale: WhiteHat Jr.** Aggressive online-coaching-for-kids marketing in India drew regulator and ASCI attention and was ultimately written off by BYJU'S. Do not build on high-pressure sales to parents. It is also a reputational fact an adjudicator can google.

### ⚠️ Teaching minors — the compliance floor

These are not optional, and several are commonly missed:

- **COPPA (16 CFR Part 312)** — under-13 users: verifiable parental consent, data minimization, retention limits, no behavioural advertising. ⚠️ The FTC's **amended COPPA Rule** tightened consent, retention and written-security-program requirements, with compliance phasing through **2025–2026** **[verify current effective dates]**. Design for the amended rule, not the old one.
- **India: DPDP Act 2023** — processing a child's data requires **verifiable parental consent** and **bans tracking and behavioural advertising directed at children** (§9 **[verify]**); Rules notified with phased compliance. This is a harder standard than COPPA in some respects.
- **FERPA + state student-privacy laws** the moment you contract with schools — the school-official exception, plus **NY Ed Law 2-d**, **California SOPIPA**, **Illinois SOPPA** (onerous). Each district will hand you a DPA.
- ⚠️ **Background checks on every instructor, both countries** — US national criminal + sex-offender registry; India police verification. Non-negotiable, and an insurance precondition.
- ⚠️ **Child-protection policy:** two-adult rule, **no unrecorded 1:1 video with a minor**, session recording and retention policy, mandated-reporter training, a written reporting escalation path.
- ⚠️ **Insurance — including the one everyone forgets:** general liability, professional liability/E&O, cyber, **D&O for the Foundation board**, and **abuse & molestation coverage**, which standard GL policies frequently **exclude**. Budget ~$3.5–8k/yr.
- **MA 201 CMR 17.00 WISP** — already required by the co-founder's location; write one WISP covering both entities.

⚠️ **One safeguarding incident is existential for a children's brand and would land while an EB-1A and two L-1A petitions are pending.** This section is cheaper than it looks and more important than the org chart.
