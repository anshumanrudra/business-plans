That raises the accreditation bar. In US K-12 there is **no federal accreditation system**; each receiving district decides credit acceptance, and the near-universal rule (verified against state board rules + CT statute) is: **credit from a *regionally* accredited school MUST be accepted; credit from a non-regionally-accredited source can be refused, exam-validated on ONE attempt, or forced to repeat.** So the single deciding factor is: **is the online school regionally accredited (Cognia / WASC / MSA / NEASC) — NOT DEAC-only.** See §2a for the full decision rule and the grade-10/11 re-entry brief.

## 1. First, Get the Model Right (why online at all)

Two distinct ways to use online school — decide which per child:

| Model | Who | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Full-time diploma track** | **Elder son (13)** | Enroll as a full-time student earning a **US high-school diploma**. This is the transcript that maps 1:1 to US Carnegie credits + GPA on return — **no credential evaluation needed.** |
| **Bridge / supplement** | Both, situationally | A few courses to (a) fill the Aug→India-intake gap so there's no idle term, (b) cover **US-specific subjects** (US History, Civics, American Lit) an Indian school won't teach, or (c) keep the Common Core math/reading sequence aligned (Khan/IXL/Time4Learning). |

**Recommended combination (the clear path):**
- **Elder son:** attend a **Cambridge IGCSE school in India in person** (social life, structure, cheaper) **+ run a part-time/full accredited US online enrollment in parallel** as the diploma insurance. If return timing ever slips, he graduates on the US diploma with zero gap.
- **Younger son (7):** in-person Indian school only; add free/cheap online supplements *if* you see math/reading drift. He carries no transferable credit at grade 2 — don't over-engineer it.

**The accreditation rule for a clean US return:** enroll only where the school is **regionally accredited (Cognia, WASC, MSA, or NEASC)** — verified in the *accreditor's own directory*, not the school's marketing page. ## 2a. The grade-10/11 re-entry decision rule (why regional accreditation is non-negotiable)

Because your son transfers back mid-high-school (grade 9–10 credits count toward US graduation + GPA), enroll only in an online school that meets **ALL** of these — and if it fails #1, stop:

1. **Regionally accredited** — Cognia / ACS WASC / MSA-CESS / NEASC (verified in the accreditor's own directory), **not DEAC-only**.
2. Issues an **official diploma-track transcript** with course names, letter grades, Carnegie-unit credits, and a grading-scale key.
3. **Teacher-led with real student-teacher interaction + defined course windows** (not open-entry self-paced test-out — that model fails NCAA vetting and reads as a diploma mill to registrars).
4. Has an **active NCAA course list** (look it up at `web3.ncaa.org/hsportal`) — a free credibility X-ray even if he never plays sports.
5. Offers **US-standard course titles** — Algebra I/II, Geometry, Biology, Chemistry, and especially **US History, US Government/Civics, Health, PE** (the US graduation requirements Indian/IGCSE curricula skip; take these online *while abroad* so he returns with no gap).
6. Its catalog **maps onto Connecticut's 25-credit requirement** — **confirmed with your target CT district in writing before re-entry** (CT delegates credit-granting to the local board via C.G.S. § 10-221a; the district's written credit map matters more than any brochure).

> **CT caution (verified):** Connecticut's SDE states it "does not accredit online or homeschooling institutions and therefore neither recognizes credits nor credentials earned through these educational processes." That language targets *unaccredited/self-styled* providers in the **homeschool** context — C.G.S. § 10-221a still permits credit from a properly **regionally accredited, teacher-led** online school. But CT is a strict-acceptance state, so **pre-confirming the credit map with the destination district is essential**, not optional.

> **IGCSE ≠ US high-school credit.** Cambridge's own US recognition guidance is about *university* admission, not HS placement. A US high school evaluates IGCSE course-by-course like any foreign transcript (Maths/Sciences/Languages map well; US History/Civics/Health/PE won't). Treat the in-person IGCSE school as enrichment + likely elective credit, and keep the **accredited US online school as the credit-of-record spine.**

## 3. The Clear Enrollment Path (do these in order)

### Step 1 — Decide the track (this week)
- Elder son → **full-time UNHS diploma** (recommended) *or* JMHS if cost/flexibility wins. Younger son → in-person only + optional supplements.
- If unsure between UNHS and JMHS: UNHS if you want the **strongest transcript / NCAA eligibility / university-run credibility**; JMHS if you want the **cheapest, fully self-paced** path.

### Step 2 — Verify eligibility + real cost (before you pay anything)
- Call/email each school: **"We will be residing in India for 2–3 years — can my child enroll and earn the diploma from India, and is there an international fee or a time-zone/proctoring requirement?"**
- Get the **exact annual cost** from their tuition calculator (the band figures above are estimates).
- Confirm **credit-transfer-in**: how many of his completed US grade-7/8 credits they'll accept (reduces cost + time).

### Step 3 — Pull & send records (overlaps with the main education checklist)
- Request the **Transfer Certificate + official transcript** from his current CT school.
- Send transcripts so the online school can **place him at the right grade + transfer prior credits.**
- Keep digitized copies of everything (transcripts, course descriptions, immunization records).

### Step 4 — Enroll & set up the home learning environment
- Enroll and pay (full-pay is cheapest; monthly is more flexible).
- **Logistics that actually matter from India:**
  - **Reliable fiber** (ACT/JioFiber in Bengaluru; verify at the flat *before* signing the lease — already on the housing checklist).
  - A **quiet study space + laptop** (ship a 100–240V laptop; see Logistics §1).
  - **Time-zone plan:** most US online HS (UNHS, JMHS) are **asynchronous/self-paced** — no live-class clash with IST. If a school has **live classes or proctored exams**, confirm they can be done in IST evenings (US daytime) or via an approved local proctor. **Prefer asynchronous** to avoid odd hours.

### Step 5 — Run it in parallel with the in-person IGCSE school
- Elder son does IGCSE in person for community + the externally-validated grade-10 board certificate, **and** carries the US online enrollment.
- **Coordinate load:** you don't need to double every subject. Common efficient split — take **US-specific requirements (US History, Civics, American Lit, sometimes English/electives) online**, and let the IGCSE school cover math/science. Confirm with the online school's counselor which combination still yields their diploma.

### Step 6 — Course-select for a clean US graduation
- Ask the online school's counselor to map a **4-year plan to the US diploma**, front-loading the **US-specific graduation requirements** Indian curricula skip.
- Keep him on track to **sit IGCSE grade-10 exams in India** (externally validated) *and* bank US credits — belt and suspenders.

### Step 7 — Maintain the record every term
- Save **each term's report card + transcript**; request **course descriptions/syllabi** (the item US registrars most want).
- Keep **standardized tests** current: **PSAT/SAT** (test centers exist in India), plus MAP/NWEA if the school uses it for placement.

### Step 8 — Time the return & land the credits
- Target a **US fall (Aug/Sep) return** so he re-enters on the US calendar with no gap.
- If graduating via the US online diploma: it's **already a US transcript — no WES needed.** If transferring mid-high-school into a US brick-and-mortar school, carry the online transcript + course descriptions; the receiving school places him directly.
