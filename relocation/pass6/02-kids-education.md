## 1. Curriculum Choice for Seamless US Re-Entry

The single biggest lever for avoiding credit loss. Ranked by ease of transferring **back to a US school mid-stream**:

| Curriculum | Age/Grade | US re-entry ease | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **US-accredited / American diploma** (in-India school or accredited online) | All | ★★★★★ Best | Regionally accredited (Cognia/WASC/Middle States) transcript maps directly to Carnegie units + US GPA. No re-evaluation, already on Aug-Jun calendar. |
| **Cambridge IGCSE** (→ AS/A-Level) | ~14-16, Gr 9-10 | ★★★★ Strong | "Accepted by leading universities and employers worldwide." Evaluated course-by-course by US registrars; well understood. IGCSE is course-level, not a diploma — the big US-college payoff attaches to **AS/A-Level (AICE)**, which earns college credit. |
| **IB (PYP/MYP/DP)** | PYP 3-12; MYP 11-16; DP 11-12 | ★★★ Strong, needs conversion | DP transfers cleanly (HL scores 5-7 earn US college credit). **MYP is a framework, NOT a credit transcript** (ibo.org) — the school issues a 1-7 report; US schools place via course-by-course review + sometimes a placement test. |
| **CBSE** (India national board) | All | ★★ Workable, most friction | Rigorous, English-medium, routinely accepted — but **no GPA/credit hours**; needs manual subject-to-credit mapping + marks-to-GPA conversion (CBSE CGPA×9.5 ≈ %). Board exams (AISSE gr10, AISSCE gr12) are terminal Feb-Mar. |

> **Why IGCSE over IB for your situation** (this reverses several earlier drafts that recommended IB): IB is genuinely US-recognized, but that recognition only *pays off if the DP is completed in grades 11-12*. Your elder son will be in **grades 8-10** in India — squarely in the **MYP** years, which do **not** transfer as credit. Cambridge **IGCSE** is the cleanest mid-grade record for a returner and is present in both Bengaluru and Kolkata.

### Recommendations

- **Elder son (13, the critical case):** He will do **grades 8 → 9 → 10 in India, then enter US grades 9-12** (returning for grades 11-12 stateside). The danger is arriving in US high school (where Carnegie-unit credits start counting in grade 9) with a foreign record that must be back-converted.
  - **First choice: Cambridge IGCSE** (Gr 9-10) — the cleanest non-US foreign record for US high school. **Strategy: have him sit the IGCSE exams (or CBSE grade-10 AISSE board) before returning** — an externally-validated certificate converts far more cleanly than a half-finished year. Then do US grades 11-12 stateside for a clean, college-ready GPA.
  - **Strongest hedge: an accredited US online high school** (UNHS, §5) so he stays on the US system the entire time — eliminates both credit conversion and the calendar mismatch.
  - **Avoid pure CBSE for the elder son** unless cost forces it: most back-conversion friction, weakest mid-grade records.
- **Younger son (7, grade 2):** Curriculum choice is **low-stakes** — primary grades don't carry transferable credit. Any of IB PYP, Cambridge Primary, or a good CBSE school transfers fine. Optimize his choice for **fit, location, and cost**, and let the elder son drive the curriculum decision so **both kids attend the same school**.

> **Plan-level note:** Because the family is moving **Bengaluru → Kolkata** mid-stay (see City Decision), prioritize a **board present in BOTH cities** — **Cambridge IGCSE** or the **DPS/CBSE** network both qualify — so the Phase-1→Phase-2 move costs no academic continuity.

## 4. Mid-Year Admission Reality (Apr-Mar vs Aug-Jun)

The ~2-3 month relocation timing is the core gap risk. India ends in **March**; US ends ~June, restarts ~Aug/Sep.

- **Time the move to the US fall (Aug/Sep) for return, and to the Indian April start (or a clean mid-year join) for arrival.** A student finishing an Indian grade in March is naturally placed into the next US grade for the fall — turning the April-August window into a normal summer, not a disruption.
- **Bridging options for the arrival into India:**
  - Apply for a **mid-year/waitlist seat** now (2026-27 cycle is mostly closed); top international schools sometimes admit off-cycle.
  - If a seat opens only in April 2026 (next Indian year), bridge with **continued US online schooling** (§5) so there is no idle period.
  - For the younger son (grade 2), mid-year entry is routine.
- **For the elder son**, prioritize entering at a natural grade boundary and, ideally, **complete grade-10 board/IGCSE exams in India before the US return**.

> **Note on the finance-vs-school-calendar tension:** The Finance plan says land **after Sept 30** to lock the tax (RNOR) window; the school calendar wants an April or clean mid-year start. **Reconcile by landing after Sept 30 and bridging the kids on accredited US online schooling (UNHS) until the next clean India intake** — the tax saving (potentially a full extra shielded year) outweighs a few months of online bridging, and the online enrollment doubles as the elder son's US-transcript hedge.

> **See `02a-online-school-path.md`** for the full step-by-step enrollment path from India: the accredited India-eligible shortlist (**UNHS** top pick, **James Madison HS** cheapest; **avoid Stride/K12 + Keystone — India-ineligible**), full-time-diploma vs bridge/supplement models, the three things to confirm by phone before paying (India eligibility + fee, exact cost, asynchronous vs live/proctored), time-zone + fiber logistics, and how to run it in parallel with the in-person IGCSE school (put US History/Civics/American-Lit online).

**Documentation to maintain (critical for course-by-course evaluation on return):**
- **Transfer Certificate (TC)** from the current US school — required to enroll in an Indian school (get it before you leave).
- Official sealed/translated **transcripts and term report cards** for every year; **apostille** the US records via your state (CT) Secretary of State if a school requires it.
- **Get written re-entry/credit-acceptance guidance from your current CT school district *before departure*** — don't rely only on "confirm with the receiving school later."
- **Board certificates** (IGCSE results, CBSE AISSE grade-10, etc.).
- **Course descriptions and syllabi** — the single most useful item for a US registrar matching foreign courses to US credits.
- **School accreditation evidence** (WASC/Cognia/MSA/IB letters).
- **Standardized test records:** IB/IGCSE/AP + PSAT/SAT/ACT, and MAP/NWEA for placement.
- For grade-12 → college: a **WES (World Education Services) course-by-course evaluation** (~$150-300/student) to convert marks to US GPA.

**US-specific subject-gap remediation (don't skip):** US History, State (Civics) History, and American Literature are US-graduation requirements that Indian curricula won't cover. Have the elder son take **US History/Civics online** during the India years (via UNHS or a supplemental provider) so he doesn't return with a graduation-requirement gap.

**Low-cost continuity tools (keep US math/reading sequence aligned):** Khan Academy (free), IXL (~$20/mo), Time4Learning (~$25-30/mo). Cheap insurance against Common Core drift, especially for the younger son.
