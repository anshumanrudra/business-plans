## 1. Tax Residency: Where You Owe Tax

**US side (Husband on H-1B):**
- H-1B by itself does **not** keep you a US tax resident. It's pure day-count under the **Substantial Presence Test (SPT)**: 31+ days this year AND 183+ weighted days = (current-year) + ⅓(prior year) + ⅙(2nd prior year).
- Once you move and your weighted US days fall below 183, you become a **US nonresident alien** for tax.
- **Year of departure = dual-status return** (Form 1040 for resident portion + 1040-NR for nonresident portion; Pub 519). US-source income still gets a 1040-NR.

**India side (FY runs April-March):** You become **Resident** if either:
- 182+ days in India in the FY, **or**
- 60+ days this FY **and** 365+ days across the prior 4 FYs.

**RNOR — your most valuable tax shield.** You are **Resident-but-Not-Ordinarily-Resident** (India taxes **only India-source / India-received income, NOT foreign income**) if you were:
- Non-resident in **9 of the 10** preceding years, **OR**
- in India ≤ **729 days** over the preceding **7 years**. *(Income Tax Act §6(6))*

Since you've been in the US for years, you'll comfortably qualify as RNOR. **This shields your US salary, 401k, US rental income, and US capital gains from Indian tax for ~2-3 years after you land** — the single biggest savings window in this whole plan. *(None of the earlier savings projections even priced this in — meaning actual savings run higher than their figures.)*

**Action / Timeline:**
- **Time your move date.** Land **after Sept 30** in any year so you stay under 182 India-days that first FY → likely **Non-Resident** year 1, then **RNOR** for ~2 more years.
- Keep a **day-count log** (boarding passes, passport stamps) for both countries.
- Map the exact FY your RNOR window ends; plan all big US income events (Roth conversions, gains, 401k moves) **before** you become Ordinarily Resident.

## 2. Avoiding Double Taxation

Because the **DTAA "saving clause"** lets the US tax its own residents/green-card holders regardless of treaty, the practical tools are credits and exclusions, not treaty exemption.

- **Foreign Tax Credit — Form 1116 (use this, NOT FEIE).** Dollar-for-dollar US credit for India taxes paid. India's top marginal rate (~39-43%) **exceeds** the US top rate (37%), so once you pay India tax, FTC typically **wipes out your US tax and banks excess credits** (carry back 1 / forward 10 years).
- **FEIE — Form 2555** excludes ~**$130,000** of 2025 foreign earned income (~$132,900 for 2026, confirm). **Two reasons to prefer FTC:** (1) FEIE-excluded income **does not count as compensation for Roth/IRA contributions**, and (2) FTC is usually a bigger offset given high India rates. Revoking FEIE locks you out for 5 years.
  - *(Correction vs an earlier draft that recommended FEIE and quoted "$126,500": that was the 2024 figure and FEIE is the wrong choice here — use FTC.)*
- **No US-India Totalization Agreement.** ⚠️ A real, unavoidable cost. If you stay on US payroll you pay **US FICA 7.65%** **and** potentially **Indian EPF (~12%+12%)** — and **FTC cannot offset US Social Security tax**. This is a key reason to get the payroll structure right (§3).

**Mandatory disclosure filings (huge penalties — do not skip):**
- **FBAR / FinCEN 114** — if all your foreign accounts combined ever exceed **$10,000** in a year. Non-willful penalty ~$16k *per form*. Due Apr 15, auto-extends to Oct 15.
- **FATCA / Form 8938** — living-abroad **MFJ thresholds $400k year-end / $600k anytime**. Penalty $10k+, up to $60k. *(Correction vs an earlier draft's "$200k" — that's the single-filer-abroad figure; MFJ abroad is $400k/$600k.)*

**Action:** Engage a cross-border CPA who files 1116 + FBAR + 8938 as a package. Decide FTC vs FEIE **before** the first India return.

## 3. Remote Work — Husband (US employer) & Wife (India job)

**Husband working remotely from India for a US employer:**
- **Sourcing:** Salary for work *physically performed in India* is **India-source income** and taxable in India once you're India-resident — regardless of employer location or pay currency. During your **RNOR/NR window**, foreign-source income is shielded, but income for work done *in India* may still be India-taxable depending on residency that FY — confirm with your CA.
- **Permanent Establishment (PE) risk for Disney:** the US-India treaty's **Service PE** triggers at **>90 days** of services in India in a 12-month window. This is **Disney's risk**; most employers will *not* allow indefinite India remote work on US payroll.
- **Confirm with Disney:** (1) will they permit India remote work at all, and for how long? (2) US W-2 vs **Employer of Record (EOR)** vs Disney India entity? (3) who handles India TDS / PF / advance tax?
- **Best structure:** an **EOR or Disney India entity** becomes your legal India employer, runs Indian withholding, and shields Disney from PE. Avoid **dual payroll**. *(This aligns with the immigration recommendation to move to India payroll or take unpaid leave — and note that working the US H-1B remotely from India is separately barred on immigration grounds; see Immigration §1.)*

**Wife working in India:**
- Her Indian salary is **India-source and fully taxable in India** from day one (RNOR only shields *foreign* income). New regime: ~0 up to ₹3-4L, rising to 30% above ₹15L + cess. EPF (~12%) mandatory with an Indian employer.
- ⚠️ If she remains a US tax resident / you file US MFJ, her India salary is **also** reportable to the IRS — offset with FTC. Coordinate MFJ vs MFS with your CPA.

## 4. Keeping the US House

- **If empty:** you still deduct **mortgage interest** (acquisition-debt cap $750k — well under at $200k) and property tax if you itemize. No income, no depreciation.
- **If you rent it out (recommended):**
  - Report rent on **Schedule E**; deduct mortgage interest, property tax, insurance, repairs, management fees, and **depreciation** (building basis ÷ 27.5 yrs). Depreciation often makes rental income near tax-neutral.
  - ⚠️ **Nonresident-alien landlord trap:** default withholding is **30% of GROSS rent**. Avoid by filing **Form W-8ECI** with your property manager + making the **IRC §871(d) net election** → taxed on *net* at graduated rates via **Form 1040-NR**.
  - **Expected result:** rent likely covers the ~$3.2k PITI with a **net surplus ~$700-1,200/mo** after ~10% management + ~25% higher insurance + maintenance.
- **State tax nexus:** ⚠️ State residency follows **domicile**, not federal rules. Keeping the house is a strong domicile tie — aggressive states (CA, NY) may keep taxing you. If applicable, break domicile (driver's license, voter reg, mailing address) before leaving.

**Recommendation:** **Rent it out** via a property manager (~8-10% of rent).

## 5. 401k / Roth While Abroad — MAXIMIZE BEFORE YOU LEAVE

**Current status:** ~$45k Roth 401(k) with Disney matching.

### A. Pre-Move: Maximize Contributions (2026 = Last Full Year on US Payroll)

**2026 Contribution Limits (verified IRS sources):**
- **Base contribution**: $24,500 (employee deferrals)
- **Catch-up contributions** (if 50+):
  - Ages 50-59 and 64+: Additional $8,000
  - Ages 60-63: Additional $11,250 ("super catch-up")
- **Combined employee + employer limit**: $72,000 (or $80,000/$83,250 with catch-ups)
- **Critical advantage**: No income limits for Roth 401(k) (unlike Roth IRAs, which phase out at $236k-$246k MAGI for 2025)

**⚠️ NEW 2026 RULE:** If your 2025 wages exceeded $150,000, you are **required** to make catch-up contributions on a Roth (after-tax) basis, not pre-tax. This is mandatory under SECURE 2.0.

**Action:** Maximize contributions NOW while you have Disney matching and US earned income. Contribute the full $24,500 (or $32,500/$35,750 if eligible for catch-ups). It's unclear if you can continue contributing after relocating.

### B. Critical Caveat: Employer Matching is NOT Tax-Free

⚠️ **Your $45k balance isn't entirely tax-free.** Disney's employer matching contributions are **ALWAYS placed in a separate pre-tax traditional 401(k) account**, NOT in your Roth account. This is an IRS rule, not a plan choice.

This means:
- **Your Roth contributions**: Tax-free growth + tax-free qualified withdrawals
- **Disney's matching portion**: Taxable as ordinary income upon withdrawal (like a traditional 401(k))

When you see "$45k Roth 401(k)", part of that is actually pre-tax employer match dollars in a parallel account. This significantly affects the "tax-free growth" assumption.

### C. Tax-Free Withdrawals Require BOTH Conditions

To withdraw tax-free from your Roth 401(k):
1. Be at least **59½ years old** (or disabled/deceased), **AND**
2. Have held the account for at least **5 years** from your first Roth contribution

**Both conditions must be met.** The 5-year rule applies even after reaching 59½. Start the 5-year clock NOW if you haven't already.

### D. HUGE Advantage: No Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs)

Unlike traditional 401(k)s (which require withdrawals starting at age 73), your **Roth 401(k) has NO RMDs during your lifetime**. This allows unlimited tax-free growth — a major advantage for wealth accumulation.

### E. India May NOT Honor Roth Tax-Free Status

⚠️ **India has no "Roth" concept.** Once you're an **Ordinarily Resident**, India could tax Roth growth/distributions as ordinary income under DTAA Article 20 (pension article assigns taxing rights to country of residence — India). The US-India tax treaty does NOT explicitly protect Roth 401(k) tax-free status when you're an India resident.

### F. Critical Gap: No IRS Guidance for 401(k) Expats

**IRS Publication 54 (Tax Guide for U.S. Citizens Abroad) contains ZERO guidance on:**
- 401(k) or Roth 401(k) contributions for expats
- Employer matching for international employees
- Withdrawals or rollovers while living abroad
- How India-U.S. tax treaty affects Roth 401(k) distributions

This creates significant uncertainty about cross-border retirement account management.

### G. What You MUST Clarify with Disney HR/Benefits

Before relocating, get **written answers** to these critical questions:

## 7. Banking Setup (NRE / NRO / FCNR / FEMA)

- ⚠️ **FEMA vs Income-Tax residency differ.** Under **FEMA**, leaving for long-term employment makes you a "**person resident outside India**" (NRI) essentially **immediately** — you can open NRI accounts right after you move, before hitting India tax-residency day counts.
- **NRE account** (rupee, funded from foreign earnings): interest **tax-free in India**, **fully repatriable**. Use for money brought from the US. Carries INR FX risk.
- **NRO account** (India-source income — wife's salary, US-house rent if routed to India, parents' transactions): interest **taxable**, TDS ~31%, repatriation **capped at $1M/FY** (Forms 15CA/15CB).
- **FCNR(B)** term deposit: held in **USD/foreign currency**, interest tax-free, no rupee risk, fully repatriable — your USD emergency buffer.
- **Convert existing resident accounts to NRO** once you're an NRI (legally required).
- **Where to open:** major NRI banks — **ICICI, HDFC, Axis** (strong NRI desks + US-facing onboarding). Keep a maintained **US account (Charles Schwab** is expat-friendly: no FX fees, ATM rebates**)** for the mortgage/Tesla autopay and 2FA.
