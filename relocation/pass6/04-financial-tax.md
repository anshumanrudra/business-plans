## 1. Tax Residency: Where You Owe Tax

**US side (Husband on H-1B):**
- H-1B by itself does **not** keep you a US tax resident. It's pure day-count under the **Substantial Presence Test (SPT)**: 31+ days this year AND 183+ weighted days = (current-year) + ⅓(prior year) + ⅙(2nd prior year).
- Once you move and your weighted US days fall below 183, you become a **US nonresident alien** for tax.
- **Year of departure = dual-status return** (Form 1040 for resident portion + 1040-NR for nonresident portion; Pub 519). US-source income still gets a 1040-NR.

**India side (FY runs April-March):** You become **Resident** if either:
- 182+ days in India in the FY, **or**
- 60+ days this FY **and** 365+ days across the prior 4 FYs.

**RNOR — your most valuable tax shield.** You are **Resident-but-Not-Ordinarily-Resident** (India taxes **only India-source / India-received income, NOT foreign income**) if you were:
- Non-resident in **9 of the 10** preceding years, **OR**
- in India ≤ **729 days** over the preceding **7 years**. *(Income Tax Act §6(6))*

Since you've been in the US for years, you'll comfortably qualify as RNOR. **This shields your foreign-source income — 401k growth, US rental income, US capital gains, Robinhood/Schwab — from Indian tax for ~2-3 years after you land.** ⚠️ **UPDATE 2026-07-20: it does NOT shield your Disney India salary** — that is India-source, fully India-taxable from day one (you're now on India payroll, not US payroll). The RNOR window is still valuable but covers a **narrower base** than the original US-salary model assumed. Time big foreign-income events (Roth conversions, US-gain realizations) inside this window.

**Action / Timeline:**
- **Time your move date.** Land **after Sept 30** in any year so you stay under 182 India-days that first FY → likely **Non-Resident** year 1, then **RNOR** for ~2 more years.
- Keep a **day-count log** (boarding passes, passport stamps) for both countries.

## 2. Avoiding Double Taxation

Because the **DTAA "saving clause"** lets the US tax its own residents/green-card holders regardless of treaty, the practical tools are credits and exclusions, not treaty exemption.

- **Foreign Tax Credit — Form 1116 (use this, NOT FEIE).** Dollar-for-dollar US credit for India taxes paid. India's top marginal rate (~39-43%) **exceeds** the US top rate (37%), so once you pay India tax, FTC typically **wipes out your US tax and banks excess credits** (carry back 1 / forward 10 years).
- **FEIE — Form 2555** excludes ~**$130,000** of 2025 foreign earned income (~$132,900 for 2026, confirm). **Two reasons to prefer FTC:** (1) FEIE-excluded income **does not count as compensation for Roth/IRA contributions**, and (2) FTC is usually a bigger offset given high India rates. Revoking FEIE locks you out for 5 years.
  - *(Correction vs an earlier draft that recommended FEIE and quoted "$126,500": that was the 2024 figure and FEIE is the wrong choice here — use FTC.)*
- **No US-India Totalization Agreement.** ⚠️ A real, unavoidable cost. If you stay on US payroll you pay **US FICA 7.65%** **and** potentially **Indian EPF (~12%+12%)** — and **FTC cannot offset US Social Security tax**. This is a key reason to get the payroll structure right (§3).

**Mandatory disclosure filings (huge penalties — do not skip):**
- **FBAR / FinCEN 114** — if all your foreign accounts combined ever exceed **$10,000** in a year. Non-willful penalty ~$16k *per form*. Due Apr 15, auto-extends to Oct 15.
- **FATCA / Form 8938** — living-abroad **MFJ thresholds $400k year-end / $600k anytime**. Penalty $10k+, up to $60k. *(Correction vs an earlier draft's "$200k" — that's the single-filer-abroad figure; MFJ abroad is $400k/$600k.)*

**Action:** Engage a cross-border CPA who files 1116 + FBAR + 8938 as a package. Decide FTC vs FEIE **before** the first India return.

## 7. Banking Setup (NRE / NRO / FCNR / FEMA)

- ⚠️ **FEMA vs Income-Tax residency differ.** Under **FEMA**, leaving for long-term employment makes you a "**person resident outside India**" (NRI) essentially **immediately** — you can open NRI accounts right after you move, before hitting India tax-residency day counts.
- **NRE account** (rupee, funded from foreign earnings): interest **tax-free in India**, **fully repatriable**. Use for money brought from the US. Carries INR FX risk.

- **FCNR(B)** term deposit: held in **USD/foreign currency**, interest tax-free, no rupee risk, fully repatriable — your USD emergency buffer.
- **Convert existing resident accounts to NRO** once you're an NRI (legally required).
- **Where to open:** major NRI banks — **ICICI, HDFC, Axis** (strong NRI desks + US-facing onboarding). Keep a maintained **US account (Charles Schwab** is expat-friendly: no FX fees, ATM rebates**)** for the mortgage/Tesla autopay and 2FA.
