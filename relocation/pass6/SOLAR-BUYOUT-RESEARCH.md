## Executive Summary

**Key finding:** Buying out your SunPower solar lease makes you eligible for the **30% federal solar Investment Tax Credit (through 2032)**, which can significantly offset the buyout cost. A $10,000 buyout becomes a $7,000 net cost after the tax credit — potentially cheaper than continuing 3 years of lease payments (~$4,266 with escalators).

**Critical unknown:** Actual buyout cost. Research found general solar lease info but zero bankruptcy-specific details. **You must contact your servicer directly for a buyout quote.**

**Decision framework:**
- Net buyout ≤ $10k → **Strong buy** (cheaper than lease + you own the asset)
- Net buyout $10-15k → **Evaluate** (depends on rental simplification value to you)
- Net buyout ≥ $15k → **Keep lease** (too expensive vs 3-year horizon)

**Decision deadline:** Mid-August (before you leave US) — buyout paperwork/payment is easier to handle stateside.

## What the Research Found (4 Verified Claims)

### 1. 30% Federal Solar ITC Eligibility (HIGH CONFIDENCE)

**The finding:**
- **Leased system:** Homeowner gets ZERO tax benefits (leasing company owns system, claims ITC)
- **Owned system (after buyout):** Homeowner becomes eligible for **30% Investment Tax Credit through 2032**
- **ITC claimed via IRS Form 5695** on your tax return

**The math:**
| Buyout Cost | 30% ITC | Net Cost |
|---|---|---|
| $10,000 | $3,000 | **$7,000** |
| $15,000 | $4,500 | **$10,500** |
| $20,000 | $6,000 | **$14,000** |

**Why this matters for you:**
- This is the biggest financial argument for buyout — it directly offsets the purchase price
- Leased system: you pay $4,266 over 3 years, get nothing back
- Owned system: you pay buyout cost, get 30% back from IRS

**Sources:** IRS Form 5695, EcoWatch

**Verification:** 3-0 vote (high confidence)

- **Net buyout cost: $7,000**

**Recommendation:** **Strong buy** — the incremental cost is modest for long-term ownership benefits.

## Tax Implications Deep Dive

### If You Buy Out:

**Confirmed tax benefits:**
1. **30% federal solar ITC** (claim via Form 5695 on 2026 tax return)
   - This is a CREDIT (reduces tax owed dollar-for-dollar), not a deduction
   - Available through 2032 under current law
   - Must have enough tax liability to use the credit (or carry forward)

**Unknown tax benefits (ASK YOUR CPA):**
2. **Rental property depreciation?**
   - If you buy out solar system, then convert house to rental property, can you ALSO depreciate the system as rental property equipment over 27.5 years?
   - If buyout cost is $10,000, depreciation is ~$364/year for 27.5 years
   - **This could be a tax arbitrage:** claim 30% ITC (residential credit) in year of purchase, THEN depreciate as rental property equipment over 27.5 years
   - Research found ZERO information on this — it's a residential-credit-on-rental-property question
   - **Must ask cross-border CPA:** "Can I claim the residential 30% ITC when I buy out the lease (while it's my residence), then depreciate the system as rental property business equipment after I convert the house to a rental?"

3. **Cost basis increase:**
   - Buyout cost gets added to your home's cost basis (relevant when you sell)
   - Reduces capital gains tax when you eventually sell the property

### If You Keep Lease:

**Tax treatment:**
- Lease payments you make are **deductible as rental expense on Schedule E** (like utilities or HOA fees you pay)
- You get ZERO credit or depreciation (you don't own the system)
- Servicer (lease owner) claims the ITC

## Rental Property Considerations

### If You Buy Out:

**Pros:**
- **Simplifies rental marketing:** house is "solar-equipped, no extra fees"
- **No disclosure complexity:** no separate lease to explain to tenants
- **Cleaner accounting:** no monthly lease payment to track
- **Tenant appeal:** owned solar may be more attractive than leased (no lease assumption concerns)

**Cons:**
- **You're responsible for maintenance:** if system fails, you pay for repairs (no longer covered by lease)
- **Insurance update needed:** must inform landlord insurance that you own solar equipment (~$50-100/yr additional cost)
- **Upfront cost:** $7k-15k (net of ITC) vs $0 to keep lease

### If You Keep Lease:

**Pros:**
- **No upfront cost:** keep liquidity for the move
- **Maintenance covered:** servicer handles repairs/monitoring per lease terms
- **Flexibility:** can revisit buyout later if you return permanently

**Cons:**
- **Must disclose to tenants:** state law typically requires disclosure of solar leases
- **Rental complexity:** property manager needs to understand the lease terms
- **You pay monthly:** ~$120/mo (~$4,266 over 3 years with escalators)
- **No tax benefit:** you get a deduction (rental expense), but servicer gets the ITC
- **No equity:** paying for 3 years with nothing to show for it

### Who Gets the Solar Electricity Savings?

**Both scenarios (buyout or keep lease):**
- **Tenant gets the solar savings** (they pay the utility bill, they see the reduction)
- **You eliminate or pay the solar cost:**
  - Buyout → you paid upfront, no monthly cost
  - Keep lease → you pay servicer ~$120/mo

**How to structure rent:**
- **Don't separately charge tenant for solar** (just build the cost into rent if you're keeping the lease)
- Market it as: "Solar-equipped home, enjoy lower electric bills"
- If you buy out: "Owned solar, no extra fees"
- If you keep lease: "Solar lease included in rent" (and adjust rent up ~$120-150/mo to cover)
