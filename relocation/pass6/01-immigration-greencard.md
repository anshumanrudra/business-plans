## 2. Priority Date Portability (EB-2 → EB-1)

- **Confirmed by regulation (8 CFR 204.5(e)):** an I-140 approved under INA 203(b)(1)/(2)/(3) confers its priority date on **"any subsequently filed petition"** in those categories, and you get **"the earliest priority date."** EB-1A is 203(b)(1)(A); EB-2 is 203(b)(2). So your **May 2021 EB-2 date attaches to a new EB-1A I-140.**
- **Survives the prior employer's withdrawal:** retention holds as long as the original I-140 was approved and **not revoked for fraud, misrepresentation, or material error / DOL revocation** (8 CFR 204.5(e)(2)). Because it's been approved >180 days, withdrawal can't revoke it. *(The priority date is effectively yours for life unless revoked for fraud — and it also ports to a new employer's petition if you ever change jobs after return.)*
- **Clarify the three "180-day" rules** (commonly confused — earlier passes conflate them):
  - **PD retention** (204.5(e)) — preserves your *queue position* for a *new* I-140. **This is the one you use.**
  - **I-140 withdrawal immunity** (205.1, after 180 days approved) — why your old InfoServices I-140 stays valid.
  - **INA 204(j) / AC21 §106(c) porting** — a *different* rule about changing to a same/similar job when an **I-485 has been pending 180+ days**. Not relevant to you until you have a pending I-485.
- You do **not** keep using the old I-140 itself — you file a *new* EB-1A petition that simply inherits the old date.

## 5. Green Card Filing Logistics From Abroad

**Consular Processing (CP) vs Adjustment of Status (AOS):**
- **AOS (I-485)** requires being **physically in the US in valid status** with a current PD (USCIS). Not available while you live in India.
- **CP** is the path from India: after I-140 approval, USCIS forwards it to the **National Visa Center**; when a visa number is available, NVC collects fees/docs and schedules the **immigrant visa interview at US Consulate Mumbai** (USCIS; travel.state.gov).
- **For a family residing in India, CP is the realistic/appropriate route.** AOS only re-opens if you're back in the US in valid H-1B/H-4 status when the PD is current.

**Impact on kids & CSPA — the critical issue:**
- CSPA covers EB **derivatives**. Formula (USCIS PM Vol 7 Pt A Ch 7): **CSPA age = age at visa availability − I-140 pending time.** Child must **"seek to acquire"** within **1 year** of visa availability, stay unmarried, and have CSPA age <21.
- **MAJOR 2025 CHANGE — worse for you:** effective **Aug 15, 2025**, USCIS calculates "visa availability" using the **Final Action Dates** chart (not the more generous Dates for Filing used since Feb 2023). This **slows** when the clock freezes — magnifying aging-out risk on backlogged EB-2 India.
- **Your 13-year-old:** ~15-16 during the India stay. Under **EB-2 India** (Unavailable / ~mid-2013 final action dates), a visa won't become "available" for many years — he very likely **turns 21 and ages out** before then, losing derivative eligibility. The limited I-140 pending-time subtraction won't save him.
- **Why switching to EB-1 fixes this:** EB-1 India is far more current, so a visa becomes "available" **much sooner**, **freezing his CSPA age at a younger number** and letting him seek to acquire within 1 year while safely under 21. **This is the strongest reason to prioritize EB-1A/EB-1C.**

**Younger son (US citizen):** no immigration process needed — he's already a citizen. Keep his **US passport current** (see §7).
