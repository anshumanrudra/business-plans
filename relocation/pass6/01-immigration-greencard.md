## Strategic Bottom Line (read first)

Your single most powerful move is to **port your May 2021 EB-2 priority date onto an EB-1 petition** — the category (EB-1A vs EB-1C) is a hedge, not a fork. Three things hold regardless of which:

1. **EB-1 India is years more current than EB-2 India.** Per the State Dept Visa Bulletin, as of mid-2026 EB-1 India sits at ~Oct 2022–Feb 2023, while **EB-2 India is "Unavailable" (July 2026 bulletin)** / otherwise ~mid-2013 — a ~12–13 year backlog. Your 2021 date on an **EB-1** petition would be **at or near current**.
2. **It protects your 13-year-old from aging out** (see §5) — via EB-1's currentness, whether EB-1A or EB-1C.
3. **The May 2021 PD ports to *any* EB-1 petition** (8 CFR 204.5(e)) — EB-1A self-petition **or** EB-1C by Disney/InfoServices. The old InfoServices I-140 gives you the *portable date*, not category eligibility.

> **Because EB-1A denial rates are elevated under the current administration, the primary GC track is now EB-1C** (multinational manager) via a genuine people-manager role at **Disney India (first choice) or InfoServices India**, with **L-1A** as the return bridge — see the dedicated **`01a-eb1c-plan.md`**. **EB-1A is kept building in parallel** as a hedge (cheap, and it carries the same 2021 PD), and **O-1A** stays in reserve as an interim return visa. Dual-tracking both EB-1 categories — since both fail on *different* grounds and both port the 2021 PD — is the real risk mitigation. This section documents all routes; §3 (EB-1A) and §6 (EB-1C/L-1A/O-1) below remain accurate, with the *primary/parallel emphasis flipped* toward EB-1C per 01a.

## 1. H-1B Maintenance While Living Abroad 2–3 Years

**The 6-year clock and AC21 extensions**
- H-1B is capped at **6 years** in up to 3-year increments (Cyrus Mehta). You entered Nov 2015; your I-797 runs to Oct 2028, so AC21 extensions are already in play.
- **AC21 §104(c) → 3-year extensions** (8 CFR 214.2(h)(13)(iii)(E)): available because you have an **approved I-140** and **no visa number is available** (EB-2 India backlog). You can keep getting 3-year extensions "so long as an approved I-140 remains in place and no immigrant visa number is available" (Cyrus Mehta).
- **Your prior-employer I-140 still counts.** An approved, un-revoked I-140 from InfoServices LLC supports §104(c) 3-year extensions even for a *new/current* employer's H-1B (Cyrus Mehta). Critical condition: the I-140 must **not be revoked** — and because it has been approved **>180 days**, employer withdrawal no longer triggers revocation (8 CFR 205.1(a)(3)(iii)(C)). It is safe.
- **The "AC21 trap" to watch:** once your priority date becomes current, §104(c) stops, and you have only **1 year** to file I-485 or an immigrant visa (8 CFR 214.2(h)(13)(iii)(D)(10)). You can't adjust on the prior employer's I-140 (job no longer exists) — so the *current* employer must have a fresh PERM/I-140 (porting your 2021 date) ready, OR your EB-1A must be approved. For EB-2 India this won't bite in your 2-3 year window, but it's the reason to get EB-1A moving.

**Recapture of time abroad**
- Only days **physically in the US in H-1B status** burn the 6-year clock. Your 2-3 years in India are **recapturable** — added back later (VisaPro). **No deadline** to claim it.
- Because you were already counted in the cap, your return petition is **cap-exempt** (INA 214(g)(7)) — **no lottery**, file any time.
- **Documentation to preserve now:** I-94 travel history (primary), passport entry/exit stamps, pay stubs/W-2s, boarding passes, employer letters. Recapture is requested on the I-129 with a day-count.

> **Correction vs earlier drafts:** Some earlier passes claimed you either (a) "keep the same H-1B and re-enter before Oct 2028" (conflates the I-797 petition, the visa stamp, and I-94 status — three different things) or (b) must "re-enter on a new H-1B lottery" (ignores cap-exemption). **Neither is right.** Your return is **cap-exempt with recapture** — no lottery, and you will almost certainly need a **new visa stamp** (the stamp, not the petition, is what expires).

**Remote work for Disney from India — DO NOT do it on the H-1B**
- H-1B is employer- AND worksite-specific and is for *US* employment. There is **no way to amend the LCA/petition to cover a foreign worksite** (Immigration-Analytics; GT Law). Working remotely from India sits outside the petition.
- **Risk:** extended work abroad may be treated as **unauthorized employment / abandonment**. At re-entry, CBP can question where you worked, and if it learns you worked remotely-from-abroad, can find you inadmissible (INA 212(a)(7)(A)) and **physically cancel your H-1B visa** (Immigration-Analytics).
- **Tax/PE:** US payroll while resident in India creates Indian tax residency (183+ day rule) and **permanent-establishment exposure for Disney** in India; treaty relief requires careful filing (see Finance §3).
- **Cleaner options:** (a) **Formal unpaid leave of absence** — Disney keeps the petition on file, un-revoked, so it still supports recapture/return; or (b) **transfer to Disney's India entity** on local Indian payroll (resolves tax/PE), keeping the US petition valid (not revoked) for your return. Confirm Disney will not withdraw the I-797 either way. *(Option (b) also builds your EB-1C/L-1A predicate — see §6.)*

**Revalidation / stamping / returning**
- Three distinct things: **I-797 petition** (valid to Oct 2028), **visa stamp** (separate expiry — likely expires before 2028), and **I-94** (admission period per entry). After 2-3 years you will almost certainly need a **new visa stamp** before returning.
- **MAJOR 2025 CHANGE:** As of **Oct 1, 2025**, State **eliminated dropbox/interview-waiver for H-1B and H-4** worldwide — **mandatory in-person interview** for everyone (VisaVerge). Add **social-media vetting** (since Dec 15, 2025) and India consulate waits of **3-6 months**. Budget significant lead time for the whole family's stamping.
- Stamp at Mumbai/Chennai/Hyderabad/New Delhi with: valid I-797, DS-160, employer letter, recent pay stubs/W-2. Re-enter on valid stamp + I-797; CBP issues a fresh I-94.
- **Abandonment risk:** a long gap with no active US work invites 221(g) questions about whether the employment relationship is genuine/ongoing. Carry an offer-to-return letter and payroll history. If a prior visa was ever cancelled for remote-abroad work, counsel advises filing a **fresh petition** rather than reusing the tainted one.

## 2. Priority Date Portability (EB-2 → EB-1)

- **Confirmed by regulation (8 CFR 204.5(e)):** an I-140 approved under INA 203(b)(1)/(2)/(3) confers its priority date on **"any subsequently filed petition"** in those categories, and you get **"the earliest priority date."** EB-1A is 203(b)(1)(A); EB-2 is 203(b)(2). So your **May 2021 EB-2 date attaches to a new EB-1A I-140.**
- **Survives the prior employer's withdrawal:** retention holds as long as the original I-140 was approved and **not revoked for fraud, misrepresentation, or material error / DOL revocation** (8 CFR 204.5(e)(2)). Because it's been approved >180 days, withdrawal can't revoke it. *(The priority date is effectively yours for life unless revoked for fraud — and it also ports to a new employer's petition if you ever change jobs after return.)*
- **Clarify the three "180-day" rules** (commonly confused — earlier passes conflate them):
  - **PD retention** (204.5(e)) — preserves your *queue position* for a *new* I-140. **This is the one you use.**
  - **I-140 withdrawal immunity** (205.1, after 180 days approved) — why your old InfoServices I-140 stays valid.
  - **INA 204(j) / AC21 §106(c) porting** — a *different* rule about changing to a same/similar job when an **I-485 has been pending 180+ days**. Not relevant to you until you have a pending I-485.
- You do **not** keep using the old I-140 itself — you file a *new* EB-1A petition that simply inherits the old date.

## 3. EB-1A Self-Petition While in India (Primary Track)

**The 10 criteria — 8 CFR 204.5(h)(3) (need ≥3):**
1. Lesser nationally/internationally recognized **prizes/awards**
2. **Membership** in associations requiring outstanding achievement (judged by experts)
3. **Published material about you** in professional/major trade/major media
4. **Judging** the work of others (panel or individually)
5. **Original contributions** of major significance (scientific/scholarly/business)
6. **Authorship of scholarly articles** in professional/major media
7. Display of work at artistic exhibitions *(N/A for you)*
8. **Leading/critical role** for distinguished organizations
9. **High salary** relative to others in the field
10. Commercial success in performing arts *(N/A)*

- **Two-step test (Kazarian):** USCIS first counts ≥3 criteria, then a **"final-merits determination"** of whether you show *sustained national/international acclaim* and are among the small percentage at the very top. Meeting 3 boxes is necessary but not automatically sufficient — quality of evidence matters. (An oft-quoted "30–40% EB-1A denial rate" is **uncited** — treat as a general reminder that quality matters, not a real figure.)
- **Self-petition confirmed (8 CFR 204.5(h)(5)):** "Neither an offer for employment… nor a labor certification is required." No employer, no PERM. File I-140 yourself.
- **File from India:** nothing requires US presence to file. After approval + current PD, finish via **consular processing** (DS-260, Mumbai), not AOS.

**Realistic criteria targets for a Lead Software Engineer (aim at 4–6 to clear the final-merits bar):** #4 Judging, #5 Original contributions, #6 Scholarly articles, #8 Critical role, #9 High salary, #2 Membership, #3 Media.

**24-Month Evidence-Building Roadmap**

*Months 0–6 (build foundation):*
- **#9 High salary:** Get a letter documenting your Lead Engineer total comp; benchmark against BLS/levels.fyi for your role/region (often the easiest early win).
- **#4 Judging:** Sign up as a reviewer for **IEEE/ACM** conferences/journals; judge hackathons (**Devpost, Major League Hacking**); serve on awards committees. Target **10–15 completed peer reviews**. Keep invitation emails + confirmation of completed reviews.
- **#2 Membership:** Apply for **IEEE Senior Member** (requires significant experience + references) or **ACM Distinguished** — these can qualify if admission requires outstanding achievement judged by experts.

*Months 6–18 (generate original work + visibility):*
- **#6 Scholarly articles:** Publish **5–7 papers/articles** — IEEE/ACM venues, arXiv, peer-reviewed industry journals, and technical outlets like **InfoQ**; speak at **QCon / re:Invent**. Co-authoring with academics is fine. Track citations (Google Scholar).
- **#5 Original contributions:** Document your impact at Disney Streaming — **file provisional/USPTO patents**, widely adopted architecture/systems, open-source projects with adoption metrics (GitHub stars/forks, downloads), conference talks. Gather **6–8 expert recommendation letters** (independent senior figures) explaining the field-wide significance of your contributions — these are the backbone of #5 and the final-merits case.
- **#3 Media:** Get profiled/quoted in major trade media (**TechCrunch, VentureBeat, Economic Times**, industry publications, podcasts, technical press) about your work.
- **#1 Awards:** Pursue recognized industry credentials/awards — **AWS Community Hero, Google Developer Expert (GDE), Microsoft MVP** — plus conference best-paper and hackathon wins.

*Months 12–24 (codify role + assemble):*
- **#8 Critical role:** Organizational chart, letters from Disney leadership describing your leading/critical role and the company's distinguished reputation.
- **Assemble petition:** 6-8 strong recommendation letters, exhibits per criterion, a petition letter mapping evidence to criteria + the final-merits narrative. **Use Premium Processing (15 business days)** on the I-140.

**Why EB-1A wins for you:** no employer dependency, no PERM, **no per-country backlog problem** for India in the way EB-2 has — and it freezes your kids' CSPA age far sooner.

## 5. Green Card Filing Logistics From Abroad

**Consular Processing (CP) vs Adjustment of Status (AOS):**
- **AOS (I-485)** requires being **physically in the US in valid status** with a current PD (USCIS). Not available while you live in India.
- **CP** is the path from India: after I-140 approval, USCIS forwards it to the **National Visa Center**; when a visa number is available, NVC collects fees/docs and schedules the **immigrant visa interview at US Consulate Mumbai** (USCIS; travel.state.gov).
- **For a family residing in India, CP is the realistic/appropriate route.** AOS only re-opens if you're back in the US in valid H-1B/H-4 status when the PD is current.

**Impact on kids & CSPA — the critical issue:**
- CSPA covers EB **derivatives**. Formula (USCIS PM Vol 7 Pt A Ch 7): **CSPA age = age at visa availability − I-140 pending time.** Child must **"seek to acquire"** within **1 year** of visa availability, stay unmarried, and have CSPA age <21.
- **MAJOR 2025 CHANGE — worse for you:** effective **Aug 15, 2025**, USCIS calculates "visa availability" using the **Final Action Dates** chart (not the more generous Dates for Filing used since Feb 2023). This **slows** when the clock freezes — magnifying aging-out risk on backlogged EB-2 India.
- **Your 13-year-old:** ~15-16 during the India stay. Under **EB-2 India** (Unavailable / ~mid-2013 final action dates), a visa won't become "available" for many years — he very likely **turns 21 and ages out** before then, losing derivative eligibility. The limited I-140 pending-time subtraction won't save him.
- **Why switching to EB-1 fixes this:** EB-1 India is far more current, so a visa becomes "available" **much sooner**, **freezing his CSPA age at a younger number** and letting him seek to acquire within 1 year while safely under 21. **This is the strongest reason to prioritize EB-1A/EB-1C.**

**Younger son (US citizen):** no immigration process needed — he's already a citizen. Keep his **US passport current** (see §7).

## 6. EB-1C Option + L-1A Bridge + O-1 Fallback (Parallel Tracks)

**EB-1C requirements (USCIS EB-1 page):**
- Employed **abroad ≥1 continuous year in the 3 years** before the petition (or before your most recent US admission), in a **managerial or executive capacity**.
- US petitioner must intend to employ you in a **managerial/executive** role.
- **Qualifying corporate relationship**: US petitioner is parent/subsidiary/affiliate/branch of your foreign employer, doing business in the US **≥1 year**.
- **Employer-sponsored only — no self-petition. No PERM required.**

**Managerial threshold — the scrutiny point (be genuinely managerial, not just senior IC):**
- Supervise **3–5+ professional employees**, with **hire/fire authority** and **budget responsibility**, and spend **<50% of time on hands-on operational tasks**.
- **Structure the India move correctly:** an **international assignment/secondment** (remain a Disney employee on assignment) OR a clean transfer to **Disney's India entity (Disney+ Hotstar / Bengaluru tech)** — **not** resign-and-rejoin (breaks the same-employer/qualifying-relationship requirement).
- **L-1A is the natural bridge** (intracompany transferee manager/exec): the same qualifying-relationship + managerial facts support both L-1A entry and a later EB-1C I-140 by the US affiliate.

**Pros/cons vs EB-1A:**
- **Pro:** No need to prove extraordinary-ability acclaim — it's about corporate role/structure. Both are EB-1 (current for India), both skip PERM, both can carry your 2021 PD.
- **Con:** Employer- and structure-dependent; "managerial/executive capacity" is heavily scrutinized. "Lead Engineer" alone likely won't qualify without real managerial scope.
- **Recommendation:** If your India role is genuinely managerial at Disney India (or another qualifying MNC), treat EB-1C as a strong backup; otherwise lean on EB-1A. Pursue both if you can.

**O-1 fallback for the return:** If the green card is approved but not yet issued when you want to return — or if timing slips — an **O-1A (extraordinary ability)** non-immigrant visa is a viable interim work visa built on the *same* judging/publications/contributions/salary evidence as EB-1A. Keep this in reserve as a bridge back to US work.

## 7. Kids & Dependents

**Returning to the US:**
- **7-year-old (US citizen):** **No visa.** Enters on a valid **US passport**.
- **13-year-old (Indian citizen):** Derivative **H-4** as the H-1B child. Needs an **H-4 visa stamp** from a US consulate in India (in-person interview — dropbox ended Oct 2025), then enters in H-4. H-4 EAD does not apply to children. **He ages out of H-4 at 21** (must be unmarried, <21) — a separate clock from CSPA, reinforcing the urgency of EB-1.

**OCI:**
- **Elder son** is an Indian citizen — he doesn't need OCI.
- **Younger son (US citizen)** already has OCI. OCI **coexists** with US citizenship (it's a lifelong visa, not Indian citizenship) — it does not endanger his US citizenship. Post-June-2021 rule: minors need only **one OCI re-issue after age 20**. If a fresh OCI is ever needed, budget **~$303 and 2–4 months** via VFS Global/BLS.

**Maintaining the 7-year-old's US citizenship/passport from India:**
- **Minors under 16 cannot "renew" — must apply in person on Form DS-11 each time** (travel.state.gov). His passport expires **Jun 2029**; plan a renewal appointment well before then. **Renew it now if at all convenient before you leave** — it avoids a both-parent consular trip later.
- **Both parents must appear/consent.** If one parent can't attend, that parent provides a notarized **DS-3053** (Statement of Consent).
- Apply at **US Embassy New Delhi or Consulate Mumbai/Chennai/Hyderabad/Kolkata.** Under-16 passports are valid **5 years.**

> **See `01c-child-passport-from-india.md`** for the full step-by-step path. **Bottom line: renew his passport in the US NOW, before you leave** — a late-2026 issuance is valid ~5 years (~2031), covering the entire relocation, and sidesteps all India consular hassle. Only apply from India (Chennai/Hyderabad from BLR; Kolkata from WB; DS-11 + both parents + DS-3053 if one can't attend; ~8–12 wks) if it lapses/is lost abroad. **After any new passport, re-link his OCI to the new passport number.**

- **Remote-work-from-India on the H-1B** → visa cancellation / inadmissibility. Avoid; use leave or India payroll.
