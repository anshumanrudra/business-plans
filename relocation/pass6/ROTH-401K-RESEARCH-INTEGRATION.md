## Key Insights from Research

### 1. Employer Matching is NOT Tax-Free
⚠️ **Your $45k balance includes pre-tax employer match dollars.** IRS rules require Disney's matching contributions to go into a **separate pre-tax traditional 401(k) account**, NOT your Roth account. This is mandatory, not a plan choice.

**Impact**: Part of your "$45k Roth 401(k)" will be taxed as ordinary income upon withdrawal, despite the Roth designation.

### 2. 2026 Contribution Limits (Verified IRS)
- Base: $24,500 (employee deferrals)
- Catch-up (50-59, 64+): +$8,000
- Super catch-up (60-63): +$11,250
- Combined employee+employer: $72,000-$83,250

**NEW 2026 RULE**: If 2025 wages exceeded $150,000, catch-up contributions MUST be Roth (after-tax), not pre-tax. This is mandatory under SECURE 2.0.

### 3. No Income Limits (Unlike Roth IRA)
Roth 401(k) has **NO income limits** for contributions. Roth IRA phases out at $236k-$246k MAGI (2025). This makes the Roth 401(k) especially valuable for high earners.

### 4. No Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs)
Unlike traditional 401(k)s (RMDs start at age 73), **Roth 401(k) has NO RMDs during your lifetime**. This allows unlimited tax-free growth — a major advantage.

### 5. Tax-Free Withdrawals Require BOTH:
1. Age 59½+ (or disabled/deceased), **AND**
2. 5 years from first Roth contribution

**Both conditions must be met.** The 5-year rule applies even after 59½.

### 6. India May NOT Honor Roth Tax-Free Status
⚠️ **Critical uncertainty**: Once you're an Ordinarily Resident in India, India may tax Roth distributions as ordinary income under DTAA Article 20 (assigns pension taxing rights to country of residence). The US-India tax treaty does NOT explicitly protect Roth 401(k) tax-free status.

### 7. IRS Provides ZERO Expat Guidance
**IRS Publication 54 (Tax Guide for U.S. Citizens Abroad) contains NO guidance on:**
- 401(k) contributions for expats
- Employer matching for international employees
- Withdrawals/rollovers while abroad
- India-U.S. tax treaty implications

This creates significant uncertainty.

### 8. FBAR Exemption Confirmed
✓ **Good news**: Roth 401(k) is **EXEMPT from FBAR reporting** when living abroad. IRAs and 401(k)-type plans where you are a participant/beneficiary are explicitly excluded. This simplifies international tax compliance.

### 9. 2026 = Last Full Year on US Payroll
If you move to India payroll or take unpaid leave (as recommended in Immigration §1), you likely **cannot contribute** to the US 401(k) anymore. 401(k) contributions require:
- US earned income (not FEIE-excluded)
- Active US payroll participation

**This makes 2026 your last opportunity to maximize contributions** before relocation.
