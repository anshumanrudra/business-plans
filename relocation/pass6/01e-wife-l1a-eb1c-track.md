## §1 — Corporate architecture

### Delaware C-corporation (parent) — form ~Sept 2026, before departure

**Not an LLC.** Four reasons, the third of which is decisive:

1. A foreign-owned LLC drags in **Form 5472 plus a pro forma 1120, with a $25,000 penalty per missed filing**, and a messy effectively-connected-income analysis.
2. An LLC has **members and managers, not a board of directors** — so the executive-capacity evidence in §2 (quarterly board minutes showing she receives only general supervision from a board) has no natural home.
3. ⚠️ **A two-owner LLC is a partnership by default, and a partner cannot be a W-2 employee of the partnership.** L-1A and EB-1C both turn on her being *employed* in a managerial capacity. A C-corp employs its own shareholder-officers as a matter of course.
4. It is the only structure US investors will fund, if that ever matters.

**Ownership:** she holds **majority equity** and is unambiguously **CEO**. The Massachusetts co-founder (US citizen, technical) holds a minority stake with vesting, serves as **CTO of record**, and reports to her.

⚠️ **Papered by a startup lawyer BEFORE any code exists:** founders' agreement, vesting schedule, and **IP assignment from both founders to the company.** Retrofitting IP assignment after the fact is how startups discover in diligence that they don't own their product.

**Massachusetts consequences** (triggered by the co-founder's location, not optional): **foreign-qualify the Delaware corp in MA**, file MA annual reports, and produce a **Written Information Security Program under 201 CMR 17.00**. Cheap now, awkward later.

### India Private Limited Company (wholly-owned subsidiary) — incorporate ~Nov 2026

**Not an LLP and not a proprietorship.** A proprietorship is not a separate legal person, so there is **no foreign entity for the US company to have a qualifying relationship with** — fatal to L-1A on its own. An LLP has no share capital, which muddies ownership analysis. A Pvt Ltd produces share certificates, a board, statutory registers, and **mandatory audited financials every year regardless of turnover** — which reads as a cost until you realise it is the documentary spine of the petition.

Structure: Delaware corp holds all but one share; a nominee holds one (a Pvt Ltd needs two shareholders minimum). Two directors, **at least one resident in India ≥182 days in the financial year — applied proportionately for newly incorporated companies**, so incorporating after she lands should let her satisfy it herself. ⚠️ Confirm with the CA rather than assume; getting it wrong means adding an outside resident director.

She joins its payroll **day one** as **Managing Director**, with a board resolution and an executive job description.

### Why US-parent rather than India-parent — and why this beats the earlier draft

An earlier version of this design had India Pvt Ltd on top, investing into the US corp via **ODI**. Inverting it is better on four counts:

1. ⚠️ **No CFC, no GILTI ambush.** The moment your EB-1A succeeds and you become US persons, an India-parent structure would make her Pvt Ltd a **controlled foreign corporation** — Form 5471 annually, Subpart F, and GILTI catching essentially all income for a services company with minimal qualifying tangible assets. Nothing in `04b` covers this. **US-parent from day one means the flip we would have had to pay for is just the starting state** — no §367 exposure, no Indian capital gains on a share swap.
2. **Simpler India compliance** — a US parent funding an Indian subsidiary is **inbound FDI on the automatic route** for health-tech services with FC-GPR reporting, instead of an AD-bank ODI filing capped at 400% of net worth.
3. **Still a clean qualifying relationship.** Parent/subsidiary works in either direction for L-1A. EB-1C then has the US parent petitioning someone employed by its foreign subsidiary — textbook.
4. It is what US investors expect.

⚠️ **The gating legal question — get a written FEMA opinion before incorporating (G1).** Once she is an Indian resident holding shares in a US company that owns an Indian company, that is **round-tripping** under the FEMA Overseas Investment Rules 2022 — permitted subject to conditions and a two-layer cap, but genuinely the point where this could stall at the AD bank. The favourable fact: **she acquires the US shares while still a non-resident of India** (Sept 2026, while US-resident), which is a materially better posture than acquiring them as a resident. **Fallback if the opinion is unfavourable:** she owns both entities directly as **affiliates**, and you accept the "substantially the same proportions" argument on ownership.

### Transfer pricing and where the IP lives

The India subsidiary builds and delivers; the Delaware parent holds the customer contracts. That requires an intercompany services agreement with **cost-plus billing** (India's Safe Harbour Rules prescribe margins for IT-enabled services), a **transfer-pricing study**, and **Form 3CEB annually**. Budget ₹1.5–3L/yr. ⚠️ Indian TP audits are aggressive on exactly this captive-service-provider pattern — document from the first invoice, do not reconstruct later.

**IP sits in the Delaware parent** (the natural consequence of US-parent structure and what investors want), licensed down or developed under the services agreement. Get the CA and US counsel to agree the mechanics *before* the India subsidiary writes code, because IP migration after the fact is expensive on both sides.

## §2 — The org chart, built backwards from the petition

### The test

**INA 101(a)(44)(A)** requires that she **supervise and control the work of other supervisory, professional, or managerial employees** — or manage an essential *function* — with authority over personnel actions and discretion over daily operations. **(44)(B)** executive capacity requires directing management, setting policy, and receiving **only general supervision from a board or stockholders**.

Two provisions dominate at this company size:

- ⚠️ **The express exclusion:** a "first-line supervisor of **non-professional** employees" does not qualify. Subordinates must hold **degree-requiring professional roles**, and the job descriptions must say so.
- ✅ **The shield — INA 101(a)(44)(C):** staffing levels cannot be the **sole** factor, and the **reasonable needs of the organization, including its stage of development**, must be considered. Build the record to invoke this deliberately; do not hope an officer reaches for it.

### Target chart at petition (Q1 2028)

Two tiers, so she supervises people who themselves supervise:

| Her direct reports | Comp | Their reports |
|---|---|---|
| **CTO** (US citizen, MA — co-founder) | equity | engineers |
| **Head of Clinical Data** (MSc/MPH or MBBS + analytics) — owns the calibration engine | ₹14–18L | 1 clinical analyst |
| **Head of Member Operations** | ₹10–14L | 1–2 care coordinators |
| **US Commercial Lead** (Delaware corp) | $40–80k | clinician network |

The co-founder as CTO of record removes the largest line in the budget (a ₹32–38L Bengaluru CTO) **and** gives the Delaware entity genuine US substance from October 2026 — which is what makes §3's regular-petition argument work.

### Minimum defensible chart — the realistic one

| Role | Comp |
|---|---|
| CTO (co-founder, MA) | equity |
| Clinical Data Scientist (India) — owns calibration | ₹16L |
| 2 engineers (India, second from month 6) | ₹16L + ₹15L |
| Member Ops Lead (India) → 1 coordinator | ₹11L + ₹6L |

Five India employees plus her, plus the US CTO. India payroll lands at **₹64L of staff + her ₹10–15L ≈ ₹75–80L (~$90–96k) annualized at petition time.**

At `06a §5`'s ~48–50% gross margin, sustaining that needs roughly **₹1.6–1.7Cr (~$190–200k) annualized revenue — about 150 India members at ₹24,000 plus 125 US members at $1,200**, producing ~₹84L of gross profit against ~₹75–80L of payroll. ⚠️ **The 125 US members are the aggressive half**, since the US launch only begins around month 10.

⚠️ **So the month-15–18 revenue gate in `06a` is not merely cash flow — it is what produces a filable org chart.** Miss it and you don't just have less money; you have a petition resting on three people.

### If the chart comes in thin — two fallbacks, used together

**Function manager.** She manages an essential function — the clinical calibration and risk-model layer — rather than headcount. Legitimate, but heavily scrutinized: name the function, prove it is essential, show she **directs rather than performs** it, show she sits senior in the hierarchy, and show **someone else does the day-to-day work in it.**

**Reasonable needs.** Argue (44)(C) explicitly: an early-stage health-data company with a US parent and an Indian delivery subsidiary reasonably needs this staffing at this stage — with a documented forward hiring plan.

⚠️ Neither fallback rescues a founder who is visibly doing the work.

### The three things that kill these petitions

1. ⚠️ **She is the only real worker.** If the commits, the reports, the customer calls, and the analyses trace back to her, it is denied regardless of titles. **Delegation stops being a management preference and becomes a legal requirement** — and it costs money, because delegating means paying someone to do what a founder would do for free. This is the central tension of the track: a bootstrapped company wants a founder who does everything; L-1A requires one who does none of it.
2. ⚠️ **Subordinates who aren't professionals.** A role that doesn't require a degree does not count toward professional supervision. Write the degree requirement into the JD **and hire someone who holds it.**
3. ⚠️ **Contractors instead of employees.** USCIS discounts contractors heavily. **Indian payroll with EPF is worth far more than freelancers on invoices** — pay the ~12% + 12% employer contribution and treat it as evidence spend, not overhead.

### The documentation regime — starts November 2026, not 2028

Cheap now, impossible to reconstruct later:

- [ ] **Written appointment letter for every hire**, with percentage-of-time breakdowns and the degree requirement stated.
- [ ] **Her appointment letter + Delaware board resolution** naming her CEO with an executive JD — this establishes "general supervision from a board."
- [ ] **Quarterly Delaware board minutes** recording that she reports to the board. Among the strongest executive-capacity evidence available; costs an hour a quarter.
- [ ] **India payroll trail:** salary slips, **EPF challans**, Form 24Q, **Form 16 for every employee**, her own ITR.
- [ ] **Versioned quarterly org charts** with names, titles, salaries, degrees.
- [ ] **Her personnel authority in writing:** offer letters she signed, appraisals she wrote, promotions, compensation decisions.
- [ ] **Proof she doesn't execute:** sprint boards with tasks assigned to engineers, commits by others and **never by her**, clinician sign-offs by clinicians.
- [ ] **Executive discretion:** vendor, lab, clinician and aggregator contracts signed by her; the bank mandate; spending-approval thresholds.
- [ ] **Her calendar**, weighted to board, strategy, partnerships and hiring rather than delivery. A CEO's calendar is genuinely persuasive.

She is **on the India subsidiary's payroll** while serving as CEO and director of the Delaware parent. The dual role is legitimate, strengthens executive capacity, and keeps the one-year-abroad employment cleanly located in India.

⚠️ The petition must also show the **US** position is managerial or executive. The US Commercial Lead is not decoration — the Delaware corp needs real or credibly planned US subordinates, and the business plan must show US hiring in year one after arrival.

## §3 — L-1A mechanics

### Regular versus new office — the co-founder is worth two years of validity

| | New office | Regular |
|---|---|---|
| Validity | **1 year** | **3 years** (7 max) |
| Scrutiny | Heightened | Normal |
| Extra proof | Premises secured; office will support a managerial role within 12 months | — |

With the co-founder operating the Delaware corp from October 2026, a **Q1 2028 filing** sees ~16–18 months of operations, a US employee on payroll, US contracts, US revenue, MA registration, and two filed tax returns. **That is a regular petition** — which recovers what going software-first would otherwise have cost.

"**Doing business**" means the regular, systematic provision of services — **not mere existence.** Evidence file: bank statements, invoices, signed customer and clinician agreements, the co-founder's payroll records, MA foreign qualification, coworking or office lease, Forms 1120.

### Requirements she must satisfy

- **One continuous year of full-time employment abroad** with the qualifying entity within the preceding three years, **in a managerial or executive capacity.** Her clock: **2 Nov 2026 → 2 Nov 2027.** ⚠️ Her Sept–Oct 2026 work for the Delaware corp while physically in the US **does not count.**
- Because she keeps working for the India subsidiary continuously, the year **stays perpetually fresh** — which is what makes this a standing option rather than a one-shot.
- No blanket L is available at this size (blanket needs 3+ US branches plus $25M sales or 1,000 US employees). Individual petition only.

### Costs at filing *(⚠️ USCIS fees moved in 2024 and may move again — verify at filing, do not budget off these)*

| Item | Approx. |
|---|---|
| Government fees, small employer, incl. premium processing (15 business days) | **$4,000–4,500** |
| Attorney | **$8,000–15,000** |
| Matter-of-*Ho*-compliant business plan | **$3,000–8,000** |
| Consular MRV, per family member | ~$205 |

### Consular step

After I-129 approval: in-person interview at **Mumbai**. Per `01d`/`04c`, **dropbox ended October 2025** — budget **3–6 months of wait** and book the moment the petition approves.

⚠️ **This is the single largest execution risk in the track, larger than the USCIS stage.** Consular officers in India are notably skeptical of small-company L-1s. Expect scrutiny and a real 221(g) probability.

⚠️ **"Return in 2028" is optimistic.** Filing Q1 2028 + premium processing + a 3–6 month interview queue realistically lands entry in **late 2028, more likely Jan or Aug 2029.**

### What the family gets

- **Her:** L-1A principal.
- **You:** **L-2, work-authorized incident to status.** Get the I-94 annotated **L-2S** (employers accept it); you may still file I-765 for a physical card. **No sponsor, no lottery, no Disney.**
- **Kids:** L-2 until 21. Elder son turns 21 ~2034 — **seven years of runway**, then F-1.

## §4 — EB-1C: the third engine

Once the Delaware corp has been doing business a year (already true by 2028) and she has completed her managerial year abroad (~Nov 2027), **the corp petitions her for EB-1C. No PERM, no labor certification.** She would be coming to work for the petitioner as CEO — clean fit.

⚠️ **It is not fast.** EB-1 India has been retrogressed (recent bulletins around early 2022), so a 2028 priority date queues for years. **It rescues nothing on CSPA.**

✅ **File it as soon as she is eligible anyway.** At $715 plus premium processing, **the queue position is the asset.** If your EB-1A fails, this becomes the family's only remaining green-card track, and filing in 2028 rather than 2030 buys two years of seniority in a line you may need. Cheapest insurance in the entire plan.

## §5 — Interaction with the EB-1A, CSPA, and a real upside

`01d` **EB-1A remains primary and is untouched** — self-petitioned, filed ~Sept 2027. This business neither helps nor hurts it.

### ✅ The upside: adjustment of status instead of consular processing

If you are in the US on L-1A/L-2 when the EB-1A is approved and your ported **May-2021 priority date** is current, you can file **I-485 adjustment of status inside the US** rather than consular processing at Mumbai. **L-1 is dual-intent**, so there is no immigrant-intent conflict.

AOS gives the whole family **EADs and advance parole**, removes the consular interview, and lets you wait in the US. Materially better than `01d`'s consular path.

⚠️ Two coordination items for counsel: switching from consular to AOS mid-stream requires handling **NVC** properly; and if the I-140 is under RFE when you leave Disney India, that needs managing rather than discovering.

### On the elder son — be precise

- ❌ This does **not** change his CSPA calculation. That rests entirely on the EB-1A being filed by ~Sept 2027.
- ✅ It **does** give him **L-2 status to age 21 (~2034)**, then F-1. "Ages out with no status and no fallback" becomes "has lawful status while we solve it."
- ⚠️ Runway, not a cure. He still needs a green card before ~2034.

## §6 — The trigger, written down

Unexercised options rot. So this lives in `08-TRACKER-JOURNAL.md`, not in anyone's head.

### File the L-1A immediately on any ONE of:

1. **EB-1A I-140 denied** — or an RFE that specialist counsel assesses as more likely than not fatal.
2. **EB-1A not filed by 30 September 2027**, for any reason at all.
3. **EB-1A approved but the elder son's CSPA calculation comes back at 21 or over.**
4. **EB-1 India final action date retrogresses past May 2021**, making your ported date non-current.
5. **Any adverse change in your Disney India employment** that damages the EB-1A record before filing.

### Readiness deadline — auditable, not aspirational

⚠️ **From 1 January 2028, the L-1A must be filable within 60 days.** Which means **by 31 December 2027**:

- [ ] Org chart in place per §2 (≥4 professional reports, or the function-manager record built)
- [ ] Matter-of-*Ho* business plan drafted
- [ ] Twelve months of her India payroll documented (slips, EPF, Form 16, ITR)
- [ ] Delaware corp doing-business evidence assembled per §3
- [ ] L-1A attorney retained and briefed

## §8 — The firewall around you ⚠️

**This is the single most important section in the file.**

If you do the tech, the answer to USCIS's central question — *who actually runs this company?* — is **you**. She isn't supervising professional employees, because the person doing the core professional work is an unpaid, undocumented non-employee. The function-manager fallback dies too, since she would be managing a function you perform. **"She is the face of the business" is, almost word for word, how an officer describes a nominee in a denial.**

⚠️ **And the worse branch:** if the petition documents her as running the technology while you actually run it, that is **material misrepresentation under INA 212(a)(6)(C)(i) — a permanent inadmissibility bar.** Not a denial you refile from. It would also contaminate your EB-1A, which is the family's only current green-card track, and the elder son's status rides on that.

The asymmetry: upside of your unpaid help ≈ ₹35L saved. Downside ≈ the whole plan plus a lifetime bar.

**Independent problem: Disney IP.** Your employment agreement almost certainly assigns inventions and may reach outside work. If you architect the calibration engine, **Disney may hold a colourable claim on the company's only real asset** — surfacing in diligence at the worst possible moment.

### The bright-line test

> **If your contribution vanished tomorrow, would the product stop?**
> If yes, you are the CTO and the petition is unfilable.

### The line, concretely

| ✅ Permissible | ❌ Not permissible |
|---|---|
| Being married to her; talking with her | Choosing the stack; owning the architecture |
| Helping her understand technical concepts well enough to manage engineers competently | Writing or reviewing code |
| Informal reactions to a résumé | Evaluating engineering candidates as the technical assessor |
| — | Sitting in the production loop |
| — | Doing the legal work (see below) |

⚠️ **The firewall follows the substance, not the org chart.** If architecture flows from you *through the co-founder*, it is the same defect wearing a different name.

**On "legal":** the FEMA opinion, the FDA scoping opinion, the HIPAA / My Health My Data / DPDP work, and the petition all need licensed professionals. The **$15–25k legal line in `06a` is the alternative to your help**, not an optional extra.

⚠️ **The immigration attorney must know the real family arrangement from day one.** A plan that requires concealing your involvement from your own lawyer has already crossed from aggressive into fraudulent.
