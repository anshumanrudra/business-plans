To SunPower Financing:

I hereby cancel the transaction evidenced by the SunPower Financing Agreement, Lease #4593311 (the "Lease"),
for the solar photovoltaic system installed at 498 Plainville Ave, Unionville (Town of Farmington), Connecticut
06085 (the "Property," and the leased equipment, the "System"). I do not intend to be bound by the Lease. This

consistent with Conn. Gen. Stat. § 42-137(c), under which a notice of cancellation is effective as long as
"it indicates the intention on the part of the buyer not to be bound" by the transaction.

## 1. Why this right is still available

The Lease is a home solicitation sale within the meaning of Conn. Gen. Stat. § 42-134a(a): the statute provides
that "home solicitation sale" "means a sale, lease, or rental of consumer goods or services" of the kind
personally solicited away from the seller's place of business, and the Lease was personally solicited away from
SunPower's place of business, signed at the Property.

The Lease is not effective against me under Conn. Gen. Stat. § 42-135a, which provides that "No agreement in
a home solicitation sale shall be effective against the buyer" unless the seller has complied with the
statute's disclosure and form requirements — including furnishing a "fully completed" copy, since among the
seller's obligations is not to "Fail to furnish the buyer with a fully completed receipt or copy of all
contracts." The copy furnished to me was not fully completed and did not comply with the statute's cancellation
form requirements, for at least three independent reasons:

- The Federal Consumer Leasing Act Disclosures page furnished to me bears three blank dollar fields in the
  "Other Charges" column, including the Total, so the "receipt or copy of all contracts" was not "fully
  completed" as § 42-135a(1) requires.
- The cancellation statement furnished with the Lease was not printed in immediate proximity to the space
  reserved for my signature, as the statute requires for a compliant notice of the right to cancel.
- The cancellation notice furnished with the Lease stated that any refund would be made within ten **calendar**
  days, when Conn. Gen. Stat. § 42-138(a) prescribes ten **business** days for the seller to tender any
  payments made — a deviation that shortens, rather than expands, my rights under the statute.

Because no compliant notice of the right to cancel was ever furnished, the cancellation period never began to
run. I note for completeness, without conceding its sufficiency, one further and independent irregularity in
the cancellation form furnished with the Lease: Exhibit C states the "(Date of Lease)" as 1/19/2023 and gives a
cancellation deadline of 1/26/2023 — that is, it runs the cancellation period from January 19, 2023, the date
SunPower's representative (Sherilyn M. Cano) countersigned, rather than from January 16, 2023, the day I signed,
as Conn. Gen. Stat. § 42-137(a) requires. That irregularity is not the basis for this notice — the three
defects identified above are — but it confirms that the cancellation form furnished did not conform to the
statute. Because the statutory period never began to run, this cancellation is timely regardless of how many
calendar days have elapsed since January 2023.

## 2. What SunPower must now do

Upon this cancellation, Conn. Gen. Stat. § 42-138(a) requires that "the seller shall tender to the buyer any
payments made by the buyer" under the Lease. I have made 41 monthly payments to date, totaling approximately
**$3,740.98** (an estimate pending the full payment ledger, evidence item E5; the exact figure will be stated in
`06-DAMAGES-SCHEDULE.md` once that ledger is obtained, and this notice's estimate is superseded by that
schedule). I demand tender of that full amount.

Conn. Gen. Stat. § 42-139(c) provides that, following a timely cancellation, "the seller is entitled to no
compensation" for any services rendered prior to cancellation. I have no obligation to pay for the System's
installation, monitoring, or any other service performed under the Lease.

I further demand that SunPower cancel any security interest arising out of the transaction, including any UCC
financing statement or fixture filing of record against the Property. This is not merely a statutory demand:
SunPower's own Exhibit C to the Lease states that upon cancellation "any security interest arising out of the
transaction will be canceled."

## 3. Tender of the System

The System remains at 498 Plainville Ave, Unionville (Town of Farmington), Connecticut 06085, and I am not
obligated to tender it at any other location. Conn. Gen. Stat. § 42-139(a) provides that, upon demand following
cancellation, the buyer shall tender to the seller any goods delivered under the sale, "but he is not obligated
to tender at any place other than his own address."

Twenty days from the date this notice is received, SunPower's right to retake the System expires. Conn. Gen.
Stat. § 42-139(a) provides that if the seller fails to take possession of the goods within twenty days of the
date of cancellation, "the goods shall become the property of the buyer without obligation to pay for them."
SunPower's own Exhibit C states the identical consequence in its own words: if the System is "not picked up
within [twenty] days," "you may retain or dispose of the goods without any further obligation" to SunPower.

## 4. The Lease's own waiver clause does not bar this notice

Lease § 4(e) purports to waive "ALL RIGHTS ... TO REJECT OR CANCEL THIS LEASE," but the clause carves out
exactly this notice by its own terms: it applies "EXCEPT AS REQUIRED BY APPLICABLE LAW OR AS SET FORTH IN
SECTIONS 8 ... AND 28 (NOTICE OF RIGHT TO CANCEL)." This cancellation is required by applicable law — Conn.
Gen. Stat. §§ 42-134a through 42-139 — and § 4(e) does not purport to waive it.

consent to entry or removal absent that notice and absent resolution of the pending arbitration.
