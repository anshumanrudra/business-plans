**My primary position is that the SunPower Financing Agreement, Lease #4593311 (the "Lease"), for the solar
photovoltaic system installed at 498 Plainville Ave, Unionville (Town of Farmington), Connecticut 06085 (the

**In the alternative, and without prejudice to that primary position, I hereby cancel the transaction evidenced
by the Lease.** I do not intend to be bound by the Lease. This statement is intended to be effective as a notice
of cancellation regardless of the form in which it is given, consistent with Conn. Gen. Stat. § 42-137(c), under

to be bound" by the transaction. Section 2 below sets out the basis on which I contend that cancellation remains
available.

## 1. The Lease is not effective against me — and that does not depend on any deadline

The Lease is a home solicitation sale within the meaning of Conn. Gen. Stat. § 42-134a(a): the statute provides
that "home solicitation sale" "means a sale, lease, or rental of consumer goods or services" of the kind
personally solicited away from the seller's place of business, and the Lease was personally solicited away from
SunPower's place of business, signed at the Property.

Conn. Gen. Stat. § 42-135a provides that "No agreement in a home solicitation sale shall be effective against
the buyer" if the seller commits any of the nine failures the section then lists — including a failure to
"Fail to furnish the buyer with a fully completed receipt or copy of all contracts." That provision is not a
cancellation right and it imposes no deadline on the buyer. It is a standing condition on the seller's ability
to enforce the agreement at all: where the seller has failed, the agreement has never been effective against the
buyer, and there is accordingly nothing for the buyer to cancel and nothing for the buyer to pay.

The copy furnished to me was not fully completed and the cancellation documents furnished with it did not comply
with § 42-135a, on each of the following five independent grounds. These five grounds, in this order, are the
grounds on which this notice is given:

- **Ground A — the copy furnished was not "fully completed."** The Federal Consumer Leasing Act Disclosures page
  furnished to me bears three blank dollar fields in the "Other Charges" column, including the Total, so the
  "receipt or copy of all contracts" was not "fully completed" as § 42-135a(1) requires.
- **Ground B — the refund period stated shortens my rights.** The cancellation notice furnished with the Lease
  stated that any refund would be made within ten **calendar** days, when Conn. Gen. Stat. § 42-138(a) prescribes
  ten **business** days for the seller to tender any payments made — a deviation that shortens, rather than
  expands, my rights under the statute.
- **Ground C — placement, detachability, and duplicate form.** The cancellation statement furnished with the
  Lease was not printed in immediate proximity to the space reserved for my signature; the NOTICE OF CANCELLATION
  was not furnished as an "easily detachable" form; and it was not furnished as "a completed form in duplicate,"
  each of which § 42-135a(1) and (2) require.
- **Ground D — the prescribed statement was not used, and not in the prescribed type.** Section 42-135a(1)
  requires that, in immediate proximity to the buyer's signature space, there appear "in boldface type of a
  minimum size of ten points, a statement in substantially the following form": "YOU, THE BUYER, MAY CANCEL THIS
  TRANSACTION AT ANY TIME PRIOR TO MIDNIGHT OF THE THIRD BUSINESS DAY AFTER THE DATE OF THIS TRANSACTION. SEE THE
  ATTACHED NOTICE OF CANCELLATION FORM FOR AN EXPLANATION OF THIS RIGHT." Section 42-135a(2) likewise requires the
  NOTICE OF CANCELLATION to carry its prescribed content "in ten-point boldface type." The statement printed in
  the Lease is not that statement, and neither it nor the Exhibit C form was set in boldface type of a minimum
  size of ten points. I do not contend that the length of the period stated in the Lease worked to my
  disadvantage; the defect I rely on is that the statute prescribes a particular statement, in a particular type
  size, and neither was used.
- **Ground E — the form was completed with the wrong transaction date.** Section 42-135a(3) required SunPower,
  before furnishing the copies, to complete both copies with the date of the transaction and a cancellation date
  "not earlier than the third business day following the date of the transaction." Exhibit C states the "(Date of
  Lease)" as 1/19/2023 and gives a cancellation deadline of 1/26/2023 — that is, it runs the period from
  January 19, 2023, the date SunPower's representative (Sherilyn M. Cano) countersigned, rather than from
  January 16, 2023, the day I signed, which is the day from which Conn. Gen. Stat. § 42-137(a) runs.

**The January 2023 dates do not answer any of this.** Section 42-137(a) fixes a three-business-day window in
which a buyer may *cancel*. Section 42-135a does something different: it withholds effectiveness from the
agreement altogether. Nothing in § 42-137(a) limits the time in which a buyer may assert that § 42-135a
condition, because it is not a cancellation right, and my assertion of it is not made under § 42-137(a) at all.
The time limit that does apply to a claim on a written instrument in Connecticut is the six-year period in Conn.
Gen. Stat. § 52-576, under which "No action for an account, or on any simple or implied contract, or on any
contract in writing, shall be brought but within six years after the right of action accrues" — which, measured
from January 2023, runs to approximately January 2029. This notice and the accompanying Demand are well within
that period.

## 4. Tender of the System — the System is made available to SunPower

**The System is hereby made available to SunPower at 498 Plainville Ave, Unionville (Town of Farmington),
Connecticut 06085.** That is where the System is and where it remains available for collection. I am not
obligated to tender it at any other location: Conn. Gen. Stat. § 42-139(a) provides that, upon demand following
cancellation, the buyer shall tender to the seller any goods delivered under the sale, "but he is not obligated
to tender at any place other than his own address." SunPower's own Exhibit C states the same obligation in its
own words — that on cancellation "you must make available to the seller at your residence, in substantially as
good condition as when received, any goods delivered to you under this contract or sale" — and I am performing it.
I will not obstruct collection of the System.

**When the twenty-day period starts.** Conn. Gen. Stat. § 42-137(b) fixes when cancellation occurs:
"Cancellation shall occur when the buyer gives written notice of cancellation to the seller at the address
specified for notice of cancellation provided by the seller or when such written notice bearing such address is
deposited in a mail box." The twenty-day period in § 42-139(a) — which runs "within twenty days after a home
solicitation sale has been cancelled" — therefore runs from that date, being the date this notice is given to
SunPower at the address specified for notice of cancellation or deposited in the mail, whichever is earlier, and
not from the date of SunPower's receipt. The date is recorded in the service record at the end of this notice.

**What happens at the end of it.** Conn. Gen. Stat. § 42-139(a) provides that "If the seller fails to take
possession of such goods within twenty days after cancellation" then "the goods shall become the property of the
buyer without obligation to pay for them." SunPower's own Exhibit C states the identical consequence in its own
words: if the System is not picked up within twenty days after the date of the notice of cancellation, "you may
retain or dispose of the goods without any further obligation" to SunPower.

## 5. The Lease's own waiver clause does not bar this notice

Lease § 4(e) purports to waive "ALL RIGHTS ... TO REJECT OR CANCEL THIS LEASE," but the clause carves out
exactly this notice by its own terms: it applies "EXCEPT AS REQUIRED BY APPLICABLE LAW OR AS SET FORTH IN
SECTIONS 8 ... AND 28 (NOTICE OF RIGHT TO CANCEL)." This cancellation is required by applicable law — Conn.
Gen. Stat. §§ 42-134a through 42-139 — and § 4(e) does not purport to waive it.
