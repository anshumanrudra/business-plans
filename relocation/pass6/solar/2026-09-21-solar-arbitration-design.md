## 1. Objective

Three outcomes, in priority order:

1. **Lease cancelled** — no further payment obligation.
2. **Lien removed** — UCC-1/fixture filing terminated, land records cleared, refinance and sale unobstructed.
3. **Panels kept and running** — title to the System transferred to Claimant, system operational.

These three are in tension under the lease as written: §12(b) and Exhibit A §4(b) provide that on termination
*SunPower removes the System*. Getting all three therefore requires either (a) a statutory route that vests title
in Claimant, or (b) a purchase. This spec pursues (a) as the primary theory and (b) as a pleaded alternative, so
that no outcome of the arbitration leaves Claimant with the lease still in force.

**JAMS, Streamlined Arbitration Rules**, per Lease §19. Venue "most convenient to your Home" — Hartford County.

Verified mechanics:

- **Claimant's cost is $125.** Lease §19: "If you initiate the arbitration, you will be required to pay the first

- **Rule 13 gives automatic document exchange** within 14 calendar days of receipt of claims, covering "all

you live, then we will use another accredited arbitration provider with offices close to your Home." There is no
JAMS office in Hartford County, which arguably opens AAA's Consumer Rules. **Decision: file with JAMS anyway.**
§19 names JAMS as administrator, and a forum fight burns the opening move for no gain.

## 6. Claims architecture

### Count 1 — Declaratory: the Lease is not effective against Claimant (§ 42-135a)

**§ 42-134a(a)** defines a home solicitation sale to expressly include a "**lease** ... of consumer goods ... in which
the seller or his representative **personally solicits** the sale ... and the buyer's agreement or offer to purchase
is made at a place other than the place of business of the seller."

Elements and how they are met:

| Element | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Lease of consumer goods | Lease §10 ("removable equipment ... Our personal property under the Uniform Commercial Code and not a fixture"); §5(a)(ix) requires use "primarily for personal, family or household purposes" — matching § 42-134a(b) |
| Personally solicited | One-to-one live video sales presentation by a SAVKAT representative |
| Signed away from seller's place of business | Signed at the Property via DocuSign |

**§ 42-135a** then provides: "**No agreement in a home solicitation sale shall be effective against the buyer** if it
is not signed and dated by the buyer or if the seller shall: (1) Fail to furnish the buyer with a fully completed
receipt or copy of all contracts ... and **in immediate proximity to the space reserved in the contract for the
signature of the buyer** ... and in **boldface type of a minimum size of ten points**, a statement in substantially
the following form: YOU, THE BUYER, MAY CANCEL THIS TRANSACTION AT ANY TIME PRIOR TO MIDNIGHT OF THE **THIRD
BUSINESS DAY** AFTER THE DATE OF THIS TRANSACTION..."

Alleged defects, verified against the executed PDF (pages inspected: 14, 24, 25):

1. **The document furnished was not "fully completed" — the strongest defect.** § 42-135a(1) requires the seller
   to "furnish the buyer with a **fully completed** receipt or copy of all contracts and documents pertaining to
   such sale at the time of its execution." On the executed **Federal Consumer Leasing Act Disclosures** page
   (Exhibits A–E p.8 / PDF p.24), the "Other Charges" column contains **three blank dollar fields**, rendered on
   the signed instrument as:

   ```
   $____________
   $____________
   Total
   $____________
   ```

   Claimant's signature appears immediately below, under the line "You acknowledge that you have received and
   read these disclosures prior to the receipt of the Lease." **This requires no interpretation** — no argument
   about what "personally solicits" means, no comparison of cancellation periods. It is a facial defect in the
   instrument SunPower drafted and furnished.

   The same blanks are independently a **Regulation M** problem (12 CFR 1013.4 requires "other charges" and
   their total to be disclosed), but note the Consumer Leasing Act's private-damages limitation period is
   **one year** (15 U.S.C. § 1667d via § 1640(e)) and expired in January 2024. **Plead Reg M only as evidence
   of the § 42-135a(1) violation, never as a standalone damages count.**

2. **A deviation that shortens Claimant's rights.** The statute's prescribed form states payments will be
   returned "WITHIN **TEN BUSINESS DAYS** FOLLOWING RECEIPT BY THE SELLER OF YOUR CANCELLATION NOTICE."
   Exhibit C says "**TEN CALENDAR DAYS**." Ten calendar days is *shorter* than ten business days. This matters
   disproportionately: a substantial-compliance defence is far weaker where the deviation **reduces** the
   consumer's statutory entitlement rather than enlarging it. Contrast defect 4, which is more generous and
   therefore more forgivable — lead with this one.

3. **Not in immediate proximity to the signature.** § 42-135a(1) requires the cancellation statement "in
   immediate proximity to the space reserved in the contract for the signature of the buyer." §28 appears on
   lease p.15; Claimant's signature block is p.16; the Exhibit C forms are at exhibit pp.9–10 (PDF pp.25–26).
   Relatedly, § 42-135a(2) requires the NOTICE OF CANCELLATION form be "**easily detachable**" — an exhibit
   embedded at p.25 of a 29-page DocuSign envelope is not detachable in any practical sense.
   *Typeface size appears to satisfy the 10-point boldface requirement — do not press that sub-point.*

4. **Wrong period and wrong prescribed text.** Lease §28 and both Exhibit C forms give "**SEVEN CALENDAR
   DAYS**," not the statutory "THIRD BUSINESS DAY" formulation. Weaker than defects 1–3 because it is more
   generous to Claimant; plead it for completeness, not as the lead.

5. **Transaction date misstated.** Both Exhibit C forms relabel the statute's "(Date of Transaction)" as
   "**(Date of Lease)**" and populate it with **1/19/2023** — the Lessor's countersignature date — setting the
   deadline at 1/26/2023. § 42-137(a) runs the period from "the day on which **the buyer** signs," i.e.
   **1/16/2023**. The form is keyed to the wrong trigger and mislabels the statutory field.

**Elements satisfied by SunPower (do not contest these):** the form is captioned "NOTICE OF CANCELLATION"; it was
furnished in duplicate (Lessor Copy and Lessee Copy, PDF pp.25–26), satisfying § 42-135a(2)'s "in duplicate"
requirement; and §19's arbitration acknowledgment **was** properly initialed ("AR", PDF p.14), so there is no
route to court and no attack on the arbitration clause itself.

**Consequence sought:** because compliant notice was never furnished, the agreement is not effective against
Claimant, and the cancellation period never began to run.

**Anticipated defence — § 42-134a(a)(3).** Excludes a transaction "conducted and consummated **entirely by mail
or telephone** and without any other contact between the buyer and the seller or its representative **prior to
delivery of the goods or performance of the services**." Two independent rebuttals:

- **Not within the enumerated media.** The transaction ran over live videoconference and DocuSign. Neither is
  "mail" nor "telephone." The list is closed and dates from 1967/1976. The CGA's own annotation to Ch. 740
  states the Act's "purpose ... is to protect the consumer from high pressure sales tactics" (6 Conn. Cir. Ct.
  745) — squarely describing a one-hour video close with an expiring $1,000 discount.
- **There was other contact before delivery.** The exclusion's second prong runs to *delivery of the goods*, not
  to signing. Lease §8(a)(i) makes "completion of (A) the engineering site audit (a **thorough physical
  inspection of the Property**...)" a condition precedent to SunPower's obligation to lease, and installation
  ran ~2023-07-18 to 2023-07-23 — all before interconnection.

**Honest weighting.** The count now has two clearly separable halves, and they carry very different risk:

- **Threshold (is this a home solicitation sale at all?) — weak/novel.** No Connecticut decision applies the HSSA
  to a video sales presentation, and § 42-134a(a)(3) is a live defence. **Case research required.** This is
  where the count will be won or lost.
- **Merits (was the notice compliant?) — strong.** Once past the threshold, defects 1–3 are mechanical and
  documentary. Defect 1 in particular is proved by the face of SunPower's own executed instrument.

So the count is not uniformly weak; it is **gated**. That is a better posture than before, but it does not change
the plan: Count 7 remains the floor, because a threshold loss defeats Counts 1 and 2 entirely regardless of how
strong the notice defects are.

### Count 3 — Breach of contract: the no-lien and refinance-cooperation covenants

The strongest count, and the one that maps directly to objective 2.

- **§5(c)(xiv)** — SunPower agrees to "**not put a lien on your Home or Property**."
- **Exhibit E** — "We hereby further acknowledge and agree that **We do not have a lien on the Real Property**, and
  that We only have an interest in the System pursuant to the Lease"; "**We will not impede any sale** of the Real
  Property"; "to the extent that We are deemed to have a lien on any portion of the Real Property that such lien
  shall be **subject and subordinate in all respects to the Security Instrument**"; "the Fixture Filing **does not
  make the home ineligible** for FHA insurance"; and a representation of compliance with Fannie Mae B2-3-04.
- **§4(b)(i)** — SunPower may charge "reasonable expenses" for cooperation "If you sell or refinance your Home and
  We are asked or required to provide any cooperation." The right to bill for cooperation presupposes the duty to
  cooperate.

**Breach theory:** a fixture filing appearing on the Farmington land records as an encumbrance on the real
property, plus refusal or failure to furnish a subordination or estoppel letter on request, obstructing the
refinance and forcing a mortgage recast instead.

**Damages:** the interest-rate differential between the refinance Claimant sought and the recast he took,
over the remaining loan term, plus recast fees and out-of-pocket costs.

**Counter-argument to meet:** §10 expressly authorises the filing — "You also acknowledge that We have the right
to file any UCC-1 financing statement or fixture filing that confirms Our interest in the System." The claim is
therefore **not** that filing was unauthorised. It is that (a) the filing as made encumbers the *Real Property*
rather than only the System, which §5(c)(xiv) and Exhibit E forbid, and/or (b) they failed the separate
cooperation and subordination duties. Framing matters — plead it that way.

**Plead this count without reference to the co-owner.** Claimant's spouse is a confirmed record co-owner who
never signed, but per §6.2 that fact is held in reserve rather than pleaded — raising it is what triggers the
§1(c) counterclaim, and Count 3 does not need it. The breach is complete on its own terms: SunPower covenanted
not to lien the Real Property and represented that it holds no such lien, and a filing that encumbers the Real
Property breaches both. The title commitment goes into evidence to prove what the examiner flagged; build no
argument on top of it.

### Count 5 — Breach of the Production Guarantee

Exhibit A §2(c) and Table A. The **Year 2** checkpoint is 10,465 kWh at a Guaranteed Energy Price of $0.187/kWh.
Guarantee Periods run in 24-month increments from the **Initial Communication Date** (first sustained internet
communication after the Lease Term Start Date) — not from the lease date.

With the Lease Term Start Date now fixed at **April 2023** and energization in **May 2023**, the Initial
Communication Date is ~May 2023 — roughly one month after the Lease Term Start Date. Because that is **under 12
months**, Exhibit A §2(c)'s degradation step-down does not apply and **Guarantee Period 1 governs at 10,465 kWh**
(not the Guarantee Period 2 figure).

- **Guarantee Period 1: ~May 2023 – May 2025. True-up came due ~May 2025 — over sixteen months ago, and
  actionable now.** Estimated two-year production is ~11,642 kWh (2 × 5,821), against a 10,465 kWh guarantee, so
  a shortfall is plausible but must be proved from the monitoring export.
- **Guarantee Period 2: ~May 2025 – May 2027.** Not due until ~May 2027. **Do not plead it as a present breach.**

Both the 6-year contract period and the 3-year CUTPA window (on/after 2023-09-21) comfortably cover the
~May 2025 true-up.

If Actual Biennial kWh fell below 10,465 and no Payment Amount was remitted once it exceeded the $5.00 minimum,
that is a breach. Requires the monitoring export to prove.

**Watch the trap:** the "Communication Requirements" condition the guarantee on Claimant maintaining an internet
connection transmitting at least two consecutive hours monthly. Expect SunPower to argue forfeiture. Exhibit A
§2(c)(iv) blunts this: on "hardware, communication, or other failure," Operator "will use commercially reasonable
methods to estimate the missing kWh based on utility bills or other available information."

### Count 6 — CUTPA (§§ 42-110b, 42-110g)

Two routes:

1. **Per se, via § 42-141(b):** "Violation of any of the provisions of sections 42-135a, or 42-137 to 42-139,
   inclusive, or failure to honor any provisions of the notice..." is made an unfair or deceptive practice.
   Counts 1–2 therefore carry CUTPA remedies automatically if they succeed.
2. **Independent unfair or deceptive acts, restricted to on/after 2023-09-21:** refusing to furnish
   subordination or estoppel documentation for the refinance; billing or requiring Claimant to pay for repairs
   covered by the Repair Promise; failing to remit the Year 2 Production Guarantee Payment Amount; failing to
   give §21 notice of the servicing transfer.

Remedies under § 42-110g: "actual damages," and "The court may, in its discretion, award **punitive damages** and
may provide such **equitable relief** as it deems necessary or proper" (subsec. (a)); "costs and reasonable
attorneys' fees" and "in addition to damages or in lieu of damages, **injunctive or other equitable relief**"
(subsec. (d)). Subsection (a) expressly reaches lessors. The equitable-relief hook in (a) and (d) is the basis
for ordering the UCC-3 termination and the title transfer.

Per § 42-110g(c) practice, mail a copy to the Attorney General and the Commissioner of Consumer Protection. (The
statute is framed for court actions; do it anyway — it costs nothing and creates regulatory pressure.)

### Count 7 is worth less than it first appeared — say so plainly

With the Lease Term Start Date fixed at April 2023, **§11(a)(i) opens the purchase option unconditionally in
April 2028** — about **19 months** from now, not the ~2 years previously estimated. And April 2028 is also
precisely when the §11(b) recapture adder **falls to zero**.

So Count 7 buys roughly 19 months of acceleration at the cost of a 20–40% tax-credit-recapture adder that would
otherwise disappear on its own. That is a genuinely narrower prize than the earlier estimate, and it cuts two

- It **weakens** Count 7 as a standalone objective. If the only goal were "own the panels eventually at FMV,"

- It **does not weaken** Count 7 as a floor inside this arbitration, which is its actual job here. It still

negotiation. The leverage in this case is Counts 1–2 (total loss of the asset plus restitution) and Count 3

## 6.2 The non-signatory co-owner — RESERVED, not filed

**Confirmed fact: Claimant's spouse is a record co-owner of 498 Plainville Ave and never signed the Lease.**

**Decision, 2026-09-21: she will not file independently unless it becomes necessary.** This section records why
that is safe, how the fact is used without filing, and the precise conditions that would change the answer.

### Why her action is not needed

Objective 2 (clear the lien) is reachable inside the arbitration alone:

- §19 gives the arbitrator "the authority to award **any legal or equitable remedy or relief that a court could
  order or grant** under this Lease."
- § 42-110g(a) permits "such **equitable relief** as it deems necessary or proper"; § 42-110g(d) permits
  "injunctive or other equitable relief" in addition to or in lieu of damages.

So the arbitrator can order SunPower to file a UCC-3 termination and record a release (relief item 6). That
binds SunPower, and once the filing is discharged her interest is cleared as a practical matter — without ever
adjudicating her title. Her separate action is therefore a **backstop, not a component**.

### Why deferring costs nothing

A § 47-31 action to settle title is not "founded upon a tort," so § 52-577's three years does not apply, and the
cloud on title is continuing rather than a single past act. Unlike the CUTPA claims — which are hard-boxed to
acts after 2023-09-21 — **her claim does not decay while held in reserve.** That asymmetry is what makes this
deferral genuinely low-cost rather than a gamble. *Confirm via research item 9 before relying on it.*

### How the fact is used without filing

The co-ownership is retained as **evidence and as a prepared defence** — never as an affirmative allegation.

- **Count 3 is pleaded without her.** The breach stands on its own terms: SunPower covenanted in §5(c)(xiv) not
  to "put a lien on your Home or Property" and represented in Exhibit E that "We do not have a lien on the Real
  Property," and a filing that encumbers the Real Property breaches both. No argument about her interest is
  required to make that out.
- **Her co-ownership appears only as causation evidence.** The title commitment from the failed refinance shows
  what the examiner actually flagged. Put in the document; do not build an argument on top of it.
- **Do not plead that the filing is void as to her interest.** That is the affirmative move that triggers the
  §1(c) counterclaim, and it is unnecessary to win Count 3.

### The prepared defence, if SunPower raises it first

They may raise her non-signature themselves — if they performed the §8(a)(i) real-estate due diligence they were
required to perform, they already know. Lease §1 has **Claimant** represent "(c) any other owners of the Property
have acknowledged this Lease by execution hereof," and §5(a)(xii) obliges him to "have anyone who has an
ownership interest in your Home sign this Lease." Both are unperformed, which they will route through §16(a)(ii)
to §17 and the **§18 Early Termination Liability**.

Keep these three answers fully briefed and ready, but **do not volunteer them**:

1. **Their own condition precedent defeats it.** §8(a)(i) makes "real estate due diligence to confirm the
   suitability of the Property for the construction, installation and operation of the System" a condition
   precedent to *their* obligation to lease. A lessor that records fixture filings runs title as a matter of
   course. Either they ran title and knew of the co-owner — so they proceeded with knowledge and cannot now
   complain — or they did not, and their own §8(a)(i) diligence failed while they filed against a fee they had
   not verified. Both branches favour Claimant.
2. **Waiver and estoppel.** They accepted 41 payments over three years with the System in place. §20 says delay
   alone is not waiver, but actual or constructive knowledge plus full performance is a materially stronger
   estoppel case than mere delay.
3. **Cure is illusory.** The §16(a)(ii) cure would be obtaining her signature now. She will not give it. The
   "default" is one they created by never obtaining it in the first place.

### The claim that stays unfiled permanently

She has a plausible **continuing trespass** claim. Do not use it. Its natural remedy is removal or ejectment,
which destroys objective 3 — winning it would cost Claimant the panels. It is not leverage either, because
threatening a remedy you do not want invites them to grant it.
