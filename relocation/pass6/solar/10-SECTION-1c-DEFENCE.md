## 1. The exposure

Lease §1(c) has the Claimant represent, as a condition of the Lease, that "any other owners of the Property have
acknowledged this Lease by execution hereof." Lease §5(a)(xii) separately obliges the Claimant to "have anyone
who has an ownership interest in your Home sign this Lease." Neither representation is true: a record co-owner
of the Property never signed the Lease, and Claimant never obtained her signature.

If SunPower raises this, the likely route is:

1. **Lease §16(a)(ii)** — failure to perform a material obligation "undertaken in this Lease," uncured for
   fifteen (15) days after written notice: "such failure continues for a period of fifteen (15) days after" We
   provide written notice.
2. **Lease §17** — SunPower's remedies on default, including termination and repossession of the System.
3. **Lease §18** — Early Termination Liability: the unpaid Monthly Payments for the year of default, plus Fair
   Market Value, plus removal costs.

That is the exposure this document is prepared to answer. It is not raised affirmatively because doing so would
hand SunPower the theory before it has one — see Objectives and Prohibitions in `00-CASE-STRATEGY.md`.

## 2. The three answers, in order of strength

### Answer 1 (strongest) — SunPower's own condition precedent defeats it

Lease §8(a)(i) makes "real estate due diligence to confirm the suitability of the Property" a condition
precedent to SunPower's own obligation to lease the System. A lessor that records UCC/fixture filings against
real property as a matter of routine business practice runs a title search before it proceeds — that is what
real estate due diligence to confirm suitability of the property means in this context.

This produces two branches, and both favour the Claimant:

- **Branch A — SunPower ran title and knew of the co-owner.** If SunPower's own §8(a)(i) diligence disclosed
  the co-owner's record interest before installation, SunPower proceeded to install the System, countersign the
  Lease, and file against the Property with that knowledge. A party that proceeds with full knowledge of the
  fact it later invokes as a default cannot now claim that fact as a material, uncured failure — the "failure"
  was known and accepted at the outset.
- **Branch B — SunPower did not run title, or ran an inadequate one.** If SunPower did not discover the
  co-owner's interest, then SunPower's own condition precedent under §8(a)(i) — confirming the suitability of
  the Property, which necessarily includes confirming who holds an interest in it before filing against it —
  was never satisfied. SunPower filed against a fee interest it never verified. It cannot rely on a condition
  precedent to its own performance that it failed to perform, to manufacture a default against the Claimant.

Either way, SunPower's own diligence obligation — not any act or omission of the Claimant's — is what stands
between SunPower and the co-owner's signature, and SunPower controlled that diligence.

### Answer 2 — waiver and estoppel

Claimant has made every Monthly Payment since 1 June 2023 — forty as at September 2026, and one more each month
thereafter (see `06-DAMAGES-SCHEDULE.md` D1) — over roughly three years with the System installed and in place. Lease
§20 (Waiver) provides that mere delay in enforcing a provision is not, by itself, a waiver. But this is not mere
delay. If SunPower had actual or constructive knowledge of the co-owner's non-signature — through its own
§8(a)(i) diligence, through the fixture filing process, or otherwise — and nonetheless accepted full performance
(every payment when due, energization, ongoing servicing) without ever invoking §1(c)/§5(a)(xii), that combination of
knowledge plus full performance is a materially stronger estoppel case than the bare delay §20 addresses. §20
answers "we were slow to enforce"; it does not answer "we knew and took the benefit of the bargain anyway."

### Answer 3 — the cure is illusory

Even if a §16(a)(ii) notice issues, the prescribed cure is to obtain the co-owner's signature within fifteen
days. She has not signed and will not sign. The "default" SunPower would be curing Claimant's failure to
produce is not curable by Claimant at all — it depends entirely on a third party's willingness, which does not
exist. And the underlying condition was one SunPower itself created by never obtaining the signature at signing,
when it controlled the closing process, rather than by any subsequent act of the Claimant's.

## 3. Anticipated counter-move

The expected SunPower response to any of the above is a **motion to compel arbitration against the non-signing
co-owner** on a direct-benefits estoppel theory (i.e., that she is bound to arbitrate because the System benefits
the Property she co-owns). This document does not brief that motion in advance beyond flagging it: it is noted
here so that its appearance is not a surprise if this reserve document is ever deployed.

## 4. Trigger conditions for the contingent § 47-31 action (deliverable `09`, not written)

A separate quiet-title-adjacent action under Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47-31 is a **contingent deliverable**,
deliberately not drafted (`09-SPOUSE-QUIET-TITLE.md` does not exist and is not to be created absent a trigger).
It would be filed only on one or more of the following:

(a) The arbitrator declines to grant equitable relief clearing the land records (i.e., Objective 2 — lien
    removal — fails in the JAMS proceeding);

(b) Counts 1–2 fail on the HSSA threshold **and** Count 7 (the §16(b) default / purchase-option floor) also
    fails, **and** SunPower refuses to release or subordinate its filing;

(c) SunPower asserts the §1(c)/§5(a)(xii) counterclaim addressed in this document. At that point the exposure
    described in Section 1 above has already materialised in the proceeding, and filing a separate action
    becomes comparatively low-risk — there is no longer a downside of "raising the point first";

(d) SunPower records any new or broader filing against the real property beyond what is currently of record.

Absent one of (a)–(d), no § 47-31 action is filed, no quiet-title theory is pleaded, and the co-owner's
non-signature is not raised. See the "Do not" list in `00-CASE-STRATEGY.md` and the trigger checklist in
`08-TIMELINE-AND-GATES.md`.
