## 0. The One Correction That Reframes Everything

**InfoServices holding your I-140 does NOT give you EB-1C eligibility. It gives you a portable priority date — which you keep no matter who sponsors EB-1C.**

- Under **8 CFR 204.5(e)**, your **May 2021 EB-2 priority date** attaches to *any* subsequently filed EB-1 petition (EB-1A **or** EB-1C), by *any* employer, as long as the old InfoServices I-140 is not revoked for fraud/misrepresentation/material error. Approved >180 days, it's safe from a withdrawal-based revocation (8 CFR 205.1).
- **Consequence:** choose the EB-1C sponsor on **who produces the strongest, lowest-risk petition** — not on "who holds the old I-140." Both InfoServices (US) → InfoServices India, and Disney (US) → Disney India, can carry your 2021 date equally.

So the real question isn't "InfoServices vs Disney because InfoServices has my I-140." It's **"which multinational gives me a cleaner qualifying relationship + a more defensible managerial role?"**

## 1. Is EB-1C Actually "Safer" Than EB-1A Right Now?

Be clear-eyed: **EB-1C is not a refuge from scrutiny.** In the current environment both categories draw heavy RFEs. They just fail on *different* things:

| | EB-1A denial/RFE hot-spots | EB-1C denial/RFE hot-spots |
|---|---|---|
| The core fight | "Sustained acclaim / final merits" is subjective | **"Managerial capacity"** — proving you manage *people/function*, not just senior work |
| Employer size | Irrelevant (self-petition) | **Small petitioners get hammered** — USCIS asks "does this org even need a manager?" |
| Structure | N/A | **Qualifying relationship** + US entity **"doing business" ≥1 yr** must be airtight |
| Your leverage | Your record | The **employer's** size/structure and how the role is written |

**The takeaway:** EB-1C de-risks *your* profile (you don't have to be "extraordinary"), but re-risks onto the *employer's structure*. That's why **the sponsor choice is the single biggest risk lever** — and why a large, obviously-multinational petitioner (Disney) is materially safer than a smaller consulting/staffing firm (InfoServices), even though both are legally eligible.

**Recommendation: dual-track.** Run **EB-1C as your primary**, but keep the **EB-1A evidence file building in parallel** (it's cheap, and both carry the 2021 PD) and hold **O-1A** as an interim work-visa fallback for the return. Don't bet the whole GC on one adjudicator's view of one category.

## 2. The Three EB-1C Requirements — and How You Meet Each

**(a) Qualifying corporate relationship.** The US petitioner must be the **parent, subsidiary, affiliate, or branch** of the foreign employer, and must have been **doing business in the US for ≥1 year** before filing.
- *Disney path:* Disney US ↔ Disney India — a textbook, unquestioned relationship. Near-zero risk on this prong.
- *InfoServices path:* InfoServices LLC (US) ↔ InfoServices India entity — **you must document the exact ownership link** (common ownership/control, share certificates, org chart). This is provable since they have an India entity, but it is the prong an RFE will probe for a smaller firm.

**(b) One continuous year of managerial/executive employment abroad, within the 3 years preceding the petition** (or preceding your last US admission).
- Your 2–3 year India stay gives you ample runway to accrue the year — **but only if the India role is genuinely managerial from day one and documented as such.** A month of "settling in as an IC" then a title change is a weak record; start managerial on arrival.
- You confirmed you'll **manage a team (people)** — this is the strongest posture ("personnel management," INA 101(a)(44)(A)). Target **≥4–6 professional direct reports**, real **hire/fire/appraisal authority**, and **budget/hiring-plan ownership**, spending **<50% of your time on hands-on engineering**.

**(c) Coming to the US to work for the petitioner in a managerial/executive role.** On return, the US petitioner (Disney US or InfoServices US) must place you in a manager/exec role too — not back to pure IC. Keep the US-side role lined up.

## 4. L-1A — The Bridge That Makes This Efficient

The **L-1A intracompany-transferee (manager/executive)** visa uses the *same* facts as EB-1C — qualifying relationship + 1 year abroad in managerial capacity — and is the natural vehicle:
- **On the way out** isn't needed (you're leaving the US, not entering it).
- **On the return**, an **L-1A gets you back into the US to work for the US petitioner as a manager** even before the green card is issued, and L-1A is **dual-intent** (no problem pursuing the GC). Then the US entity files/continues the **EB-1C I-140**, and you consular-process or adjust.
- L-1A requires the **1 continuous year abroad** in the prior 3 years — the same year that anchors EB-1C. Build it once, use it twice.
- **Blanket L** (large firms like Disney) makes L-1A faster; smaller firms file individual L-1A petitions.

This is often cleaner than returning on a fresh H-1B: L-1A → EB-1C is a single coherent manager narrative.
