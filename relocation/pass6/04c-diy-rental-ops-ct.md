## 1. The no-proxy architecture

You don't need a proxy for *most* of what a proxy does. You need one for a specific short list. Here's every function `04a §2` assigned to a co-trustee, and what replaces it.

| Function `04a` gave the co-trustee | Thumbtack can do it? | Replacement |
|---|---|---|
| **Show the house to prospects** | ❌ No — Thumbtack is a contractor marketplace, not a licensed leasing service | **Leasing-only agent** (§4), or smart-lock self-showing with pre-screening (§3 W2) |
| **Hold a spare set of keys** | ❌ | **Keyed lockbox + smart lock with per-visitor codes** you issue and revoke from India (Schlage Encode / Yale + Wi-Fi bridge). No physical key custody needed. |
| **Receive legal notices and service of process** | ❌ | ⚠️ **A named in-state agent — legally required disclosure, see §2.1.** Use a CT attorney offering this, or the leasing agent's brokerage. **This one cannot be automated away.** |
| **Emergency access at 3am (burst pipe, no heat)** | ⚠️ Partly — but Thumbtack is lead-gen, not 24/7 dispatch; response is hours-to-days | **Pre-vetted 24/7 plumber + HVAC on file** (call them in September, confirm they take after-hours calls and will bill a card on file) **+ tenant pre-authorized in the lease** to call them directly up to a spend cap **+ automatic water shutoff** (§8). The hardware is the real answer. |
| **Annual interior inspection** | ✅ Yes | Hire a handyman via Thumbtack, ~$100–150, with a written photo checklist you supply. Works fine. |
| **Biannual exterior drive-by** | ✅ Yes | Same, or fold into HVAC/gutter service visits. |
| **Move-out inspection** | ✅ Yes, but timing is unforgiving | Handyman + video walkthrough, **but the 30-day deposit clock (§2.2) means you must have this booked before the tenant's move-out date**, not after. |
| **Coordinate an insurance adjuster** | ⚠️ Weak | Adjusters will work with you by phone/email and with the tenant for access. Name a **US financial POA holder** (already a tracker task from `04b §8`) as the fallback signer. |
| **Sign documents you can't sign** | ❌ | **Limited (special) POA for this property** — see below. |

**The two pieces of paper that replace the co-trustee (do both, this month):**

1. **A limited/special power of attorney scoped to the property** — authorizes one named person to sign property-related documents, deal with the insurer, and authorize repairs if you're unreachable. Cheap and fast (~$150–300 with a CT attorney; notarization while you're still in the US). This is *not* the RLT and does not require the estate-planning engagement. If you truly have no willing individual, a **CT attorney can serve in this role for a fee** — ask when you engage them for the lease review.
2. **The in-state agent designation disclosed to the tenant** — §2.1. Legally required, and it's the item most likely to bite you.

> **Decouple the trust from all of this.** `04a §5` sequences "trust → rent" and implies the RLT must happen before the house becomes a rental (Garn-St. Germain framing). Reading the statute, **§1701j-3(d)(8) exempts a transfer into an inter vivos trust where you remain a beneficiary and which "does not relate to a transfer of rights of occupancy"** — it does not by its terms require the property to be your residence at transfer. So the RLT is **still available after the tenant moves in**, and it belongs with the estate-attorney engagement you need anyway for **QDOT + cross-border wills** (`04b §1.D`). Don't let a 5-week trust scramble delay the lease, and don't let the lease foreclose the trust. **Confirm this reading with counsel** — it's the one place I'm departing from `04a`.

## 2. The Connecticut layer (verified — and three of your planned terms are non-compliant)

*Sources: CT Judicial Branch,* Rights and Responsibilities of Landlords and Tenants in Connecticut *(HM-031); CGS §47a-6 text. Statute-level details flagged **[verify]** where I could not read the primary text.*

### 2.1 You must disclose an agent for notices and service of process — CGS §47a-6

**What it says:** it is the landlord's duty to notify the tenant **in writing, on or before the commencement of the tenancy**, of the person authorized to **manage the premises** and the person authorized to **receive notices and service of process**. If you don't, **the person who signed the rental agreement on your behalf is deemed your agent** for service and for performing the landlord's obligations.

**Why this matters more to you than to a normal landlord:** if you name yourself at a Bengaluru address, then notices and legal process are directed 8,000 miles away with Indian postal transit. If you name a leasing agent who then walks away after placement, you have designated an agent who isn't serving. And the fallback rule means **whoever signs the lease for you may become your statutory agent by default** — so if the leasing agent signs, that's a consequence you should choose deliberately rather than discover.

**Action:** name a **CT-resident agent** (attorney, brokerage, or your POA holder) in the lease and in a standalone written disclosure at signing. Get their written consent to serve. **[verify]** with counsel whether CT additionally requires a nonresident owner to *file* an agent designation with the Farmington Town Clerk or the Secretary of the State — I could not confirm a filing requirement from primary text, and it's a five-minute question for a CT attorney with real consequences (in some states an unfiled nonresident landlord cannot maintain a summary-process action).

### 2.2 Security deposit — escrow, interest, and a 30-day double-damages clock

**Verified:**
- **Cap: 2 months' rent** — but **1 month if the tenant is 62 or older.** (Screening consequence: you cannot apply a uniform "2 months for weak credit" rule to an older applicant.)
- The full deposit must sit in an **escrow account** at a financial institution **for the benefit of the tenant**. You hold only a security interest. **This is not your rental operating account** — `04a §2` item 2 routes everything through one account; the deposit must be segregated.
- It must earn **annual interest at a rate set by the CT Banking Commissioner**. The tenant earns interest **only for months rent was paid on time (within the grace period)**.
- **Return within 30 days** of move-out, with **interest**, and a **written itemized list** of any damages charged.
- ⚠️ **A landlord who does not respond within 30 days may be liable for DOUBLE the security deposit.** The tenant must give written notice of their new address.
- Questions go to the **Security Deposit Investigator, CT Banking Department, 260 Constitution Plaza, Hartford, (860) 240-8170**.

**What this changes for you:** a 30-day hard clock, run from India, on a task that requires a move-out inspection, contractor quotes, and an itemized statement. `04a §2` item 22 treats this casually ("within 30 days per state law"). **Build it as a dated calendar chain the moment any notice to vacate arrives**, and pre-book the inspection handyman for the move-out week rather than after it.

### 2.3 Grace period is 9 days — not 5 — and it gates eviction

**Verified:** rent unpaid **by midnight of the ninth day** after the due date (monthly/yearly leases; 4th day for week-to-week) is when the landlord may **start legal proceedings**.

⚠️ **`04a §2` items 15 and 18 are wrong for CT:** they set a "5-day grace period" with a "day-6 auto-reminder and late fee." **Change to a 9-day grace period.** Do not draft a lease that purports to charge a late fee on day 6 — and note the tenant-side booklet tells tenants the 9-day rule explicitly, so a non-compliant clause is likely to be challenged.

**Late-fee amount: [verify].** CT caps residential late fees (my understanding: the **lesser of $5/day up to a $50 maximum, or 5% of the monthly rent**, and chargeable only once the grace period has run). `04a` proposes "$50–75 or 5%" — the $75 figure is very likely over the cap. **Confirm the current cap and trigger with counsel and set the lease to it.** Separately, note the deposit-interest rule above ties tenant interest credit to payment "within the grace period," so define the grace period in the lease consistently.

### 2.4 You must offer a non-electronic way to pay rent — CGS §47a-3a **[verify cite]**

**Verified from the booklet:** for leases executed on or after **October 1, 2013**, the landlord must provide **a non-electronic option for tenants to pay rent**.

⚠️ **This directly conflicts with `04a §2`'s all-digital collection design** (TurboTenant / Zelle / Venmo only). You cannot make electronic payment mandatory. Offer a check mailed to your **US virtual mailbox** (the same address as the `01d §0` EB-1A address of record, per `04b §8`) or to your in-state agent — but **strongly incentivize ACH** in practice, because a check landing in a US mailbox you read remotely is the worst possible collection path from Bengaluru. Practical framing in the lease: ACH/portal is the default and preferred method; check-by-mail available on request to a stated address.

### 2.5 Farmington has a Fair Rent Commission

**Verified — Farmington is listed by name:** Human Services, 1 Monteith Dr., Farmington, CT 06034, (860) 675-2390. A tenant may bring a complaint that a **rent increase is unfair**, that they're being charged for utilities that were included in the original rent, or that the rent is unfair given unsafe/unhealthy conditions.

**What this changes:** `04a §2` item 21 says "raise rent 2–5% at renewal." That's fine, but **document comps every time you raise rent** (screenshot Zillow/Rentometer comps into the property folder) so an increase is defensible. Also: **don't restructure utilities mid-tenancy** — moving a utility from included to tenant-paid is explicitly a complaint ground.

**Also verified:** rent **cannot be increased during the term of a written lease** unless the lease says so. And CT sets **no statutory notice period** for an increase at renewal unless the lease specifies one — so **put a notice period in your lease** (e.g., 60 days), which also gives you the lead time you need from India.

### 2.6 Heat: make the tenant responsible in writing, or you owe 65°F year-round

**Verified:** "Unless the rental agreement states that the tenant is responsible, the landlord must supply running water and reasonable amounts of heat and hot water." And **if heat is the landlord's responsibility, the law requires at least 65 degrees at all times of the year.**

**Action:** the lease must **state explicitly that the tenant is responsible for heat, hot water, and electricity**, with accounts in the tenant's name from 10/10/26. Get the utility transfer confirmed in writing before you fly — an unpaid-heat situation in a CT January that becomes *your* obligation is a catastrophic remote-landlord scenario.

⚠️ **If the house is oil or propane heat** (common in that housing stock), add a fuel clause: tank level at move-in, same level at move-out, tenant contracts the fill. Name your existing fuel dealer. If it's natural gas, this is simpler.

### 2.7 Eviction is longer and more formal than `04a` says

**Verified process:** notice to quit (**form JD-HM-7**, CGS §47a-23) served by a **state marshal or indifferent person** → summons (**JD-HM-32**) + complaint (**JD-HM-8** for nonpayment) → return date → tenant has 2 days after the return date to file an Appearance (JD-CL-12) → judgment → **5-day stay of execution** → then a marshal executes. A **Connecticut Right to Counsel (CT-RTC) notice** must accompany the notice to quit.

⚠️ **The stays are the part `04a` gets wrong.** `04a` says "eviction takes 60–90 days." In CT:
- Nonpayment judgment: if the tenant **pays all rent due within 5 days of judgment**, they may ask the court to let them stay **up to 3 months**.
- Lapse of time / lease violation: tenant may ask for a stay of **up to an additional 6 months**.

**Realistic worst case is therefore many months, not 90 days** — which is a direct argument for (a) screening hard, (b) sizing the reserve to the real downside, and (c) retaining the eviction attorney **before** you leave (already a tracker item, still unchecked). Cases for Unionville go to the **Hartford Judicial District** housing session.

### 2.8 Other CT items to confirm with counsel before signing

- **Lead paint** — if built before 1978: federal Title X disclosure + EPA pamphlet, **plus** CT's own lead rules (a unit with cracked/chipping lead paint and a child under 6 can be deemed unfit). **Pull the year built from the Farmington assessor card this week.**
- **Smoke/CO detectors** — working smoke detector is on the landlord-duty list; CT has affidavit requirements on transfer and specific placement rules. Verify and document with photos at move-in.
- **Farmington rental registration / certificate of occupancy** — call the Farmington building/health department and ask whether a single-family rental requires registration or inspection. Five-minute call; towns vary.
- **Water/sewer liens** — unpaid municipal water/sewer can lien the property regardless of who agreed to pay. Ask Farmington whether that's the case for this address; if so, **keep water/sewer in your name and build it into the rent**, which also avoids the §2.5 "utilities that were included" trap later.
- **Flood** — Unionville sits on the Farmington River and parts of the village are in FEMA flood zones. **Confirm the flood zone for the exact address**; DP-3 landlord policies exclude flood. If your lender already escrows flood insurance you'll see it on the statement.

## 4. Hire a leasing-only agent. Keep the self-management.

**This is the main recommendation of this document, and it's a change from `04a`.**

`04a` framed a property manager as an all-or-nothing escape hatch at 8–10% of rent. There's a middle option it doesn't consider: **tenant placement only**, typically **half to one month's rent** as a one-time fee in the Hartford County market, for finding, showing, screening, and papering the lease. You then self-manage the ongoing tenancy — which is where the recurring 8–10% actually goes.

**Why it's right specifically for you, right now:**

| | Cost | Against |
|---|---|---|
| One month's rent, once (~$2,800) | **~$2,800 one-time** | **One vacant month = ~$3,279.** The fee is less than the vacancy it's buying insurance against — and you're starting 6 weeks late, in the off-season, with no proxy. |
| Full PM at 9% over 33 months | ~$8,300 + placement | This is the deal you already correctly declined. Don't re-open it. |

**What the agent structurally fixes that money can't otherwise buy:** a **licensed CT person who shows the house after you fly**, who knows the §2 mechanics natively (deposit escrow, 9-day grace, notice forms), and who can be your **§2.1 in-state agent** — collapsing three of your open problems into one engagement.

**How to buy it well:** flat fee or one month, **placement only, no ongoing management, no renewal commission, no repair markup**; they hand you the signed lease, the screening file, the keys/codes, and the deposit for *your* escrow account. Get it in writing. Interview the three from W1.3 and pick on CT-compliance fluency, not price.

**Then self-manage the steady state** (§7). You keep ~$8,300 of the PM fees. You spend ~$2,800 once, in the five weeks where you have no other way to be in two places.

## 5. Lease terms that must change

### 5.1 ⚠️ Don't sign a 12-month lease. Sign ~21 months.

A 12-month lease starting **10/10/26 ends 10/10/27** — dumping you into a **Connecticut October** re-let, off-season, from Bengaluru, with no proxy. That's the worst month of the year to fill a New England single-family, and you'd repeat it in 2028.

**Better: a ~21-month initial term, 10/10/2026 → 6/30/2028.** Then one 12-month renewal, **7/1/2028 → 6/30/2029**.

Why it's strictly better for your situation:
- **It removes an entire turnover** from your three years abroad. Every turnover is the highest-risk, highest-touch event in remote landlording (§2.2's 30-day clock, cleaning, repaint, re-list, re-screen).
- **Both terms end June 30** — peak New England leasing season, when families relocating for the Farmington schools are actually looking.
- **It lands the final expiry at 6/30/2029**, which lines up with your planned **fall 2029 US return** (`00` Phase 2, `02` "time return to US fall"). You get the house back with a summer to recommission it, instead of negotiating an early termination from India.
- Offer a **modest concession (~2–3%)** if needed to get the odd term accepted. Cheap relative to one avoided vacancy.

### 5.2 Solar: disclose the mess accurately, and don't let it block the lease

Solar is still unresolved — Track 1 arbitration engaged, Track 2 buyout quote never obtained (tracker §C). **Do not wait for it.** Both tracks are explicitly workable from India (arbitration counsel confirmed 2026-07-08; a buyout is a 30–60 day paperwork-and-wire process).

## 6. Money and tax — two corrections and one thing nobody has computed

### 6.1 ⚠️ Compute the actual monthly gap before you set the rent

`04a §6` says "if rent already covers PITI + solar (~$3,279/mo)... doing nothing is fine," and `00` projects **"$700–1,200/mo net surplus."** Both assume rent ≥ carry. **At `04a`'s own rent assumption of $2,500–3,000, it doesn't.** Fill this in with W1.7's real numbers:

```
Market rent (verified, 3 sources)          $ ______
− P&I                                        $ 2,039
− Taxes + insurance (landlord, +15–25%)      $ ______   (base ~$1,120; get the DP-3 delta from W1.5)
− Solar charge                               $ ______   (~$120 until resolved)
− Reserve for repairs/vacancy (~8–10% rent)  $ ______
= Monthly surplus / (gap)                    $ ______
      × 33 months (Oct '26 → Jun '29)        $ ______
```

If that's negative, the answer isn't "don't rent" — a vacant house costs the full $3,279 **and** carries the CT winter risk. The answer is that **the reserve must cover the gap for the whole stay**, on top of `04a`'s 6-month PITI reserve. Size it once, fund it before you fly, and tell the CPA — it also changes the Schedule E picture (a paper loss may be suspended under the passive-activity rules for a nonresident; ask).

### 6.2 ⚠️ Establish the depreciation basis at conversion — this month, or never

At the 10/10/26 conversion from personal use to rental, your depreciable basis is the **lesser of adjusted basis or FMV at the conversion date** — then you strip out land, because land isn't depreciable. **This is nearly impossible to reconstruct years later and it drives 27.5 years of deductions.** Assemble now:

- **Original purchase price + closing costs**, plus **every capital improvement** with receipts (roof, HVAC, kitchen, windows — dig these out while you still have the file cabinet).
- **FMV at 10/10/26** — get it in writing: a broker price opinion from one of the W1.3 agents (often free), or an appraisal. Screenshot comps as backup.
- **Land vs building allocation** — use the **Farmington assessor's land/building split** (W1.6) as the defensible ratio.
- **A solar buyout, if it happens, is a separate basis item** — and `04a §3`'s open CPA question (30% ITC *and* depreciation as rental equipment) stays open.

**Also for the CPA:** 2026 is a **split year for this property**. Mortgage interest and property tax for **Jan 1 – Oct 9** are personal (Schedule A); **Oct 10 – Dec 31** is Schedule E. And confirm the **placed-in-service date is when the property was first held out for rent** (listing published, per W1.1) rather than tenant move-in — it affects which September/October costs are deductible versus capitalized.

### 6.3 ⚠️ W-8ECI has no recipient — the "property manager" doesn't exist

`04a §5` step 5, `04 §4`, and `03` all instruct you to **"file W-8ECI with your property manager."** Under self-management **there is no property manager**, and this is not a paperwork nit: for a nonresident landlord, US-source gross rent is subject to **30% withholding** unless the payor holds a W-8ECI and you make the **IRC §871(d)** net election.

**With no PM, the payor is the tenant.** That makes your **tenant** the withholding agent — with Form 1042/1042-S filing duties they will not know about and will not perform.

**Timing you have in your favor:** you're filing **2026 as a full-year US resident** (`04b §4.F`), so NRA withholding isn't live on the Oct–Dec 2026 rent. **The exposure starts 1 Jan 2027.**

**Three options — put this to the cross-border CPA as a named question:**
1. **W-8ECI to the tenant**, with a plain-English cover note. Simplest, but pushes 1042/1042-S onto a layperson.
2. **Interpose a US agent as the withholding agent** — your §2.1 in-state agent, the leasing brokerage, or a bookkeeping service that receives rent and remits to you. Cleanest legally; costs a small monthly fee. **Probably the right answer, and it stacks with §2.1.**
3. Reconsider a PM purely for withholding hygiene — almost certainly not worth 9%.

**Either way:** make the **§871(d) election** with your first Form 1040-NR (the 2027 return), understanding it's binding going forward, and **collect W-9s from every contractor at first payment** — with ECI/trade-or-business treatment, 1099-NEC obligations for contractors paid over $600 are live, and backfilling W-9s from India is miserable. Ask the CPA to confirm scope.

### 6.4 Connecticut wants a nonresident return too

CT-source rental income means a **CT nonresident income tax return (CT-1040NR/PY)** on top of the federal 1040-NR. Add CT to the CPA's scope explicitly — the plan currently discusses only federal treatment and "break high-tax-state domicile," which is a different issue. Also **[verify]** whether CT imposes any withholding on rent paid to a nonresident owner.

### 6.5 The refi window is closed. The recast still isn't the move.

`04a §1` makes an owner-occupied refi contingent on doing it "**before you leave, while on W-2 income**," and honestly. It's **Sept 2**, departure is ~5 weeks out, the relocation is committed, and a purchase-money-style close runs 30–45 days. **An owner-occupied refi is now off the table** — signing an occupancy representation while boarding a flight to Bengaluru is the misrepresentation `04a` correctly warns about. An investment-property refi is theoretically available but won't close before you fly and costs 0.5–0.875% more.

**Recommendation: close the refi question. Keep the 6.1% loan.** And per `04a §1`, **don't recast either** — you now need liquidity for the reserve, the §6.1 monthly gap, the leasing fee, two international moves, and the $3,520 in USCIS fees (`01d §0`). The recast is a good trade only with idle cash, and you don't have idle cash in September 2026.

## 7. Steady-state operations from Bengaluru

### 7.1 The IST-anchored cadence

The 12.5-hour offset is actually workable if you stop trying to be reachable and instead be *predictable*. US Eastern business hours (9am–5pm ET) are **6:30pm–2:30am IST**. So:

- **Your daily rental window: 7:00–8:30pm IST** (= 8:30–10:00am ET). Contractors, servicer, insurer, town hall — all reachable, and it's after work and before the family evening. One window, every weekday, is enough.
- **Tenant-facing SLA in the lease: acknowledge within 24 hours.** Don't promise faster; you'll break it and habituate the tenant to calling instead of ticketing.
- **Never take the 3am call.** That's what §7.2 and the §8 hardware are for.

### 7.2 The escalation ladder (this replaces the co-trustee)

Put this in the lease and on a laminated sheet on the fridge:

| Tier | Situation | Tenant does | You do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | Life safety — fire, gas smell, structural | **Call 911**, then text you | Insurer + POA holder |
| **1** | True emergency — burst pipe, no heat below freezing, sewage, no water | **Call the named 24/7 vendor directly**, pre-authorized up to **$500**; card on file bills you | Confirm next IST window; approve overage |
| **2** | Urgent, not emergency — appliance dead, partial leak, no AC in heat | Submit a ticket with **photos/video** | Triage and dispatch within 24 hrs; quote first above $500 |
| **3** | Routine | Ticket | Batch weekly |

**Two authority thresholds to set now:** tenant self-dispatch cap **$500** (Tier 1 only), and your own no-quote-needed cap for vendors — `04a` suggested $200; **raise it to ~$500.** From India, the cost of a 48-hour delay while you collect three quotes on a $350 repair is higher than the savings, and CT habitability claims accrue while you shop.

## 8. The Connecticut winter is your real catastrophic risk

`04a §3`'s risk table lists "major repair $3,000–15,000." For an unattended house in Unionville with the owner in India, the actual tail risk is **a frozen pipe burst** — tens of thousands in damage, mold, a displaced tenant, and a habitability claim, discovered late because nobody is watching. This is also the scenario where "I'll find someone on Thumbtack" fails hardest: by the time you're sourcing a plumber, the water has been running for hours.

**Spend ~$700–900 on hardware before you fly. It's the best money in this entire plan:**

| Item | Cost | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Automatic water shutoff** (Moen Flo / Phyn Plus) | ~$500–800 installed | Detects abnormal flow and **shuts the main off by itself**, then alerts your phone in Bengaluru. This is the single item that most substitutes for a human being in the house. |
| **Freeze/leak sensors** — basement, under sinks, near washer, water heater | ~$100–150 | Early warning at the actual failure points |
| **Smart thermostat with remote alerts** (Nest/ecobee) | ~$150–250 | Alerts if indoor temp drops — a dead furnace in January is visible from India before pipes freeze |

**Lease terms that pair with it:** tenant must **maintain heat at no less than 60°F** at all times during heating season and **notify you immediately of any heating failure or extended absence** — the CT booklet notes tenants should tell the landlord about long absences, so it's a normal ask. Tenant must not disable or interfere with the shutoff or sensors.

**Insurance — ask both carriers in W1.5, in writing:**
1. **Does the policy exclude freeze/burst-pipe damage if the property is unoccupied**, and what's the occupancy definition and the day threshold?
2. **What happens between 10/10 and a tenant move-in if it's vacant** — do you need a **vacant/unoccupied endorsement**, and what does it cost? `04a §2a` flags that standard homeowners coverage lapses after 30–60 days vacant; **do not let this run on assumption.** Confirm the exact date coverage changes character and bind whatever you need.
3. **Loss-of-rent coverage** amount and trigger.
4. **Flood** (§2.8) — is the address in a zone, is it excluded, is it already escrowed?
